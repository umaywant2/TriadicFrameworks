Standard Model

Humanity's most precisely verified physical theory — a gauge quantum field theory unifying the electromagnetic, weak, and strong forces under a single SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) symmetry group with 17 fundamental particles.

Focus: The Standard Model is examined through the TriadicFrameworks pillars of Matter, Force, and Symmetry Breaking — the three structural layers that together account for all known particle interactions except gravity, with extraordinary predictive precision.

This module covers the fermion content (quarks, leptons), gauge boson sector, Higgs mechanism, electroweak unification, QCD confinement, and the 19 free parameters of the Standard Model, with cross-module resonances to Quantum Field Theory, Electromagnetism, and General Relativity.

Triadic Pillars

⚛
Pillar I

Matter

Matter consists of 12 fundamental fermions (spin-½ particles): 6 quarks arranged in 3 generations (up/down, charm/strange, top/bottom) and 6 leptons (electron, muon, tau and their neutrinos). Each fermion has an antiparticle counterpart with opposite quantum numbers.

⟨⟩
Pillar II

Force

Three fundamental forces are mediated by 12 gauge bosons: the photon (EM, U(1)), W⁺/W⁻/Z⁰ (weak, SU(2)), and 8 gluons (strong, SU(3)). Each boson is the quantum of its respective gauge field, introduced by demanding local gauge invariance of the Standard Model Lagrangian.

photon γ W/Z bosons gluons g
✦
Pillar III

Symmetry Breaking

The Higgs mechanism breaks electroweak SU(2)×U(1)_Y symmetry spontaneously at ~246 GeV, giving W/Z bosons mass while leaving the photon massless. The Higgs field also couples to fermions via Yukawa terms, generating all fermion masses. The Higgs boson (125 GeV) was discovered in 2012.

Core Concepts

Color Confinement (QCD)

Quarks carry color charge (red, green, blue) and are permanently confined within color-neutral hadrons by gluon exchange; the strong coupling grows with distance (asymptotic freedom at short range).

Electroweak Unification

Glashow, Salam, and Weinberg unified EM and weak forces at ~100 GeV under SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y, broken by the Higgs VEV to yield the photon and massive W/Z bosons.

CKM Matrix

The Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix describes quark flavor mixing in weak decays; its CP-violating phase accounts for the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry in kaon systems.

PMNS Matrix

The Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata matrix governs neutrino flavor oscillations, implying neutrinos have small but nonzero masses — beyond the minimal SM.

Renormalization

Ultraviolet divergences in loop calculations are systematically removed by renormalization; the SM is renormalizable, ensuring finite predictions at all perturbative orders.

Open Problems

The SM does not incorporate gravity, dark matter, dark energy, or explain the hierarchy problem, strong CP problem, matter-antimatter asymmetry, or neutrino mass origin.

Cross-Module Connections

  • Quantum Field Theory — The Standard Model is the specific QFT realized in nature; all SM predictions follow from path-integral quantization of its Lagrangian.
  • Electromagnetism — QED (U(1) gauge theory) is the SM's electromagnetic sector; its coupling constant α ≈ 1/137 runs with energy.
  • General Relativity — Gravity is excluded from the SM; combining QFT and GR into a consistent quantum gravity theory remains unsolved.
  • Thermodynamics — The SM describes phase transitions in the early universe: QCD confinement at ~150 MeV and electroweak symmetry breaking at ~100 GeV.
  • Information Theory — Quantum computing exploits SM-described particles; qubit decoherence timescales are governed by SM interactions with the environment.
