Focus: Chaos Theory is examined through the TriadicFrameworks lens of Sensitivity, Self-Organization, and Emergence — three irreducible pillars that together account for why deterministic systems generate behavior indistinguishable from randomness at sufficient scales.

This module maps classical formulations (Lorenz, Feigenbaum, Mandelbrot) onto the triadic scaffold, surfacing cross-module resonances with Thermodynamics, Quantum Mechanics, and Evolutionary Biology.

Triadic Pillars

🦋
Pillar I

Sensitivity

The butterfly effect: exponential divergence of trajectories from arbitrarily close initial conditions. Lyapunov exponents quantify the rate at which uncertainty grows, rendering long-term prediction fundamentally impossible beyond the predictability horizon.

Self-Organization

Despite sensitivity, chaotic systems converge onto bounded structures called strange attractors — low-dimensional geometric objects that constrain the long-run statistical behavior of trajectories without fixing individual outcomes.

Core Concepts

Strange Attractors

Fractal subsets of phase space that chaotic trajectories approach asymptotically, exhibiting non-integer Hausdorff dimension.

Feigenbaum Constants

Universal constants (δ ≈ 4.6692, α ≈ 2.5029) governing the rate of period-doubling bifurcations across all unimodal maps.

Lyapunov Spectrum

The ordered set of Lyapunov exponents characterizing expansion/contraction rates along all principal directions in phase space.

Fractal Dimension

A non-integer measure of geometric complexity; the Hausdorff dimension of the Lorenz attractor ≈ 2.06.

Bifurcation Theory

Qualitative changes in a system's long-term behavior as a control parameter crosses critical thresholds, producing period-doubling or crisis bifurcations.

Ergodicity

The statistical equivalence of time averages and ensemble averages in ergodic chaotic systems, enabling thermodynamic-style analyses.
