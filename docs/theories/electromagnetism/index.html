Pillar I

Field

The electromagnetic field (E, B) is the primary physical reality — a tensor quantity permeating spacetime whose values at each event encode forces on charged matter. Field lines carry energy density ½(ε₀E² + B²/μ₀) and propagate as electromagnetic waves at c.

Pillar II

Force

The Lorentz force F = q(E + v×B) describes how fields exert force on charged particles. Together with Newton's second law, this governs classical charged-particle dynamics. Radiation reaction and radiative damping reveal the feedback between motion and field.

Pillar III

Symmetry

U(1) gauge symmetry — invariance under local phase transformations ψ → e^{iα(x)}ψ — is the deep organizing principle. Noether's theorem links this symmetry to charge conservation. Its gauging predicts the photon as the force mediator.

Core Concepts

Maxwell's Equations

Four equations (Gauss, Gauss-Magnetism, Faraday, Ampère-Maxwell) fully characterizing classical electromagnetic phenomena and predicting light as an EM wave.

Electromagnetic Waves

Self-sustaining oscillations of E and B fields propagating at c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀) ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s across the full spectrum from radio to gamma rays.

Quantum Electrodynamics

The quantum field theory of EM interactions, with photons as U(1) gauge bosons. QED predictions agree with experiment to 10 decimal places.

Special Relativity

Maxwell's equations are Lorentz-covariant; the unification of E and B into Fμν was one of Einstein's key insights motivating special relativity.

Faraday's Law

A time-varying magnetic flux induces an EMF proportional to its rate of change — the principle behind electric generators, transformers, and inductors.

Displacement Current

Maxwell's correction ε₀(∂E/∂t) to Ampère's Law closed the inconsistency with charge conservation and predicted electromagnetic radiation.

Key Equations

// Maxwell's Equations (Differential Form)
∇ · E = ρ/ε₀ Gauss's Law: electric field divergence is proportional to local charge density.
∇ · B = 0 No magnetic monopoles: magnetic field lines form closed loops.
∇ × E = −∂B/∂t Faraday's Law: a changing magnetic field induces a curl in the electric field.
∇ × B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀(∂E/∂t) Ampère–Maxwell Law: currents and changing electric fields produce magnetic field curl.
