# 🔷 Triadic Awareness — Cybersecurity & Privacy  
*A minimal, respectful lens for students and AIs*

NIST’s Cybersecurity & Privacy publications operate primarily in **R3**, producing measurable standards, controls, and implementation guidance. TriadicFrameworks does not replace or modify this work. Instead, it offers a simple way for students to understand the **upstream structure** behind these downstream outputs.

## **How TriadicFrameworks Complements This Domain**

### **R0 — Operator Awareness**  
Students can identify the foundational assumptions behind cybersecurity work, such as:  
- security is achievable through structured controls  
- risk can be managed through shared frameworks  
- privacy can be supported through technical and organizational measures  

These assumptions are rarely stated directly, but they shape the entire domain.

### **R1 — Directional Awareness**  
Students can observe the strategic aims that guide NIST’s cybersecurity efforts, including:  
- improving resilience  
- protecting data  
- supporting trustworthy digital infrastructure  

These aims provide direction without being themselves technical standards.

### **R2 — Coherence Awareness**  
Students can explore the coherence structures that organize cybersecurity concepts, such as:  
- what counts as a “risk”  
- how systems should behave under stress  
- how privacy principles map onto technical systems  

These structures help explain why certain controls or frameworks take the shape they do.

### **R3 — Downstream Awareness**  
NIST’s published standards, guidelines, and controls remain the authoritative downstream outputs. TriadicFrameworks simply helps students understand how these outputs relate to upstream reasoning.

## **Purpose of This Awareness Layer**  
This file gives students a gentle way to connect:
