# 🎓 Student Exercises — Fire  
*Short, safe, structural prompts for building regime awareness*

## **1. Identify the Primary Regime**  
Using the Fire overview and the examples visible on the NIST Fire Publications page, answer:

- Which regime (R0, R1, R2, or R3) does this domain primarily operate in?  
- What evidence supports your answer?

*(Hint: Look for experiments on thermal runaway, flame spread, calorimetry, and burning behavior — all of which are measurable R3 activities.)*  
  [nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/publications/search?ta%5B0%5D=248586)

---

## **2. Upstream Assumptions**  
Choose one fire‑science concept from the publication list (e.g., “thermal runaway,” “flame spread,” “WUI firebrands,” “pyrolysis kinetics”) and identify:

- What **coherence assumptions (R2)** does this concept rely on?  
- What **operator assumptions (R0)** might be implicit behind it?

Keep answers short — 1–2 sentences per layer.  
  [nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/publications/search?ta%5B0%5D=248586)

---

## **3. Downstream Behavior**  
Pick a specific NIST fire activity or experiment (e.g., calorimetry time‑response characterization, burning rates of vegetative fuels, smoke yield measurement) and describe:

- What is being **measured** or **verified**?  
- How does this reflect **R3** reasoning?

Use examples from the Fire publications page.  
  [nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/publications/search?ta%5B0%5D=248586)

---

## **4. Triadic Awareness Check**  
In 3–4 sentences, explain how TriadicFrameworks could *complement* (not replace) NIST’s fire research by:

- clarifying upstream assumptions (R0–R2)  
- supporting downstream measurement and modeling (R3)

This is an awareness exercise, not a critique.

---

## **5. Optional: Cross‑Domain Thinking**  
Pick another NIST domain (e.g., Cybersecurity, Electromagnetics, Materials) and compare:

- How does Fire’s regime alignment differ from that domain?  
- What stays the same across both?

This helps students see patterns across the entire NIST landscape.
