# 🎓 Student Exercises — Buildings & Construction  
*Short, safe, structural prompts for building regime awareness*

## **1. Identify the Primary Regime**  
Using the Buildings & Construction overview and the examples visible on the NIST Publications page, answer:

- Which regime (R0, R1, R2, or R3) does this domain primarily operate in?  
- What evidence supports your answer?

*(Hint: Look for structural tests, plumbing pressure‑loss measurements, airflow‑model validation, refrigerant flammability studies, and earthquake observations — all classic R3 activities.)*  
  [nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/publications/search?ta%5B0%5D=248371)

---

## **2. Upstream Assumptions**  
Choose one building‑science concept from the publication list (e.g., “natural ventilation,” “precast concrete moment connections,” “pressure‑loss behavior,” “refrigerant flammability,” “earthquake fragility,” “additive‑construction acceptance criteria”) and identify:

- What **coherence assumptions (R2)** does this concept rely on?  
- What **operator assumptions (R0)** might be implicit behind it?

Keep answers short — 1–2 sentences per layer.

---

## **3. Downstream Behavior**  
Pick a specific NIST Buildings & Construction activity or experiment (e.g., plumbing pressure‑loss measurements, column‑removal structural tests, VOC speciation in indoor air, natural‑ventilation airflow validation, earthquake reconnaissance) and describe:

- What is being **measured** or **verified**?  
- How does this reflect **R3** reasoning?

Use examples from the Buildings & Construction publications page.  
  [nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/publications/search?ta%5B0%5D=248371)

---

## **4. Triadic Awareness Check**  
In 3–4 sentences, explain how TriadicFrameworks could *complement* (not replace) NIST’s building‑science work by:

- clarifying upstream assumptions (R0–R2)  
- supporting downstream measurement, modeling, and performance‑based design (R3)

This is an awareness exercise, not a critique.

---

## **5. Optional: Cross‑Domain Thinking**  
Pick another NIST domain (e.g., Fire, Chemistry, Ceramics, Electromagnetics) and compare:

- How does Buildings & Construction’s regime alignment differ from that domain?  
- What stays the same across both?

This helps students see structural patterns across the entire NIST landscape.
