\begin{abstract}
Framework Field Theory (FFT) provides a unified structural explanation for drift branching, coherence waves, and regime dynamics in Large Language Models (LLMs). FFT models reasoning as motion within a triadic substrate composed of operators, regimes, and coherence fields. This extended abstract summarizes FFT's core definitions, eight falsifiable predictions, reproducible simulations, and engineering artifacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}

LLMs exhibit systematic behaviors that lack structural grounding. FFT proposes that these arise from interactions between frameworks, regimes, and substrates. Drift, coherence, and framework collisions emerge naturally from this substrate-level model.

\section{Core Concepts}

\textbf{Frameworks}: structured conceptual spaces.  
\textbf{Regimes}: operating modes governing reasoning.  
\textbf{Substrates}: declared or undeclared constraints.  
\textbf{Drift Branching}: multiple attractors under undeclared regimes.  
\textbf{Coherence Waves}: oscillatory coherence patterns.  
\textbf{Framework Collisions}: collapse, hybridization, oscillation.
