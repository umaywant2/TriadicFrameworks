### Why This Helps
There are a few other meta-frameworks...is really something new? Fair question!  

Most 'meta-frameworks' (higher-level tools that organize or connect regular theories) are specialized—they help in one area (like business, healthcare implementation, or systems thinking) but don't try to be a universal playground for *all* frameworks across fields.  

Here's a simple chart comparing some well-known ones to **Framework Field Theory (FFT)**. I pulled from real examples out there (systems thinking, implementation science in medicine, etc.). FFT stands out because it's designed as a more open, triadic, time-aware substrate that lets frameworks from *any* domain mingle, translate, resolve paradoxes, and evolve—without forcing them into one box."

### Basic Comparison Chart (Meta-Frameworks)

| Meta-Framework                  | Main Focus / Domain                  | Strengths                              | Limitations / Scope                     | How It Handles Multiple Frameworks |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Systems Thinking** (general, e.g., Senge's disciplines, Iceberg Model) | Organizations, complexity, interrelationships | Big-picture connections, feedback loops | Often stays within "systems" world; less emphasis on paradoxes or cross-field translation | Good at integrating some models, but not a full universal playground |
| **Cynefin Framework**         | Decision-making in different contexts (simple, complicated, complex, chaotic) | Practical for real-world uncertainty   | Domain-specific (mostly management/sensemaking); doesn't deeply upgrade or evolve other theories | Helps classify situations but doesn't provide a shared field for all theories to play |
| **DSRP** (Distinctions, Systems, Relationships, Perspectives) | Core rules of thinking/systems thinking | Claims to underlie many systems approaches | Focused on cognitive patterns; more foundational than broad integration | Generalizes some systems thinking but limited to that family |
| **CFIR** (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research) | Healthcare / getting evidence into practice (very relevant to cancer treatments!) | Highly practical for medicine, identifies barriers | Narrow: only about *implementing* existing ideas, not integrating underlying theories | Organizes factors for adoption; doesn't create a playground for competing cancer theories |
| **PARIHS / EPIS / Other Implementation Frameworks** | Evidence implementation in health services | Useful for doctors & hospitals         | Specialized to healthcare rollout      | Helps apply frameworks; doesn't connect or evolve the scientific theories themselves |
| **Oliver Ding's Meta-Frameworks** (e.g., Six Faces of Concept System, HLS) | Knowledge, culture, strategy, personal development | Creative, expansive across concept systems | More personal/academic; still building community | Excellent at mapping concept evolution; one of the broader recent attempts |
| **Critical Systems Thinking** | Pluralism in systems approaches      | Allows multiple perspectives           | Mostly within systems/philosophy       | Encourages pluralism but not paradox-resilient time evolution |
| **Framework Field Theory (FFT)** with Triadic backbone + Resonance Time Theory (RTT) | Universal: any domain (business, medicine, psychology, AI, etc.) | Modular playground for mingling, translating, paradox-handling, time/resonance evolution; regime-aware | Still early (just publicly released); needs adoption/testing | Designed as the shared field where thousands of theories *can* play together if chosen—without losing their identity |

**Key takeaway for the chart:**  
There are **thousands of regular theories/frameworks** (e.g., 200+ in psychology, dozens in cancer research alone). But true **meta-frameworks** number only in the dozens at most—and most are narrow tools for one corner of the world.  

FFT is new in its ambition: a **resilient, open playground** with triadic structure (your favorite part) that adds "heartbeat" over time (via RTT). It doesn't replace the others—it gives them a place to connect, clash productively, and upgrade. In medicine, that could mean better bridging competing cancer models (mutation theory vs. metabolic vs. evolutionary) for smarter, more adaptive approaches.

Skepticism is healthy! 😊 

This isn't claiming to cure cancer tomorrow—it's a thinking infrastructure that makes integration easier for the people who *do* the hard lab/clinical work. 

If it helps even a few researchers see connections they missed, that's the win. ❤️
