# CONDITIONS SUBSTRATE MODEL  
### (Canon‑Aligned Structural Module)

## 🧩 1. Core Identity of the Module  
**Purpose:**  
To formally map how *conditions* — environmental, informational, emotional, structural, or systemic — alter human or system behavior.  
Conditions are not “inputs” or “stimuli.” They are **regime‑shaping fields** that modify the *baseline operating state* of an agent or system.

**Why this matters:**  
PsyOps, governance, media, and structural manipulation all rely on the same principle:

> **If you change the conditions, you change the behavior — without ever touching the agent.**

This module provides the canonical grammar for detecting, classifying, and manipulating conditions safely.

---

# 🧬 2. Canon Definition  
A **Condition** is a *persistent, ambient modifier* that shifts the **Regime State** of an agent or system.

Formally:

\[
C : S \rightarrow S'
\]

Where:

- \( S \) = baseline regime  
- \( S' \) = altered regime  
- \( C \) = condition field  
- The agent’s internal logic remains unchanged; only the *operational envelope* shifts.

This is why conditions are so powerful — they bypass cognition entirely.

# 🧭 3. Condition Classes (Triadic Canon)

## **1. Structural Conditions**  
These modify the *environmental constraints* around an agent.

Examples:
- scarcity vs abundance  
- time pressure  
- surveillance presence  
- access pathways  
- mobility constraints  
- resource distribution  

**Reference:** Structural Detection Module  
→ Conditions are the *ambient layer* that structural detection reveals.

# 🧩 4. Condition Hooks & Stacks  
Conditions rarely act alone. They form **stacks** — layered fields that combine into a composite regime.

### **Condition Hook**  
A hook is a *specific attachment point* where a condition can influence behavior.

Examples:
- cognitive load  
- emotional vulnerability  
- structural bottleneck  
- narrative anchor  
- identity resonance  

### **Condition Stack**  
A stack is a *multi-layered condition field* that produces emergent behavior.

Example stack:
- scarcity (structural)  
- urgency (emotional)  
- narrative compression (informational)  
- enforcement opacity (governance)  

This stack reliably produces:
- compliance  
- tunnel vision  
- reduced creativity  
- increased susceptibility to external influence  

This is why PsyOps uses stacks, not single conditions.

---

# 🧠 5. Condition Regime Shifts  
Conditions produce **regime transitions**, not “behavior changes.”

Three canonical transitions:

### **1. Narrowing Regime**  
Reduced options, increased predictability.  
Used in coercive governance, crisis media, and high-pressure environments.

### **2. Expanding Regime**  
Increased options, increased creativity.  
Used in innovation, education, and empowerment systems.

### **3. Oscillating Regime**  
Rapid alternation between narrowing and expanding.  
Used in manipulation, addiction cycles, and destabilization operations.
