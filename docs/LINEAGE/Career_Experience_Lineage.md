## Purpose

This document preserves the professional lineage of Andrew C. Wilson.

It is not intended to function primarily as a conventional résumé. Conventional résumés compress a working life into a short sales document tailored to a particular opening. This record serves a different purpose: to preserve the progression of roles, systems, projects, responsibilities, skills, and contributions that formed a career.

The source documents span different periods and were written for different audiences. Titles, dates, and numerical estimates sometimes vary between versions. Where the records disagree, this digest preserves the discrepancy rather than silently choosing one account.

Obsolete personal contact information and credential identifiers have been omitted.

# 1. Career Overview

Andrew C. Wilson developed his career through hands-on technical work, systems administration, enterprise infrastructure, desktop engineering, service management, project implementation, and technical leadership.

His early professional development combined formal study with direct work in computer labs, networking, and technical support. By the mid-1990s, that experience had developed into systems-administration work at the University of Michigan.

From 1996 through 1999, he worked in the College of Literature, Science, and the Arts as a System Administrator III. During this period, he helped design and build a large Windows NT network and co-pioneered an automated Windows NT deployment method.

From 2000 through 2002, he served as an ITCS Technologist and Windows Technical Lead. His work included the creation and technical development of the University’s UMROOT Active Directory forest, along with experience involving directory services, authentication, public-key infrastructure, security, storage, and enterprise systems.

He subsequently moved into desktop-support management within the central Information Technology Services organization. He managed support operations, mentored technical staff, remained involved in systems and project work, and represented his organization in cross-campus technology initiatives.

Beginning in late 2011 or 2012, depending on the source record, he moved into the design and implementation of MiWorkspace. He helped create the Advance implementation team, participated in planning and build procedures, coordinated unit discovery and deployment, and eventually managed a high-performing implementation organization serving a large university population.

From March 2015 through June 2018, he managed a MiWorkspace desktop-support team responsible for Tier-2 service across approximately 136 buildings.

Across these roles, a consistent pattern appears:

- remaining technically engaged while managing people;
- building processes that other people could repeat;
- connecting infrastructure decisions to user experience;
- helping technical staff succeed;
- documenting systems and methods;
- participating in cross-organizational work;
- treating support as an engineering and service discipline;
- creating structures that could continue after the initial project was complete.

After his formal university career, that same pattern continued through independent technical research, open-source documentation, AI-assisted knowledge systems, and stewardship of TriadicFrameworks.

# 13. Closing Reflection

A résumé asks a narrow question:

> Why should someone hire this person for this position?

A career lineage asks a broader one:

> What did this person learn, build, support, lead, preserve, and pass forward?

The surviving record shows a career built across changing generations of technology: computer labs, local networks, Windows NT, automated deployment, directory services, enterprise identity, desktop engineering, security, service management, cloud migration, distributed support, and later AI-assisted documentation.

The technology changed repeatedly.

The underlying pattern did not.

Andrew learned systems deeply enough to explain them. He turned repeated work into procedures. He helped technical staff develop. He connected architecture to the needs of people doing real work. He documented methods so they could be reused. He moved between hands-on engineering and management without treating them as unrelated worlds.

Later, those same instincts found a new setting in TriadicFrameworks:

- preserve the lineage;
- expose the structure;
- identify assumptions;
- make the work inspectable;
- give students and systems a path through it;
- leave the method available for someone else to test, challenge, adapt, or continue.

The formal résumé history ends.

The lineage does not.
