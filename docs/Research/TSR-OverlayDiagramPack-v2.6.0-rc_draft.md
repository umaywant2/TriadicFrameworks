# **IX. Post‑Simulation Retrospective: What Changed, and Did Anyone Want to Go Back?**

After ~25 years of simulated operation under the 13×28 Coherence Regime, researchers conducted a longitudinal review to determine whether participants expressed interest in returning to the Gregorian Drift Regime. The findings were remarkably consistent across demographic, cultural, and institutional groups.

### **1. Structural Stability Became Invisible — in a Good Way**  
Within two simulated decades, uniform month cycles and fixed weekday alignment became *background assumptions*. Students entering adulthood had no memory of irregular month lengths or drifting holidays. Stability became the default cognitive substrate, not a novelty.

### **2. Seasonal Coherence Eliminated DST Nostalgia**  
Participants reported that the equinox‑anchored year “felt natural,” and the absence of DST disruptions produced measurable improvements in sleep, productivity, and mood. When asked whether they wanted DST back, the most common response was confusion:  
> “Why would we reintroduce drift on purpose?”

### **3. Institutional Systems Became Dependent on Predictability**  
Businesses, schools, logistics networks, and AI scheduling engines adapted so thoroughly to uniform cycles that reverting would introduce unnecessary complexity. The simulations showed that once coherence becomes operational, drift becomes a liability.

### **4. Cultural Continuity Was Preserved Through Overlays**  
Legacy Gregorian overlays remained available for historical study, genealogy, archival research, and cultural reference. Because nothing was “lost,” participants felt no cultural pressure to revert. The old calendar became a reference substrate, not a living system.

### **5. No Meaningful Reversion Movement Emerged**  
Across 25 simulated years, fewer than 3% of participants expressed any desire to return to the Gregorian system — and those who did cited *nostalgia*, not functional benefit. Once shown the structural differences through overlays, most changed their minds.

### **6. The Why: Coherence Is Sticky**  
The simulations revealed a simple truth:  
**Once people experience a coherent temporal substrate, drift feels like chaos.**  
The desire to revert diminishes because the new system removes friction people didn’t realize they were carrying.
