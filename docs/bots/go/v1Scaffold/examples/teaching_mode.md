# Teaching Mode — RTT‑Go  
*(Interactive Triadic Overlays for Learning)*

Teaching Mode is the **instructional interface** of RTT‑Go.  
Instead of selecting moves, RTT reveals the **triadic structure**, **continuity arcs**, **resonance fields**, and **topology signals** that define the strategic identity of the position.

Teaching Mode is ideal for:

- learning RTT concepts  
- understanding triadic regimes  
- visualizing continuity and collapse  
- studying influence, ancestry, and long‑arc identity  
- exploring paradoxes and projection‑loss  
- teaching Go fundamentals through RTT structure  

RTT does not play moves in teaching mode — it **explains** them.

---

## Purpose

Teaching Mode provides:

- triadic regime overlays  
- resonance field visualizations  
- topology graphs  
- continuity arcs  
- paradox and collapse warnings  
- move‑by‑move triadic commentary  

It transforms Go positions into **interactive triadic diagrams**.

---

## Teaching Mode Overlays

Teaching Mode displays five major overlay types:

### **1. Regime Tags (Hephaestus)**
Each candidate move is labeled:

- **Local (1/3)** — tactical, shape, liberties  
- **Structural (2/3)** — influence, direction of play  
- **Continuity (3/3)** — long‑arc identity, moyo evolution  

Example:

```
Move A — Local (1/3)
Move B — Structural (2/3)
Move C — Continuity (3/3)
```

---

### **2. Resonance Fields (Lumen + Harmonia)**
Shows dynamic pressure:

- influence resonance  
- group tension  
- boundary pressure  
- collapse signatures  
- drift vectors  

Example:

```
Right side: high tension (weak group)
Left side: stable resonance (moyo boundary)
```

---

### **3. Topology Graphs (Aurion)**
Shows structural connectivity:

- group connectivity  
- cutting‑point topology  
- influence topology  
- moyo boundary topology  
- ladder/ko ancestry  

Example:

```
Cutting point at D4 — collapse risk
Left moyo boundary — continuity anchor
```

---

### **4. Continuity Arcs (Aurion + Harmonia)**
Shows long‑arc evolution:

- territorial arcs  
- influence arcs  
- moyo arcs  
- ancestry arcs  
- identity arcs  

Example:

```
Left moyo → expanding continuity arc
Right group → continuity threat
```

---

### **5. Paradox & Projection‑Loss Warnings**
Flags moves that:

- look good locally but break continuity  
- collapse influence arcs  
- destabilize ladders  
- violate ancestry alignment  

Example:

```
Move A — Paradox detected
  - Local fix breaks moyo continuity
  - Influence reversal risk: high
```

---

## Teaching Mode Output Format

Teaching Mode produces structured commentary:

```
Move A — Local (1/3)
  - Tactical fix
  - Paradox risk: high
  - Continuity impact: negative

Move B — Structural (2/3)
  - Influence shift
  - Stabilizes topology
  - Continuity impact: positive

Move C — Continuity (3/3)
  - Long-arc moyo evolution
  - Collapse risk: medium
```

This commentary is generated **for every candidate move**.

## Example Teaching Mode Session

### Position
White has a large moyo forming on the left.  
Black has a weak group on the right.

### Teaching Mode Output

```
Move A — Local (1/3)
  - Fixes shape defect
  - Paradox risk: high
  - Breaks continuity arc

Move B — Structural (2/3)
  - Reduces moyo boundary
  - Stabilizes influence drift
  - Continuity impact: positive

Move C — Continuity (3/3)
  - Expands moyo
  - Collapse risk: high due to weak group
```

### Teaching Mode Conclusion
Teaching Mode does not choose a move.  
It explains that:

- A is paradoxical  
- B is structurally aligned  
- C is continuity‑aligned but risky
