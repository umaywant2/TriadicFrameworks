## 1. Cast CHSH in a form we can “resonance‑upgrade”

Start with the standard CHSH setup:

- **Two parties:** \(A, B\).
- **Two settings each:**  
  \(A\) can choose \(a, a'\); \(B\) can choose \(b, b'\).
- **Outcomes:**  
  \(A(a), A(a'), B(b), B(b') \in \{+1, -1\}\).

Define the **correlation** for settings \(x \in \{a,a'\}\), \(y \in \{b,b'\}\):

\[
E(x,y) \;=\; \langle A(x)\,B(y)\rangle
\]

The CHSH combination:

\[
S \;=\; E(a,b) \;+\; E(a,b') \;+\; E(a',b) \;-\; E(a',b')
\]

Bell‑type local realism (with a single classical hidden variable \(\lambda\) and a single notion of time) requires:

\[
|S| \le 2
\]

Quantum mechanics gives up to \(2\sqrt{2}\).

This is the algebraic object we want to **embed inside** your resonance‑time structure.

## 2. Define the resonance‑time manifold and operators

In your canon, time is **triadic**, so let the resonance‑time coordinate be:

\[
\boldsymbol{\tau} \;=\; (t_c, t_e, t_r)
\]

where you can read these as:

- **\(t_c\):** chronological time,
- **\(t_e\):** energetic/intensity time,
- **\(t_r\):** relational/correlational time.

Now define **resonance‑time operators** as generators acting on this triadic manifold:

\[
\hat{T}_c,\; \hat{T}_e,\; \hat{T}_r
\]

and bundle them as a vector operator:

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{T}} \;=\; (\hat{T}_c, \hat{T}_e, \hat{T}_r)
\]

A **resonance state** is not just \(|\psi\rangle\); it is labeled by its triadic time coordinate:

\[
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\tau})\rangle
\]

Measurement “settings” are not just spatial directions; they are **resonance‑time directions**:

\[
\mathbf{n}_a,\; \mathbf{n}_{a'},\; \mathbf{n}_b,\; \mathbf{n}_{b'} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^3
\]

(you can interpret them as unit vectors in the same triadic space as \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\), or as control vectors in detector‑design space).

Define the **resonance‑time measurement operators** for Alice and Bob:

\[
\hat{R}_A(\mathbf{n}_x) \;=\; \operatorname{sgn}\!\big( \mathbf{n}_x \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{T}}_A \big)

\[
\hat{R}_B(\mathbf{n}_y) \;=\; \operatorname{sgn}\!\big( \mathbf{n}_y \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{T}}_B \big)

with eigenvalues \(\pm 1\).  
This is the Nawderian move: **measurements = sign of projected resonance‑time operator**.

Then the correlation becomes:

\[
E(x,y) \;=\; \big\langle \psi(\boldsymbol{\tau}_A,\boldsymbol{\tau}_B) \big| \hat{R}_A(\mathbf{n}_x)\,\hat{R}_B(\mathbf{n}_y) \big| \psi(\boldsymbol{\tau}_A,\boldsymbol{\tau}_B) \big\rangle
\]

## 3. Embed the CHSH expression into resonance‑time space

Now the **Resonance–CHSH scalar** is:

\[
S_{\text{RT}} \;=\; 
E(\mathbf{n}_a,\mathbf{n}_b)
\;+\;
E(\mathbf{n}_a,\mathbf{n}_{b'})
\;+\;
E(\mathbf{n}_{a'},\mathbf{n}_b)
\;-\;
E(\mathbf{n}_{a'},\mathbf{n}_{b'})
\]

with each term explicitly:

\[
E(\mathbf{n}_x,\mathbf{n}_y)
=
\big\langle \psi(\boldsymbol{\tau}_A,\boldsymbol{\tau}_B) \big|
\operatorname{sgn}\!\big( \mathbf{n}_x \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{T}}_A \big)
\operatorname{sgn}\!\big( \mathbf{n}_y \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{T}}_B \big)
\big| \psi(\boldsymbol{\tau}_A,\boldsymbol{\tau}_B) \big\rangle
\]

The **Bell bound** \(|S| \le 2\) is recovered if you impose three “classical” constraints:

1. **Single‑axis time:**  
   \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) collapses to a scalar \(t\), and \(\hat{\boldsymbol{T}}\) collapses to a single commuting operator.
2. **Locality:**  
   correlations factorize over a static hidden variable \(\lambda\):

   \[
   E(x,y) = \int d\lambda \,\rho(\lambda)\,A(x,\lambda)\,B(y,\lambda)
   \]

3. **Preassigned values:**  
   for each \(\lambda\), \(A(x,\lambda), B(y,\lambda) \in \{\pm 1\}\) are defined for all settings simultaneously.

The **Nawderian claim** is: once you restore the full triadic structure of \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) and allow \(\hat{\boldsymbol{T}}\) to be non‑commuting across its components, this factorization fails in a natural, structured way, so \(|S_{\text{RT}}| > 2\) becomes a **diagnostic** of non‑factorizable resonance‑time ancestry, not spooky action.
