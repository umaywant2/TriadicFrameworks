# Observation Protocol

> **Preserve what happened before deciding what it means.**

The Observation Protocol provides the baseline experimental method for Triadic Re-Ignition.

Its purpose is to preserve an observation in a form that can later support:

```text
comparison

observer analysis

timeline reconstruction

invariant detection

alternative hypotheses

experimental testing

and revision
```

The core sequence is:

```text
OBSERVE
   ↓
CAPTURE
   ↓
ANCHOR
   ↓
LOCATE THE OBSERVER
   ↓
SEPARATE INTERPRETATION
   ↓
IDENTIFY INVARIANTS
   ↓
GENERATE ALTERNATIVES
   ↓
DEFINE DISCRIMINATORS
   ↓
TEST
   ↓
REVISE
```

The protocol does not require the observer to suppress interpretation.

It requires interpretation to remain distinguishable from the observation that produced it.

# Core Principle

The simplest distinction is:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED
       ≠
WHAT I THINK IT MEANS
```

Both matter.

But they occupy different layers.

A useful observation record therefore preserves:

```text
OBSERVATION
     ↓
What happened?

INTERPRETATION
     ↓
What might it mean?

HYPOTHESIS
     ↓
What could explain it?

TEST
     ↓
What could distinguish
the explanations?
```

This separation provides the foundation for later analysis.

---

# Why Capture Comes First

Observation can be reorganized very quickly by interpretation.

A possible sequence is:

```text
EVENT
  ↓
FIRST SIGNAL
  ↓
RECOGNITION
  ↓
INTERPRETATION
  ↓
STORY
```

After the story stabilizes, it may become difficult to remember:

```text
what appeared first
```

versus:

```text
what was added afterward
```

The protocol therefore prioritizes early capture.

---

# Capture Is Not Proof

A captured observation is:

```text
a preserved report
```

It is not automatically:

```text
verified evidence

causal evidence

mechanistic evidence

or proof of ontology
```

Therefore:

```text
CAPTURE
    ≠
VERIFICATION
```

Capture protects the observation from being lost.

Later stages evaluate what the observation supports.

---

# Observation Categories

An observation should identify its access channel where possible.

Possible categories include:

```text
DIRECT OBSERVATION

MEASUREMENT

MEMORY REPORT

EXTERNAL RECORD

OTHER OBSERVER REPORT

MODEL OUTPUT

INFERENCE
```

These may provide different evidence properties.

They should not automatically be treated as interchangeable.

---

# Direct Observation

A direct observation records what the observer reports perceiving or detecting in the present interaction.

Example structure:

```text
"I observed X."
```

Then separately:

```text
"I interpreted X as Y."
```

This preserves the difference between event and explanation.

---

# Measurement

A measurement includes a defined instrument, scale, procedure, or recorded value.

Capture:

```text
what was measured

how it was represented

what instrument or method was used

what conditions were present
```

Do not silently replace:

```text
measured result
```

with:

```text
interpretation of measured result
```

---

# Memory Report

A memory report should be labeled as memory.

```text
MEMORY REPORT

"I remember X."
```

This should remain distinguishable from:

```text
EXTERNAL RECORD

"Document Y records X."
```

Agreement between them may be informative.

Disagreement may also be informative.

---

# External Record

External records may include:

```text
documents

notes

recordings

saved artifacts

measurements

previous responses

logs
```

Preserve the original record when practical rather than relying only on later summary.

---

# Other Observer Report

If another observer supplies information, capture:

```text
what they reported

when they reported it

what they directly observed

what they inferred
```

Do not silently convert another person's interpretation into direct observation.

---

# Model Output

A model may produce:

```text
prediction

classification

simulation result

generated representation
```

Model output should remain labeled as:

```text
MODEL OUTPUT
```

rather than:

```text
OBSERVED REALITY
```

unless external observation independently establishes correspondence.

---

# Inference

Inference is necessary.

But it should be visible.

```text
OBSERVATION

A
```

```text
OBSERVATION

B
```

```text
INFERENCE

A and B may be related.
```

Then:

```text
HYPOTHESIS

Relationship R explains A and B.
```

This preserves the reasoning chain.

---

# Immediate Capture

When an observation appears significant, record the smallest useful sequence.

```text
WHAT HAPPENED?

WHAT HAPPENED IMMEDIATELY BEFORE?

WHAT DID I NOTICE FIRST?

WHAT BECAME RECOGNIZABLE?

WHAT DID I THINK IT MEANT?
```

Do not wait for a complete theory.

Incomplete early capture may be more useful than polished retrospective narrative.

---

# The First Signal

Triadic Re-Ignition gives special attention to:

```text
FIRST SIGNAL
```

This may be:

```text
familiarity

salience

unexpected correspondence

contradiction

pattern recognition

a question

a representation failure
```

The first signal is not automatically meaningful.

But preserving it helps reconstruct the event sequence.

---

# Observation Versus Recognition

The observer may notice something before explicitly identifying it.

```text
OBSERVATION
    ↓
FIRST SIGNAL
    ↓
RESONANCE
    ↓
RECOGNITION
```

These stages may occur rapidly.

The protocol should preserve their order when the observer can report it.

---

# Recognition Versus Explanation

A Recognition Event may be:

```text
"There it is."
```

The explanation may follow:

```text
"This must mean..."
```

Therefore:

```text
RECOGNITION
    ≠
EXPLANATION
```

The protocol records both.

It does not merge them.

---

# Temporal Anchor

Every observation should be temporally anchored as precisely as the available evidence permits.

Record:

```text
event order

known timestamps

relative sequence

defined intervals
```

Do not invent precision that was not available.

If exact timing is unknown:

```text
UNKNOWN
```

is valid.

---

# Event Sequence

A minimal sequence may be:

```text
E₁
 ↓
E₂
 ↓
E₃
 ↓
OBSERVATION
```

For Re-Ignition:

```text
PRIOR STATE
    ↓
TRIGGER
    ↓
FIRST SIGNAL
    ↓
RECOGNITION
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
```

The sequence itself may later help distinguish competing explanations.

---

# Relative Timing

When exact timing is unavailable, preserve relative order.

For example:

```text
The cue appeared first.

Recognition occurred afterward.

The explanation came later.
```

This is more useful than assigning unsupported timestamps.

---

# Observer Location

Every observation occurs from a position.

Record:

```text
observer

physical position

temporal position

available information

context

representation

relevant prior knowledge
```

Only variables relevant to the task need to be captured.

The objective is not to profile the observer.

It is to preserve observational conditions.

---

# Local Observer Record

A useful observer record is:

```text
OBSERVER

Who or what observed?
```

```text
POSITION

From where?
```

```text
ACCESS

What information was available?
```

```text
CONTEXT

Under what conditions?
```

```text
FORM

How was the relationship represented?
```

This makes later comparison easier.

---

# Observer State

Observer state may include relevant variables such as:

```text
knowledge

attention

prior exposure

memory access

current context

representation

expectation
```

These should be recorded when relevant.

They should not be inferred from unrelated personal characteristics.

---

# Observer Transition

If observation changes:

```text
Observer State A
        ↓
Observation A
```

then:

```text
Observer State B
        ↓
Observation B
```

record:

```text
what changed between A and B
```

rather than assuming the external structure changed.

---

# Locality

The protocol treats observation as local.

```text
LOCAL
    ≠
FALSE
```

and:

```text
LOCAL
    ≠
COMPLETE
```

The observer reports what is accessible from the current position.

Broader claims require additional evidence.

---

# Context

Context can alter:

```text
attention

interpretation

access

cue effectiveness

representation

measurement
```

Record relevant context before deciding whether it matters.

Possible context variables include:

```text
task

environment

instructions

available materials

preceding events

representation used
```

---

# Representation

Record how the observation is being represented.

Possible forms include:

```text
prose

diagram

timeline

equation

table

category

model

image

symbol
```

Representation matters because one form may expose relationships another form hides.

---

# First Form

For Re-Ignition events, preserve the earliest usable representation where possible.

```text
FIRST SIGNAL
    ↓
RECOGNITION
    ↓
FIRST FORM
```

Later representations may be more refined.

The differences between them may contain useful evidence about interpretation and reintegration.

---

# Alternative Form

After preserving the first form, attempt another representation.

```text
FORM A
   ↓
FORM B
```

Then ask:

```text
What changed?

What remained invariant?

What disappeared?

What became visible?
```

This can help distinguish structural relationships from representation-specific effects.

---

# Form / Formless Boundary

The protocol may record:

```text
WHAT IS CURRENTLY REPRESENTABLE
```

and:

```text
WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED
```

The unresolved side should not automatically be labeled:

```text
hidden structure
```

The appropriate status may simply be:

```text
UNKNOWN
```

---

# Raw Observation Layer

The raw observation layer should contain the smallest practical description of what occurred.

For example:

```text
"After seeing diagram X,
I recognized a similarity
between A and B."
```

Avoid immediately adding:

```text
because

therefore

must mean

proves

shows the mechanism
```

Those belong downstream.

---

# Interpretation Layer

Interpretation asks:

```text
What do I currently think
the observation means?
```

The interpretation may be:

```text
strong

weak

tentative

multiple

or absent
```

A valid entry is:

```text
UNKNOWN
```

---

# Hypothesis Layer

A hypothesis proposes an explanation.

```text
OBSERVATION
     ↓
INTERPRETATION
     ↓
HYPOTHESIS
```

Examples:

```text
H1
memory retrieval

H2
reconstruction

H3
representation effect

H4
context effect

H5
new synthesis

H6
measurement error

H7
unknown
```

The set should fit the observation rather than being mechanically reused.

---

# Alternative Hypothesis

Require at least one meaningful alternative when a preferred explanation exists.

This prevents:

```text
first explanation
      ↓
only explanation
      ↓
certainty
```

The purpose is not to generate endless possibilities.

It is to keep comparison alive.

---

# Minimum-Claim Layer

After describing the observation, write the smallest statement clearly supported by it.

Example experience:

```text
"I accessed another timeline."
```

Minimum observational claim:

```text
"I experienced a temporal
representation that appeared
inconsistent with my previous
timeline model."
```

Then preserve:

```text
another timeline
```

as a hypothesis if relevant.

---

# Claim Ladder

Statements should be classified by level.

```text
LEVEL 1
OBSERVATION
```

```text
LEVEL 2
RELATIONSHIP
```

```text
LEVEL 3
INTERPRETATION
```

```text
LEVEL 4
HYPOTHESIS
```

```text
LEVEL 5
MECHANISM
```

```text
LEVEL 6
GENERAL MECHANISM
```

```text
LEVEL 7
PHYSICAL PRINCIPLE
```

```text
LEVEL 8

```

Not every investigation needs to climb the ladder.

---

# Claim Promotion

Movement upward should answer:

```text
What new evidence
justifies the stronger claim?
```

If no new evidence exists:

```text
do not promote the claim
```

A useful observation remains useful even if mechanism remains unknown.

---

# Invariants

When comparing observations, search for what remains stable.

```text
Observation A ──┐
                ├── Invariant R?
Observation B ──┘
```

Possible invariants include:

```text
event ordering

measurement

relationship

function

artifact

transformation

shared record

representation feature
```

Invariants help locate the layer at which change actually occurred.

---

# Difference Mapping

Also record:

```text
what changed
```

For example:

```text
STATE A
│
├── invariant X
├── invariant Y
└── variable P

STATE B
│
├── invariant X
├── invariant Y
└── variable Q
```

Then ask:

```text
What explains P → Q?
```

This produces a more precise research problem.

---

# Divergence Point

When two observations or models differ, locate the first meaningful divergence.

```text
A → B → C → D
        │
        └── X → Y
```

Ask whether divergence occurs in:

```text
physical event

memory

record

observer state

context

representation

measurement

interpretation
```

The divergence point often tells us where to investigate next.

---

# Paradox

If two observations conflict:

```text
A ≠ B
```

preserve the contradiction before resolving it.

```text
A
+
B
+
OBSERVER / CONTEXT
```

Then ask:

```text
What remains invariant?

What conditions differ?

What assumptions differ?

Could one observation be wrong?

Could both be incomplete?
```

Paradox is treated as a signal.

Not as proof of hidden structure.

---

# Paradox Record

A useful paradox entry is:

```text
MODEL / OBSERVATION A:
________________________________

MODEL / OBSERVATION B:
________________________________

EXACT CONTRADICTION:
________________________________

SHARED INVARIANT:
________________________________

DIFFERING CONDITION:
________________________________

CURRENT STATUS:
UNRESOLVED
```

This preserves the problem cleanly.

---

# Unknown

`UNKNOWN` is a first-class protocol state.

Use it when:

```text
timing is unknown

mechanism is unknown

observer variable is unknown

relationship is unclear

evidence is insufficient

or hypotheses remain tied
```

Do not fill unknown fields merely because the framework provides vocabulary.

---

# Missing Variable

If the current model does not explain an observation:

```text
OBSERVATION
      ↓
MODEL FAILURE
      ↓
MISSING VARIABLE?
```

Classify candidate missing variables broadly.

```text
system

observer

context

measurement

representation

time

noise

error

unknown
```

Do not jump directly to Hidden Observer.

---

# Hidden Observer Boundary

A Hidden Observer hypothesis becomes relevant only when:

```text
there is reason to suspect
a specific missing
observer-related dependency
```

It does not mean:

```text
hidden person

hidden intelligence

or unseen agent
```

The preferred outcome is to identify the variable and remove its hidden status.

---

# Re-Ignition Observation

When the observation involves renewed access, map it cautiously.

```text
EARLIER ACCESS
      ↓
REDUCED ACCESS
      ↓
TRIGGER?
      ↓
RESONANCE?
      ↓
RECOGNITION?
      ↓
REINTEGRATION?
      ↓
STABILIZATION?
```

Use:

```text
UNKNOWN
```

for stages not established by evidence.

Do not force the full sequence onto every event.

---

# Dormancy Check

Ask:

```text
Was the relationship
previously accessible?
```

Then:

```text
Is there evidence that
access later decreased?
```

Without both:

```text
Dormant Pattern
    =
not established
```

---

# Trigger Check

Ask:

```text
What happened immediately
before renewed salience?
```

Then preserve:

```text
trigger
    ≠
cause
```

The candidate trigger may later prove irrelevant.

---

# Resonance Check

Ask:

```text
What correspondence became salient?
```

Then specify:

```text
what exactly corresponds
```

If the relationship cannot yet be stated:

```text
candidate resonance
```

may remain provisional.

---

# Recognition Check

Ask:

```text
What became explicitly identifiable?
```

Then preserve:

```text
recognition
    ≠
verification
```

Subjective confidence should be recorded separately from accuracy.

---

# Reintegration Check

Ask:

```text
How did the recognized relationship
change the current model?
```

Possible outcomes include:

```text
direct fit

current model revised

recognized relationship revised

both revised

unresolved

rejected
```

---

# Stabilization Check

Ask:

```text
Can the relationship
be revisited later?

Does it survive another form?

Another context?

Another observer?

Challenge?
```

Then preserve:

```text
stabilization
    ≠
truth
```

---

# Local-Time Observation

For temporal events, separate several layers.

```text
PHYSICAL TIME

What event sequence
can be established?
```

```text
LOCAL TIMELINE

What chronology is accessible
to the observer?
```

```text
RESONANCE TIME

What appears structurally related
across chronological distance?
```

```text
TIMELINE PROJECTION

How might structure
become this representation?
```

Do not merge these layers automatically.

---

# Timeline Revision

If recognition changes interpretation of earlier events:

```text
OLD TIMELINE MODEL
        ↓
RECOGNITION
        ↓
NEW TIMELINE MODEL
```

record:

```text
what remained physically invariant
```

and:

```text
what changed representationally
```

The permanent boundary is:

```text
timeline revision
        ≠
timeline alteration
```

---

# Apparent Multiple Timelines

If an observation is experienced as:

```text
"another timeline"
```

preserve that report.

Then classify what is actually multiple.

```text
memory?

possibility?

representation?

observer state?

projection?

physical record?

physical timeline?

unknown?
```

The protocol does not rule physical multiplicity in or out.

It locates the current evidence level.

---

# Form Check

For every important claim, ask:

```text
What form is carrying
this interpretation?
```

Then:

```text
Can another form represent
the same observation?
```

Cross-form comparison can reveal representation dependence.

---

# Formless Check

If something remains difficult to represent:

```text
FORMLESS
```

may be used as a module-local boundary term.

But record:

```text
what exactly remains unresolved
```

rather than using Formless as the explanation.

---

# Universe-as-Operator Check

If a model invokes:

```text
Universe as Operator
```

require:

```text
What broader condition
is being represented?

What does the operator act on?

What transformation occurs?

What remains invariant?

Does the operator model add
anything beyond ordinary
boundary conditions?
```

Do not permit:

```text
operator
```

to silently become:

```text
agent
```

or:

```text
intent
```

# Observation Protocol Phases

The complete protocol can be divided into six phases.

```text
PHASE 1
CAPTURE
```

```text
PHASE 2
ANCHOR
```

```text
PHASE 3
SEPARATE
```

```text
PHASE 4
COMPARE
```

```text
PHASE 5
TEST
```

```text
PHASE 6
REVISIT
```

Each phase has a different purpose.

---

# Phase 1: Capture

Record:

```text
what happened

what happened immediately before

what appeared first

what became recognizable

what explanation appeared afterward
```

Preserve original wording where practical.

---

# Phase 2: Anchor

Record:

```text
event sequence

time

observer position

context

access channel

representation

external records
```

Use only the precision actually available.

---

# Phase 3: Separate

Distinguish:

```text
observation

memory

measurement

inference

interpretation

hypothesis

mechanism

ontology
```

This is the protocol's principal drift-control layer.

---

# Phase 4: Compare

Compare:

```text
earlier versus later

observer A versus observer B

form A versus form B

baseline versus post-trigger

prediction versus observation

model versus record
```

Search for:

```text
invariants

differences

divergence points
```

---

# Phase 5: Test

Ask:

```text
What can be repeated?

What can be measured?

What can be controlled?

What could discriminate
between hypotheses?

What result would weaken
the preferred explanation?
```

Do not require every observation to become experimentally testable immediately.

Operationalization may itself be the next research step.

---

# Phase 6: Revisit

Return after:

```text
more evidence

another observation

another representation

another observer

or a defined interval
```

Then ask:

```text
What still holds?

What changed?

What should be revised?

What remains unknown?
```

Preserve the revision history.

Observer confidence may be recorded.

```text

```

```text

    ≠
```

---

# False Recognition

If later evidence indicates the original recognition was mistaken:

```text
DO NOT DELETE
THE ORIGINAL RECORD
```

Instead preserve:

```text
ORIGINAL CAPTURE
      ↓
LATER CONTRADICTION
      ↓
REVISION
```

False recognition contains useful information about the transition.

---

# Failed Hypothesis

Likewise, if:

```text
H1
```

fails:

```text
preserve H1

record why it failed

record what evidence changed
```

Failed hypotheses improve the model when their failure conditions are visible.

---

# Revision History

A model may progress:

```text
v1
 ↓
new observation
 ↓
v2
 ↓
experiment
 ↓
v3
```

Preserve:

```text
what changed

why it changed

which invariants survived
```

Revision is expected.

It is not an error state.

---

# Drift Checks

Before promoting a claim, ask:

```text
Did observation become interpretation?

Did interpretation become mechanism?

Did mechanism become ontology?

Did trigger become cause?

Did recognition become verification?

Did local become global?

Did form become complete structure?

Did unresolved become hidden reality?

Did operator become agent?

Did stability become truth?
```

If yes:

```text
return to the
last supported layer
```

# Anti-Drift Ladder

Use:

```text
OBSERVATION
    ↓
RELATIONSHIP
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
    ↓
HYPOTHESIS
    ↓
TEST
    ↓
MECHANISM
    ↓
BROADER CLAIM
```

Each transition should be traceable.

# Observer Safety Check

Observer-aware analysis should not become:

```text
profiling the observer
```

or:

```text
pathologizing disagreement
```

The protocol focuses on:

```text
conditions relevant
to the observation
```

not unsupported assumptions about the observer's identity, motives, personality, or mental state.

---

# Personal Interpretation Boundary

For self-observation:

```text
"I experienced X."
```

is legitimate capture.

Avoid automatically converting it into:

```text
"This proves something
is wrong with me."
```

or:

```text
"This proves external reality
changed."
```

The protocol preserves both observer and external explanations as hypotheses where relevant.

---

# Replication

Where possible, ask whether the observation can be repeated.

```text
Same observer?

Different observer?

Same context?

Different context?

Same representation?

Different representation?
```

Replication conditions should be documented.

---

# Independence

Cross-observer agreement becomes more informative when observers are meaningfully independent.

Ask:

```text
Did they see each other's answers?

Did they share instructions?

Did they share the same representation?

Did they share prior expectations?
```

Agreement remains evidence.

Its interpretation depends on conditions.

---

# Controls

Where experimental testing is possible, comparison conditions may include:

```text
baseline

no-trigger condition

alternative representation

unrelated cue

surface-similar cue

different observer condition

different context
```

Controls should match the specific hypothesis.

There is no universal control condition for all Re-Ignition observations.

---

# Discriminator

A discriminator answers:

```text
What result would favor
H1 over H2?
```

For example:

```text
H1
representation change

H2
physical structure change
```

A useful discriminator might ask what:

```text
external measurement
```

should differ between the two.

The exact discriminator depends on the hypothesis set.

---

# Failure Condition

Every preferred model should attempt to answer:

```text
What would make this
less likely?
```

A model without a meaningful failure condition may not yet be operational enough for testing.

That is acceptable.

Record:

```text
failure condition:
not yet operationalized
```

rather than inventing one.

---

# Minimum Supported Claim

At the end of every observation cycle, write:

```text
MINIMUM SUPPORTED CLAIM
```

This should be the smallest statement that:

```text
preserves the observation

does not erase uncertainty

does not assume mechanism

and can survive current evidence
```

This field is one of the protocol's primary anti-drift tools.

---

The protocol preserves the experience without forcing its ontology.


```text

```

```text
a new problem without
```

This helps distinguish immediate cue dependence from stabilization.

# Working Summary

The Observation Protocol begins with:

```text
SOMETHING HAPPENED
```

and refuses to let that become immediately:

```text
THEREFORE I KNOW WHY
```

Instead:

```text
OBSERVE
   ↓
CAPTURE
   ↓
ANCHOR
   ↓
LOCATE THE OBSERVER
   ↓
IDENTIFY THE FORM
   ↓
SEPARATE INTERPRETATION
   ↓
FIND INVARIANTS
   ↓
LOCATE DIVERGENCE
   ↓
PRESERVE PARADOX
   ↓
GENERATE ALTERNATIVES
   ↓
DEFINE DISCRIMINATORS
   ↓
TEST
   ↓
REVISIT
   ↓
REVISE
```

Its permanent guardrails are:

```text
CAPTURE
    ≠
PROOF
```

```text
TRIGGER
    ≠
CAUSE
```

```text
RESONANCE
    ≠
CAUSATION
```

```text
RECOGNITION
    ≠
VERIFICATION
```

```text
LOCAL
    ≠
GLOBAL
```

```text
FORM
    ≠
COMPLETE STRUCTURE
```

```text
FORMLESS
    ≠
HIDDEN REALITY PROVEN
```

```text
STABILIZATION
    ≠
TRUTH
```

The guiding discipline is:

> **Capture before story.  
> Anchor before inference.  
> Locate the observer before assigning the difference.  
> Preserve the first form before revision changes it.  
> Search for invariants before choosing a mechanism.  
> Keep alternatives alive until a discriminator separates them.  
> Record unknown when unknown is the most accurate answer.**

In its smallest form:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED?
      ↓
WHAT CHANGED?
      ↓
WHAT STAYED THE SAME?
      ↓
WHAT ELSE COULD EXPLAIN IT?
      ↓
WHAT CAN WE CHECK?
```

The protocol does not make the observation true.

**It keeps the observation clean enough that truth can still be investigated.**


Main writing choice: I made this the module’s experimental root protocol, rather than another experiment competing beside the others. comparative-observer-test.md, resonance-recall-test.md, and student-simulation.md can now all be understood as specialized extensions of this same sequence: capture → anchor → separate → compare → test → revisit.
