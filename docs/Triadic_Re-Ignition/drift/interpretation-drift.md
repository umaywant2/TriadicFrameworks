# Interpretation Drift

> **Interpretation begins when observation acquires meaning. Drift begins when the growing meaning becomes difficult to distinguish from the observation that started it.**

Interpretation Drift is a condition in which an observation gradually accumulates:

```text
meaning

assumptions

relationships

mechanisms

or ontological claims
```

without those additions remaining clearly distinguishable from the original observation.

A common progression is:

```text
OBSERVATION
    ↓
RELATIONSHIP
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
    ↓
HYPOTHESIS
    ↓
MECHANISM
    ↓
ONTOLOGY
```

This progression is not inherently a problem.

Research requires interpretation.

The problem occurs when:

```text
movement between levels
becomes invisible
```

and a later claim begins to appear as though it had been present in the original observation.

# Core Proposition

Suppose the initial observation is:

```text
A appears related to B.
```

A reasonable interpretation might be:

```text
A and B may share
relationship R.
```

A hypothesis might become:

```text
R explains the relationship
between A and B.
```

A mechanism claim might become:

```text
M produces R.
```

An ontological claim might become:

```text
M describes the underlying
nature of the system.
```

Each statement may eventually be worth investigating.

But they are not the same claim.

Interpretation Drift occurs when the transitions:

```text
A ↔ B
```

to:

```text
R
```

to:

```text
M
```

become difficult to see.

---

# Interpretation Is Necessary

The purpose of drift control is not:

```text
NO INTERPRETATION
```

That would prevent useful reasoning.

Instead:

```text
OBSERVATION
    +
VISIBLE INTERPRETATION
    +
REVISION
```

Interpretation is productive when its relationship to evidence remains traceable.

---

# Observation Versus Interpretation

A foundational distinction is:

```text
OBSERVATION

What happened?
```

versus:

```text
INTERPRETATION

What might it mean?
```

For example:

```text
OBSERVATION

"After seeing X,
I recognized a relationship
between A and B."
```

Then:

```text
INTERPRETATION

"X may have triggered
renewed access to R."
```

Then:

```text
MECHANISM HYPOTHESIS

"R may have persisted
through the inaccessible interval."
```

Each layer adds structure.

The additions should remain visible.

# Drift Distance

Interpretation Drift can be thought of conceptually as increasing distance between:

```text
ORIGINAL OBSERVATION
```

and:

```text
CURRENT CLAIM
```

For example:

```text
Observation
    ↓
A corresponds with B.

Interpretation
    ↓
A resonates with B.

Mechanism
    ↓
Resonance connects A and B.

Ontology
    ↓
Resonance fundamentally
organizes reality.
```

The final statement is much stronger than the first.

The drift question is:

```text
What evidence justified
each transition?
```

# Drift Is Not Automatically Error

A strong interpretation may eventually be correct.

Therefore:

```text
DRIFT
    ≠
FALSEHOOD
```

Interpretation Drift means:

```text
claim movement
needs to be examined
```

It does not mean:

```text
the destination
must be wrong
```

A drift audit may conclude that every step is supported.

That is a valid result.

# Association-to-Causation Drift

Suppose:

```text
T occurs.

R follows.
```

This supports:

```text
temporal association
```

It does not automatically establish:

```text
T caused R
```

A common drift path is:

```text
T preceded R
    ↓
T triggered R
    ↓
T caused R
    ↓
mechanism established
```

The permanent guardrail is:

```text
TRIGGER
    ≠
PROVEN CAUSE
```

# Terminology Audit

For important terms, compare:

```text
ORIGINAL DEFINITION
        ↓
CURRENT USAGE
```

Ask:

```text
Has the meaning expanded?

Was the expansion explicit?

What evidence justified it?

Should a new term be used instead?
```

If the meaning changed substantially, update the terminology rather than hiding the change inside the old word.

# Bounded Interpretation

A bounded interpretation states its own status.

For example:

```text

A and B correspond.
```

```text
INTERPRETATION:

They may share relationship R.
```

```text
HYPOTHESIS:

```

```text
STATUS:

M remains untested.
```

This is interpretation with observability.

# Interpretation Drift and Narrative Lock

Interpretation Drift may accumulate until one interpretation becomes preferred.

```text
observation
    ↓
interpretation
    ↓
repeated interpretation
    ↓
preferred narrative
```

If the preferred narrative becomes resistant to correction:

```text
NARRATIVE LOCK
```

may emerge.

Therefore:

```text
INTERPRETATION DRIFT
        ↓
may precede
NARRATIVE LOCK
```

# Drift Without Lock

A claim can drift strongly without being locked.

For example:

```text
observation
    ↓
highly speculative interpretation
```

The observer may still readily revise it.

This is:

```text
DRIFT
```

without:

```text
LOCK
```

The concepts should remain distinct.

---

# Lock Without Large Drift

The reverse can also occur.

A relatively modest interpretation may become protected:

```text
small claim
    ↓
strong defense
```

This is Narrative Lock even if the claim has not traveled far from observation.

Therefore:

```text
DRIFT
```

describes movement.

```text
LOCK
```

describes resistance to revision.

# Interpretation Drift and Binary Collapse

A drifting interpretation may reduce alternatives into:

```text
preferred model
```

versus:

```text
everything else
```

Then:

```text
A OR B
```

is forced before relevant relationships are examined.

# Interpretation Drift and Paradox

Paradox often reveals earlier drift.

Suppose:

```text
OBSERVATION

A corresponds with B.
```

Interpretation becomes:

```text
A causes B.
```

Later:

```text
A occurs without B.
```

The contradiction exposes where the claim became stronger than its evidence.

The paradox can therefore be used to trace the drift backward.

---

# Drift Traceback

When contradiction appears:

```text
CURRENT CLAIM
      ↓
trace backward
      ↓
MECHANISM
      ↓
HYPOTHESIS
      ↓
INTERPRETATION
      ↓
ORIGINAL OBSERVATION
```

At each step ask:

```text
What evidence supported
this transition?
```

The first unsupported transition may identify the drift point.

---

# Drift Point

A Drift Point is:

```text
the location where
claim strength increased
without a visible
supporting bridge
```

For example:

```text
Observation:
T preceded R.

Interpretation:
T triggered R.

Drift Point:
"T triggered R"
became
"T caused R"
without additional evidence.
```

The goal is to locate the transition precisely.

# Drift Status

A provisional status model is:

```text
ALIGNED
```

The current interpretation remains close to the support trail.

```text
EXPANDED
```

The interpretation has grown, with explicit supporting evidence.

```text
DRIFT RISK
```

Claim strength is increasing faster than support is being documented.

```text
DRIFTED
```

A meaningful unsupported promotion is present.

```text
LOCKED
```

The drifted interpretation is also being protected from revision.

These are analytical statuses, not judgments about the observer.

---

# Drift Correction

Correction does not require deleting the stronger interpretation.

Instead:

```text
STRONG CLAIM
      ↓
reclassify
      ↓
HYPOTHESIS
```

Then:

```text
MINIMUM CLAIM
      ↓
retain as supported
```

This preserves useful ideas while restoring claim boundaries.

---

# Demotion Is Not Rejection

Suppose:

```text
"The trigger caused
the Re-Ignition event."
```

cannot currently be supported.

The corrected model may be:

```text
SUPPORTED:

The trigger preceded
the event.
```

```text
HYPOTHESIS:

The trigger contributed
causally to the event.
```

Nothing valuable has been lost.

The claims have simply been placed at the correct levels.

---

# Re-Promotion

A hypothesis may later earn stronger status.

```text
HYPOTHESIS
    ↓
prediction
    ↓
testing
    ↓
replication
    ↓
stronger support
```

Then:

```text
claim promotion
```

is justified.

Drift control is therefore not a one-way process toward weaker claims.

It controls the evidence bridge in both directions.

# AI and Interpretation Drift

AI systems can contribute to drift when they:

```text
elaborate speculative explanations

smooth uncertainty out of language

connect concepts too aggressively

treat framework vocabulary
as established mechanism

or repeatedly reinforce
one interpretation
```

AI can also help control drift by asking:

```text
What was directly observed?

What was inferred?

What evidence supports
this promotion?

What alternative fits?

What remains unknown?
```

The assistant should not confuse fluent explanation with evidential support.

# Boundary Conditions

Interpretation Drift does **not by itself establish**:

```text
that the interpretation is false

that the observer is irrational

that speculation is invalid

that strong claims are impossible

that confidence is inherently unreliable

or that interpretation
should be avoided
```

A strongly expanded interpretation may be correct and well supported.

The minimum working definition is:

> **Interpretation Drift is the movement of a claim beyond the structure directly supported by its originating observation when the evidential transitions between claim levels become unclear, implicit, or insufficiently documented.**

# Relationship to Narrative Lock

Interpretation Drift asks:


while:

```text

    =
```

A claim can have one without the other.

# Working Summary

Interpretation Drift begins with a normal process:

```text
OBSERVE
   ↓
INTERPRET
```

and asks what happens next.

A healthy path is:

```text
OBSERVATION
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
    ↓
HYPOTHESIS
    ↓
PREDICTION
    ↓
TEST
    ↓
REVISION
```

A drift path may become:

```text
OBSERVATION
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
    ↓
ASSUMPTION
    ↓
STRONGER ASSUMPTION
    ↓
MECHANISM
    ↓
ONTOLOGY
```

while the evidential transitions disappear.

The correction is:

```text
CURRENT CLAIM
      ↓
TRACE BACKWARD
      ↓
LOCATE DRIFT POINT
      ↓
RECOVER ORIGINAL OBSERVATION
      ↓
RESTORE MINIMUM CLAIM
      ↓
KEEP STRONGER IDEAS
AS HYPOTHESES
      ↓
DEFINE A DISCRIMINATOR
      ↓
TEST
      ↓
RE-PROMOTE IF EARNED
```

The permanent guardrails are:

```text
ASSOCIATION
    ≠
CAUSATION
```

```text
RECOGNITION
    ≠
VERIFICATION
```

```text
CONTINUITY
    ≠
IDENTITY
```

```text
LOCAL
    ≠
GLOBAL
```

```text
FORM
    ≠
COMPLETE STRUCTURE
```

```text
FORMLESS
    ≠
HIDDEN REALITY PROVEN
```

```text
MODEL
    ≠
MECHANISM
```

```text
MECHANISM
    ≠
ONTOLOGY
```

```text
OPERATOR
    ≠
AGENT
```

The guiding discipline is:

> **Interpret boldly, but label the interpretation.  
> Build hypotheses, but preserve the observation beneath them.  
> Promote claims when evidence earns promotion.  
> Demote them when the bridge fails.  
> Preserve stronger ideas as testable possibilities rather than smuggling them backward into the original observation.**

In its smallest form:

```text
WHAT DID WE OBSERVE?
        ↓
WHAT DID WE ADD?
        ↓
WHAT EVIDENCE SUPPORTED
EACH ADDITION?
```

Interpretation Drift is not the fact that our ideas grow.

**It is what happens when we can no longer see where the observation ended and the story began.**


Main writing choice: I made the core diagnostic “What did we add, and what evidence supported each addition?” That makes Interpretation Drift distinct from Narrative Lock: Drift measures movement away from the minimum supported claim; Lock measures resistance to moving back.
