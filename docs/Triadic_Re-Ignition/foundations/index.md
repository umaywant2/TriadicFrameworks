# Foundations

> **Triadic Re-Ignition begins with renewed access, then asks what became detectable, how it became representable, and what broader relationships frame the transition.**

The Foundations branch establishes the conceptual structure used throughout Triadic Re-Ignition.

It begins with a deliberately small observation:

```text
something was accessible

        ↓

access became reduced
or unavailable

        ↓

something related became
accessible again
```

From that transition, the Foundations branch develops four primary questions:

```text
RE-IGNITION PRINCIPLE
      ↓
What transition are we studying?

RESONANCE BEFORE FORM
      ↓
What may become detectable
before stable representation?

FORM AND FORMLESS
      ↓
What is represented,
and what remains unresolved?

UNIVERSE AS OPERATOR
      ↓
What broader relational
conditions frame the system?
```

Together:

```text
ACCESS
   ↓
DETECTION
   ↓
REPRESENTATION
   ↓
RELATIONAL CONTEXT
```

These foundations provide an investigative architecture.

They do not predetermine mechanism or ontology.

# 1. Re-Ignition Principle

**File:** `./re-ignition-principle.md`

The Re-Ignition Principle provides the foundational observation:

```text
ACCESSIBLE
    ↓
REDUCED / LOST ACCESS
    ↓
RENEWED ACCESS
```

The smallest supported claim is:

```text
access changed
```

The mechanism remains open.

---

## Re-Ignition Question

The central question is:

> **What actually survives the transition between earlier accessibility and later renewed accessibility?**

Possible interpretations include:

```text
continuous persistence

partial persistence

distributed persistence

memory

reconstruction

relearning

regeneration

new synthesis

or another process
```

The principle does not choose among them by definition.

---

## Re-Ignition Boundary

A renewed relationship should not automatically be interpreted as:

```text
the exact old pattern
returning unchanged
```

Nor should it automatically be interpreted as:

```text
something completely new
```

Instead:

```text
EARLIER STATE
      ↓
DORMANT / INACCESSIBLE INTERVAL
      ↓
RENEWED ACCESS
      ↓
LATER STATE
```

Then ask:

```text
What remained invariant?

What changed?

What was reconstructed?

What was added?

What was lost?
```

# 2. Resonance Before Form

**File:** `./resonance-before-form.md`

Resonance Before Form examines whether a relationship may become salient or recognizable before the observer has produced a stable explicit representation.

The working proposition is:

```text
RESONANCE
    ↓
RECOGNITION
    ↓
FORM
    ↓
REPRESENTATION
```

The important phrase is:

```text
working proposition
```

The ordering remains something to investigate.

---

## Detection Before Explanation

An observer may experience:

```text
"Something is here."
```

before:

```text
"I know how to explain it."
```

This creates a possible distinction between:

```text
DETECTION
```

and:

```text
REPRESENTATION
```

The Foundations branch preserves that distinction without requiring a specific mechanism.

---

## Resonance Boundary

The proposition does not establish:

```text
a hidden realm

representation-independent physics

nonlocal information

or a metaphysical substrate
```

The minimum claim is smaller:

```text
recognition may sometimes
precede stable explicit form
```

That question can be investigated.

# 3. Form and Formless

**File:** `./form-and-formless.md`

Form and Formless defines the representational boundary used throughout the module.

```text
FORM
    =
relationship represented
through accessible structure
```

```text
FORMLESS
    =
relationship not yet,
or not fully,
resolved by the
current representation
```

This creates two permanent guardrails:

```text
FORM
    ≠
COMPLETE STRUCTURE
```

and:

```text
FORMLESS
    ≠
HIDDEN REALITY PROVEN
```

---

## Representation Boundary

The model can be represented as:

```text
UNRESOLVED RELATIONSHIPS
          ↓
------------------------------
   REPRESENTATION BOUNDARY
------------------------------
          ↓
         FORM
          ↓
       OBSERVER
```

The boundary may move through:

```text
learning

measurement

new context

new representation

observer transition

or additional evidence
```

Therefore Formless is not necessarily permanent.

---

## The Important Question

The useful question is not simply:

```text
FORM
or
FORMLESS?
```

It is:

```text
What does the current form preserve?

What does it fail to resolve?

What changes when the form changes?

What remains invariant?
```

The boundary itself becomes available for investigation.

# Foundation Claim Ladder

Foundation claims can be organized by increasing strength.

### Level 1: Observation

```text
A relationship became accessible
or representable.
```

### Level 2: Structural Relationship

```text
A recurring transition or invariant
can be identified.
```

### Level 3: Process Model

```text
A defined architecture describes
the transition.
```

### Level 4: Operational Model

```text
Variables and failure conditions
can be specified.
```

### Level 5: Formal Model

```text
The relationship can be expressed
through a rigorous formal system.
```

### Level 6: Mechanism

```text
A defined process explains
why the transition occurs.
```

### Level 7: Physical Principle

```text
The mechanism generalizes
as a physical process.
```

### Level 8: Ontological Claim

```text
The model describes the
underlying nature of reality.
```

The current Foundations branch is primarily concerned with the earlier structural and operational levels.

> Let unresolved structure remain unresolved.  
> Add the relational third position before collapsing into binaries.  
> Make every stronger claim earn its way into the model.**
