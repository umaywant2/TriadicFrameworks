# 🌗 **SCENE 3 — “THE FIRST LESSON”**  
### *Final · Polished · Canonical*

## **Purpose of the Scene**  
Scene 3 is the moment Little Gravity receives the *first real teaching* — not a lecture, not an explanation, but a **felt demonstration** of what gravity *is* in the Little Science universe:

> **Gravity is the art of holding things together without touching them.**

This is the emotional and conceptual anchor for the entire book.

---

# **Scene 3 — Full Narrative**

The morning mist still clung to the stones of the Apprentice Yard when Little Gravity arrived, cloak trailing behind him like a soft, falling wave. The Master was already there, standing in the center of the courtyard, hands folded behind his back, watching the sky as if waiting for something only he could see.

Little Gravity approached quietly, unsure if he should speak.

The Master didn’t turn.  
He simply said:

**“Tell me what you think gravity is.”**

Little Gravity swallowed.  
He had practiced this answer.

“Gravity is… the force that pulls things down.”

The Master finally looked at him — not unkindly, but with the faintest smile of someone who has heard a thousand apprentices say the same thing.

“Down?” the Master asked.  
“Show me where ‘down’ is.”

Little Gravity pointed to the ground.

The Master shook his head.

“That is *your* down. But what about the bird above us? What about the moon? What about the drifting ember from last night’s fire? Each has its own down. So tell me again: what is gravity?”

Little Gravity hesitated.  
His certainty wavered.

The Master raised his hand.

A small stone lifted from the ground — not rising, not falling, simply *held* in place, as if the world itself were taking a breath.

“Gravity,” the Master said, “is not a direction.”

The stone trembled gently, suspended in the air.

“It is a **relationship**.”

The stone drifted toward the Master’s palm, as if drawn by a quiet promise.

“It is the art of holding things together,” he continued, “without touching them. It is the quiet agreement between things that they will not drift apart.”

The stone settled softly into his hand.

Little Gravity felt something shift inside him — a recognition, a resonance, as if a door had opened in a room he didn’t know he carried.

The Master stepped closer.

“Your task,” he said, “is not to pull. Not to push. Not to force.”

He placed the stone in Little Gravity’s hands.

“Your task is to **listen** to the space between things.”

Little Gravity looked down at the stone.  
It felt heavier than before — not in weight, but in meaning.

The Master’s voice softened.

“Gravity is the first kindness of the universe. It keeps things from being alone.”

Little Gravity looked up, eyes wide.

And for the first time, he understood why he had been chosen.
