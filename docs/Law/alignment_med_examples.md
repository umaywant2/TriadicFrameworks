# 🟡 **MEDIUM ALIGNMENT (AACC)**  
### *Central laws with contested interpretation, political charge, or uneven enforcement.*

RTT Governance places AACC laws in the **Process Substrate**, where drift forces are active but not catastrophic.

These laws generate **tension** between formal rules and real‑world application.

## **For People Upholding the Law**  
- **High interpretive load** — officers and judges must “choose a lane.”  
- **Inconsistent enforcement** — jurisdictional variance increases drift.  
- **Political pressure** — decisions may be scrutinized or weaponized.  
- **Procedural friction** — more hearings, appeals, and administrative churn.  
- **Identity stress** — enforcers must justify decisions more often.

## **For People Subjected to the Law**  
- **Uncertainty** — outcomes depend on jurisdiction, judge, or agency.  
- **Perceived unfairness** — similar cases yield different results.  
- **Higher emotional load** — fear, confusion, and distrust increase.  
- **Greater need for representation** — lawyers become essential navigators.  
- **Risk of selective enforcement** — marginalized groups feel pressure.

### **RTT Summary**  
AACC laws create **process drift**, **interpretive tension**, and **identity stress** for both sides.  
They are structurally important but unstable.
