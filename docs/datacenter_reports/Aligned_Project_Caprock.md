### Thermal envelope, cooling coherence, seasonal drift  
- **Climate profile:** Semi‑arid, high‑insolation, large diurnal swings, hot summers, cool winters, with strong wind presence typical of the Texas Panhandle.  
- **Cooling architecture:** High‑density AI loads are matched with liquid‑to‑chip and advanced air‑cooled rejection, tuned for high dry‑bulb temperatures and low relative humidity. - **Seismic regime:** West Texas and the South Plains are low‑to‑moderate seismicity compared to coastal or plate‑boundary regions; primary geophysical variability is more tied to subsurface extraction (oil/gas) than tectonics.  
- **Structural implication:** The campus sits in a relatively stable crustal environment with low expected high‑magnitude events; design focus is more on wind loading, hail, and storm hardening than on major seismic isolation.

- **Topology:** The site is positioned near Abernathy, with access to multiple long‑haul fiber routes and carrier‑neutral connectivity, leveraging regional paths that link Lubbock and broader Texas backbones.   [dcpulse.com](https://dcpulse.com/project/aligned-project-caprock-540mw-texas)

### Environmental continuity and substrate fatigue  
- **Land use:** 313‑acre campus in an agricultural/energy corridor, with large contiguous land and relatively low surrounding structural density.
