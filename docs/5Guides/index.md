# Awareness → Alignment → Coherence → Clarity → Continuity

Welcome to **5Guides**.

You don't need to know TriadicFrameworks.

You don't need to know mathematics.

You don't need to know Lean.

You don't need an AI assistant.

You just need something you're curious enough to look at twice.

---

# Start With Five Questions

The Five Guides are built around five simple questions:

| Guide | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | **What am I observing?** |
| **Alignment** | **What corresponds?** |
| **Coherence** | **Does this hold together?** |
| **Clarity** | **Can this be understood and inspected?** |
| **Continuity** | **What persists through change?** |

You'll meet them in this order:

```text
Awareness
    ↓
Alignment
    ↓
Coherence
    ↓
Clarity
    ↓
Continuity
```

But they aren't meant to stay in separate boxes.

As you progress, you'll begin using earlier questions while learning the next one.

Eventually they become a small toolkit.

---

# 1. Awareness 👀

> ## What am I observing?

Let's start before explanation.

Before deciding what something means, ask:

> **What is actually here?**

You'll practice noticing the difference between:

- what you observe;
- what you interpret;
- what you assume;
- what you don't know;
- what might be missing;
- and where you're observing from.

You'll play with viewpoints, blind spots, imagination, everyday objects, and small systems.

You may discover something surprisingly difficult:

> **What seems obvious isn't always what was actually observed.**

# 2. Alignment 🔗

> ## What corresponds?

Now suppose you have two observations.

Do they connect?

Maybe.

Alignment isn't about **making** things match.

You'll learn to ask:

- What actually corresponds?
- What only looks similar?
- What doesn't match?
- What assumptions connect these pieces?
- Is the relationship complete, partial, or conditional?
- What happens when the relationship begins to drift?

And sometimes:

> **Should these things align at all?**

A mismatch isn't automatically a problem.

Sometimes it's the most useful thing you found.

# 3. Coherence 🧩

> ## Does this hold together?

Here's where things get interesting.

Individual pieces can look perfectly reasonable...

while the whole structure makes no sense.

You'll practice finding:

- contradictions;
- missing connections;
- unsupported conclusions;
- incompatible assumptions;
- and pieces that work individually but conflict when combined.

When something doesn't hold together, don't immediately repair it.

First ask:

> **Where does the structure stop working?**

Finding the break is part of understanding the system.

### Continue

[Explore Coherence](./coherence/)

---

# 4. Clarity 🔎

> ## Can this be understood well enough to inspect?

You can understand something perfectly well inside your own head...

and explain it so badly that nobody else has any idea what you're talking about. 😉

Clarity helps make structure visible.

You'll practice exposing:

- important definitions;
- relationships;
- assumptions;
- missing steps;
- ambiguity;
- uncertainty;
- and the path between an observation and a conclusion.

But there's a catch.

> **Making something simpler isn't helpful if you quietly change what it means.**

Clarity means making something easier to inspect **while preserving what matters**.

### Continue

[Explore Clarity](./clarity/)

# 5. Continuity 🔄

> ## What persists through change?

Nothing stays perfectly still.

Systems change.

People learn.

Evidence accumulates.

Models get revised.

States transition.

Patterns repeat, drift, break, and sometimes return.

You'll practice asking:

- What changed?
- What persisted?
- What returned?
- What drifted?
- What broke?
- What remains connected to what came before?
- Can I trace how we got here?

Continuity brings the earlier Guides together.

# How the Guides Work

Each Guide follows a familiar rhythm:

## Explore

Meet the idea through simple examples, games, observations, or puzzles.

## Practice

Use the idea deliberately.

Move from everyday examples into systems and more structured problems.

## Demonstrate

Try the skill on something fresh.

Not from memory.

Not by repeating a definition.

By **using it**.

## Reflect

Ask:

> **What changed about how I approached the problem?**

Then continue.

The overall rhythm is:

```text
Experience
    ↓
Notice
    ↓
Inspect
    ↓
Apply
    ↓
Demonstrate
    ↓
Reflect
    ↓
Continue
```

# There Is No Rush

These aren't timed lessons.

You don't need to get everything on the first pass.

In fact, one of the first things we'll practice is being comfortable saying:

> **I don't know yet.**

That's not an empty answer.

Sometimes it means:

> I know what information I'm missing.

That's progress.

---

# Awareness Checks

Some Guides include an optional **Awareness Check**.

This asks:

> **What stuck after my first encounter?**

It's not meant to punish you for forgetting something.

It's a way to notice:

- what you already understand;
- what you partly understand;
- what you interpreted differently;
- and what might deserve another look.

An Awareness Check is not the same thing as mastery.

---

# Mastery

Later, you'll encounter a **Mastery Challenge**.

The question changes from:

> **What do I remember?**

to:

> **Can I use this?**

You'll receive a fresh problem and apply what you've learned.

If something doesn't work, you haven't reached the end.

You've discovered something worth revisiting.

> **Retry is part of the learning process.**

# AI Is Optional 🤖

You can complete 5Guides without AI.

If you do use an AI assistant, treat it as a **thinking partner**, not an answer machine.

Try questions like:

> **Challenge my assumption.**

> **What might I be missing?**

> **Give me another interpretation.**

> **Find a mismatch I may have overlooked.**

> **What would make this explanation fail?**

> **Can you help me make this clearer without changing its meaning?**

And remember:

> **The AI's answer is also something you can inspect.**

It can be questioned.

It can misunderstand.

It can make assumptions.

It can be wrong.

You still make the final judgment.

# Want to Go Deeper? — Lean 4

There's an optional formal path too.

The Guides include small examples written in **Lean 4**, a formal proof environment.

The first five correspond directly to the Guides:

```text
01_awareness.lean
02_alignment.lean
03_coherence.lean
04_clarity.lean
05_continuity.lean
```

Additional examples explore selected structures from the history of the project:

```text
06_tft_origin.lean
07_nawderian_theorem.lean
08_nawderian_set_theorem.lean
09_temperature_engine_theorem.lean
10_unified_resonance_theorem.lean
11_rtt_origin.lean
```

The complete set was compiler-checked using:

```text
Lean 4.36.0
x86_64-w64-windows-gnu
```

But there's an important lesson hiding here.

Lean can tell us whether a formal conclusion follows from the definitions and assumptions we gave it.

That does **not** automatically tell us whether those assumptions accurately describe the world.

So always keep two questions separate:

> **Did Lean verify the proof?**

and:

> **Did we formalize what we actually intended to claim?**

# Your Privacy Matters

There is one design principle behind the Guides that we want to state clearly:

> ## **Learning does not grant us permission to observe the learner.**

You should not need to surrender unnecessary personal information simply because you want to learn something.

The learning experience, mastery process, and badges should remain separate from optional feedback.

# Where We're Going

The final destination isn't:

> **Now memorize TriadicFrameworks.**

It isn't:

> **Now agree with the framework.**

And it definitely isn't:

> **Now ask an AI what to think.**

The destination is **independent application**.

When you're finished, choose something we did **not** choose for you:

- something you're learning;
- a technical problem;
- a disagreement;
- a model;
- a research claim;
- a piece of software;
- a system;
- an everyday situation;
- or something entirely your own.

Then ask:

> ## What am I observing?

> ## What corresponds?

> ## Does this hold together?

> ## Can I make it clear enough to inspect?

> ## What persists when it changes?

If those questions travel with you beyond these pages...

**5Guides has done its job.**
