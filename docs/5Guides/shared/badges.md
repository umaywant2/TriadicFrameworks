# Badges

> **A small marker for a skill you prac**ced and demonstrated***

5Guides uses badges to recognize learning milestones.

They're deliberately simple.

A badge means:

> **I completed this Guide and demonstrated the skill it was designed to practice.**

It does not mean:

> **I am now an expert.**

And it definitely doesn't mean:

> **I beat everybody else.** 😉

The badges exist to mark progress, not rank people.

---

# The Five Badges

There is one primary badge for each Guide:

```text
Awareness Badge
      ↓
Alignment Badge
      ↓
Coherence Badge
      ↓
Clarity Badge
      ↓
Continuity Badge
```

Together, they represent a learner's journey through the Five Guides.

---

# Awareness Badge 👀

## Core Question

> **What am I observing?**

The Awareness Badge recognizes that the learner has practiced separating:

- observation;
- interpretation;
- assumption;
- uncertainty;
- missing information;
- perspective;
- and observational boundary.

The learner has demonstrated an ability to pause before jumping directly from:

> **I see this**

to:

> **Therefore this must mean...**

### Badge Meaning

> **I can distinguish what I observe from what I interpret, assume, or do not yet know.**

---

# Alignment Badge 🔗

## Core Question

> **What corresponds?**

The Alignment Badge recognizes that the learner has practiced:

- identifying candidate correspondences;
- examining the evidence supporting a mapping;
- recognizing partial alignment;
- identifying conditional alignment;
- preserving mismatches;
- exposing assumptions;
- and resisting the temptation to force things to match.

### Badge Meaning

> **I can examine how things correspond without forcing them to align.**

---

# Coherence Badge 🧩

## Core Question

> **Does this hold together?**

The Coherence Badge recognizes that the learner has practiced identifying:

- contradictions;
- missing relationships;
- unsupported conclusions;
- incompatible assumptions;
- locally reasonable pieces that conflict globally;
- and structures that require additional information before they can hold together.

### Badge Meaning

> **I can inspect whether the pieces and relationships of a structure work together.**

---

# Clarity Badge 🔎

## Core Question

> **Can this be understood well enough to inspect?**

The Clarity Badge recognizes that the learner has practiced making important structure visible by examining:

- definitions;
- relationships;
- ambiguity;
- missing steps;
- hidden assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- and alternative representations.

The learner has also practiced an important constraint:

> **Improve clarity without silently changing meaning.**

### Badge Meaning

> **I can make important structure easier to understand and inspect while preserving what matters.**

---

# Continuity Badge 🔄

## Core Question

> **What persists through change?**

The Continuity Badge recognizes that the learner has practiced following:

- states;
- transitions;
- persistence;
- drift;
- recurring patterns;
- broken patterns;
- and structural change over time.

The learner has demonstrated the ability to ask not only:

> **Where are we now?**

but:

> **How did we get here?**

### Badge Meaning

> **I can inspect what changes, what persists, and how one state connects to another.**

---

# Five Guides Completion

After completing all five Guide milestones, the learner may receive a combined:

# Five Guides Completion Badge

This represents completion of:

```text
Awareness
    ↓
Alignment
    ↓
Coherence
    ↓
Clarity
    ↓
Continuity
```

It means:

> **I completed the Five Guides and practiced applying all five structural questions.**

It does not mean the learner has finished learning.

Quite the opposite.

The intended next step is:

# Independent Application

Take the questions somewhere new.

---

# What a Badge Represents

A 5Guides badge represents three things:

## 1. Participation

The learner worked through the relevant Guide.

## 2. Application

The learner did more than read the material.

They practiced the skill.

## 3. Demonstration

The learner completed the Guide's mastery challenge sufficiently to demonstrate practical use of the skill.

The badge therefore represents:

> **completed + practiced + demonstrated**

rather than:

> **visited + clicked + collected**

---

# What a Badge Does Not Represent

A 5Guides badge is **not** intended to represent:

- professional certification;
- academic credit;
- accreditation;
- licensing;
- scientific authority;
- mastery of the entire TriadicFrameworks corpus;
- expertise in RTT;
- mathematical expertise;
- Lean expertise;
- or superiority over another learner.

It marks a defined educational milestone.

Nothing more needs to be added to make that worthwhile.

---

# Mastery Before Badge

The intended sequence is:

```text
Explore
   ↓
Practice
   ↓
Awareness Check
   ↓
Apply
   ↓
Mastery Challenge
   ↓
Badge
```

The optional Awareness Check does not award the badge by itself.

Its purpose is:

> **What did I notice and retain?**

The Mastery Challenge asks something different:

> **Can I use the skill?**

The badge follows the second question.

---

# Retry Is Part of Mastery

A learner who does not demonstrate mastery on the first attempt has not failed the Guide.

Instead:

```text
Attempt
   ↓
Notice Gap
   ↓
Revisit
   ↓
Practice
   ↓
Try Again
```

The learner might receive feedback such as:

> **Your observations are clear, but one conclusion still depends on an unstated assumption. Take another look at assumptions, then try a fresh example.**

Or:

> **Several mappings are useful, but one mismatch has been forced into alignment. Preserve the mismatch and reconsider the relationship.**

The system should help identify **what deserves another look**.

It should not turn learning into punishment.

---

# Badges Are Not Scores

Avoid attaching values such as:

```text
Awareness: 84%
Alignment: 91%
Coherence: 76%
```

unless a future learning design has a specific, well-justified reason for such scoring.

The Five Guides are primarily about demonstrated structural habits.

A badge answers:

> **Was the defined skill sufficiently demonstrated?**

It does not need to answer:

> **How much better are you than someone else?**

---

# No Badge Rankings

Avoid:

- bronze/silver/gold learner rankings;
- badge leaderboards;
- rarity scores;
- fastest-completion awards;
- comparison against other learners;
- competitive country rankings;
- or public completion hierarchies.

Those features would quietly change the purpose of the badges.

The badges are intended to help a learner recognize:

> **I reached this milestone.**

Not:

> **I outranked someone.**

---

# No Artificial Scarcity

Badges should not become valuable because only a certain number of learners are allowed to receive them.

If a learner demonstrates the skill, the learner earns the milestone.

There is no need for:

> **Only 1% of learners achieve Continuity!**

The achievement comes from the work.

Not scarcity.

---

# Badge State Can Be Simple

A minimal learning state might be:

```text
Awareness:
  completed: true

Alignment:
  completed: false

Coherence:
  completed: false

Clarity:
  completed: false

Continuity:
  completed: false
```

Or even more simply:

```text
Awareness       ✓
Alignment
Coherence
Clarity
Continuity
```

The implementation should remain as small as the learning experience allows.

---

# Badge Provenance

If badges eventually become portable or downloadable artifacts, they should carry enough information to explain what they represent.

A future badge might identify:

```text
Badge:
Awareness

Guide:
5Guides / Awareness

Meaning:
Demonstrated practical use of the Awareness skill

Guide Version:
[version if needed]

Criteria Version:
[version if needed]
```

The exact technical format can wait until implementation.

The important principle is:

> **Someone inspecting the badge should be able to determine what achievement it actually represents.**

---

# Badge Versioning

The Guides may evolve.

Exercises may improve.

Mastery criteria may become clearer.

If those changes become significant enough to alter what a badge represents, the badge system should be able to distinguish versions.

That does not mean every text correction requires a new badge version.

It means:

> **If the meaning of the achievement changes, preserve that history.**

This is Continuity applied to badges.

---

# A Badge Should Not Quietly Change Meaning

Suppose the Awareness Badge originally means:

> **Demonstrated the ability to distinguish observation from interpretation, assumption, and uncertainty.**

Later, the Guide receives additional material.

We should not silently redefine the old badge as:

> **Certified mastery of advanced observational reasoning and RTT.**

That would break the relationship between the badge and the accomplishment it originally represented.

If the meaning changes substantially, make the change visible.

---

# The Badge Is Not the Learner

A learner is more than a set of achievements.

Avoid turning:

```text
Awareness ✓
Alignment ✓
Coherence ✓
```

into a public identity profile unless the learner explicitly chooses some future feature that requires it.

The badge marks an accomplishment.

It does not need to become another mechanism for profiling the learner.

---

# Privacy and Badges

Badge design should preserve the privacy principles of 5Guides.

Receiving a badge should not automatically require:

- a real name;
- an email address;
- a public account;
- a public student profile;
- a school;
- an employer;
- a location;
- or a Student Footprint.

Keep these separate:

```text
Mastery
   ↓
Badge
```

and:

```text
Optional Footprint
```

A learner may earn a badge and leave nothing publicly behind.

---

# Badge and Student Footprint Are Different

The badge says:

> **I completed and demonstrated this skill.**

The Footprint says:

> **I voluntarily chose to leave a small note about my experience.**

That gives us four perfectly valid situations:

| Badge | Footprint | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | Yes | Completed and voluntarily left feedback |
| Yes | No | Completed and left no feedback |
| No | Yes | Should not normally occur if Footprints are offered only after completion |
| No | No | No completed milestone recorded |

The important case is:

> **Badge = Yes, Footprint = No**

That learner has completed exactly the same educational milestone as someone who chose to leave feedback.

---

# AI Does Not Earn the Badge

AI can help the learner:

- explore an idea;
- generate another example;
- challenge an assumption;
- question a mapping;
- provide an alternative explanation;
- or help identify something worth revisiting.

But the learner should still demonstrate the skill.

The intended relationship is:

```text
Learner
   ↕
Optional AI Partner
   ↓
Practice
   ↓
Learner Demonstration
   ↓
Badge
```

Not:

```text
AI answers challenge
       ↓
Badge awarded
```

The goal is not to test whether a learner knows how to copy an AI response.

---

# Lean Does Not Earn the Badge Either

The optional Lean examples are formal companions.

A successful Lean compilation means:

> **Lean accepted the formal definitions and proofs in that file under the selected environment.**

It does not automatically mean:

> **The learner demonstrated the corresponding Five Guides skill.**

Likewise, a student does not need to complete the Lean path to earn the normal Guide badge.

The formal path is an optional way to go deeper.

---

# Badge Awarding Should Be Explainable

If a system awards a badge, the learner should be able to understand why.

Prefer:

> **You identified the observations, separated your interpretations, preserved uncertainty, and explained what information you would seek next.**

over:

> **Badge awarded: score threshold reached.**

The achievement should remain connected to the skill.

---

# Mastery Criteria Should Be Visible

A learner should not have to guess what "mastery" means.

Each Guide should explain its practical criteria before or during the Mastery Challenge.

For example:

## Awareness

Can the learner:

- distinguish observation from interpretation?
- identify assumptions?
- preserve uncertainty?
- identify observational boundaries?
- identify useful missing information?

## Alignment

Can the learner:

- propose meaningful correspondences?
- justify them?
- preserve important mismatches?
- identify assumptions?
- qualify uncertainty?

## Coherence

Can the learner:

- inspect relationships as a whole?
- identify contradictions or gaps?
- distinguish missing information from contradiction?
- locate where the structure stops holding together?

## Clarity

Can the learner:

- expose important structure?
- reduce ambiguity?
- make relationships inspectable?
- preserve the meaning of the original?

## Continuity

Can the learner:

- identify what changed?
- identify what persisted?
- trace transitions?
- recognize drift or breaks?
- connect present state to prior state?

These are practical skills, not vocabulary tests.

---

# The Five Guides Completion Challenge

The final milestone should eventually ask the learner to apply all five questions to **one new problem**.

For example:

```text
New Problem
    ↓
Awareness
    ↓
Alignment
    ↓
Coherence
    ↓
Clarity
    ↓
Continuity
```

The problem should not be one the learner has already practiced step by step.

The purpose is transfer.

Can the learner carry the toolkit into unfamiliar territory?

---

# Independent Application Matters More Than the Final Badge

The Five Guides Completion Badge is a nice milestone.

But the real destination comes afterward.

Give the learner a simple invitation:

> **Choose something we didn't choose for you.**

Then apply:

> **What am I observing?**

> **What corresponds?**

> **Does this hold together?**

> **Can I make it clear enough to inspect?**

> **What persists when it changes?**

If the learner can do that...

the badge has already served its purpose.

---

# Badge Design Language

Keep badge language modest.

Prefer:

> **Completed Awareness**

> **Demonstrated Awareness**

> **Awareness Guide Completed**

Avoid:

> **Certified Awareness Expert**

> **Master Structural Analyst**

> **RTT Certified Practitioner**

> **Professional Coherence Specialist**

The first set describes what happened.

The second set claims authority that the learning experience does not establish.

5Guides should describe achievements accurately.

---

# Badge Visuals

Badge artwork can eventually be designed around each Guide without changing the meaning of the achievement.

Possible visual themes:

```text
Awareness   → eye / observation / opening
Alignment   → links / correspondence / connection
Coherence   → connected structure / fitting pieces
Clarity     → lens / focus / visible structure
Continuity  → path / transition / persistent thread
```

The visual design should support recognition.

It should not imply institutional authority the project does not possess.

---

# Accessibility

A badge should never rely only on color or imagery to communicate its meaning.

Where badges appear, also provide readable text such as:

> **Awareness — Completed**

or:

> **Alignment — Demonstrated**

The badge should remain understandable:

- without color;
- without hovering;
- without recognizing an icon;
- and without knowing the visual theme in advance.

---

# A Badge Should Be Inspectable

A badge should answer:

> **What did this person have to demonstrate?**

If that question cannot be answered, the badge has become decoration.

An inspectable badge connects:

```text
Badge
  ↓
Guide
  ↓
Mastery Criteria
  ↓
Demonstrated Skill
```

That's the relationship we want to preserve.

---

# Use AACCC on the Badge System

The Five Guides should inspect their own recognition system too.

## Awareness

> **What does the badge actually record?**

## Alignment

> **Does the badge correspond to the skill the learner demonstrated?**

## Coherence

> **Do the mastery criteria and badge meaning hold together?**

## Clarity

> **Can a learner understand what the badge means and does not mean?**

## Continuity

> **If the Guide changes, does the badge still represent the same accomplishment?**

If one of those answers becomes uncomfortable, inspect the badge design again.

---

# The Badge Promise

A 5Guides badge should make a small, honest promise:

> **This learner completed a defined learning journey and demonstrated the corresponding skill under the criteria used by that Guide.**

No inflated authority.

No hidden ranking.

No unnecessary identity.

No required public profile.

No artificial scarcity.

No competition.

Just:

> **You learned something.**

> **You practiced it.**

> **You demonstrated it.**

> **Here's a small marker of that work.**

That's enough. 🙂
