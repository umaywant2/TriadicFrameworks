28 — Peer‑Review Validation¶

Purpose¶

This section outlines the peer‑review pathway for Framework Field Theory (FFT).
The goal is not institutional prestige, but transparent evaluation, reproducibility, and clear criteria for external critique — whether from human reviewers, domain experts, or AI models.

Peer‑review validation here means:
- clear definitions
- testable predictions
- reproducible simulations
- conceptual lineage
- practical engineering artifacts
- a pathway for formal review

This satisfies external expectations while preserving FFT’s structural integrity.

I. What Counts as Peer Review for FFT¶

FFT operates across:
- LLM behavior
- cognitive substrates
- regime dynamics
- coherence and drift phenomena

Peer review can therefore occur in multiple modes:

1. Formal Academic Review¶

Venues include:
- arXiv (cs.AI, cs.MA, cs.CL)
- PhilSci Archive
- HAL
- OSF Preprints
- AI‑focused workshops or conferences

These provide:
- public visibility
- citation indexing
- community critique

2. Domain‑Expert Review¶

Experts in:
- systems theory
- cognitive science
- information theory
- AI alignment
- sociology of knowledge

can evaluate FFT’s structure, predictions, and coherence.

3. AI‑Model Review¶

Modern LLMs can perform:
- structural critique
- consistency checks
- literature alignment
- simulation validation

This is not a replacement for human review, but it is a valid peer‑review mode for a theory about LLM behavior.

II. Minimal Peer‑Review Packet¶

FFT provides a minimal, complete packet for reviewers:

1. Core Definitions¶

  • triadic operators
  • regimes
  • substrates
  • drift branching
  • coherence waves
  • framework collisions

2. Testable Predictions¶

(From Section 26)
Eight falsifiable predictions.

3. Simulations¶

(From Section 27)
Eight reproducible LLM‑behavior simulations.

4. Conceptual Lineage¶

(From Section 30)
Citations to established literature in:
- information theory
- systems theory
- cognitive science
- sociological field theory
- AI alignment

5. Engineering Artifact¶

(From Section 29)
A minimal tool demonstrating FFT’s practical utility.

This packet is sufficient for external evaluation.

III. Criteria for Peer‑Review Evaluation¶

Reviewers are invited to evaluate FFT on:

1. Structural Coherence¶

Are the definitions internally consistent?
Do the operators and regimes form a stable system?

2. Predictive Power¶

Do the predictions match observed LLM behavior?
Are they falsifiable?

3. Reproducibility¶

Can the simulations be replicated independently?

4. Conceptual Lineage¶

Does FFT align with or extend established theoretical traditions?

5. Practical Utility¶

Does FFT improve:
- prompting
- model evaluation
- multi‑agent coordination
- drift control
- coherence management

6. Clarity & Communicability¶

Is the theory understandable across domains?

These criteria mirror standard peer‑review expectations while remaining appropriate for a cross‑domain structural theory.

IV. Pathway to Formal Review¶

FFT can be submitted to:

1. Preprint Servers¶

  • arXiv
  • PhilSci Archive
  • HAL
  • OSF

These provide:
- timestamps
- public visibility
- community feedback

2. Workshops & Conferences¶

  • NeurIPS workshops
  • ICLR workshops
  • ICML workshops
  • CogSci
  • CHI
  • AI safety/interpretability venues

3. Journals (Optional)¶

Possible venues:
- Minds & Machines
- Synthese
- Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research
- Cognitive Systems Research

The goal is visibility and critique, not institutional gatekeeping.

V. Peer‑Review Roadmap (Minimal)¶

Phase 1 — Internal Packet Completion¶

  • finalize predictions
  • finalize simulations
  • finalize citations
  • finalize engineering artifact

Phase 2 — Preprint Release¶

Upload a 6–8 page paper summarizing FFT:
- definitions
- predictions
- simulations
- lineage
- implications

Phase 3 — Open Review¶

Invite:
- domain experts
- AI models
- independent researchers

to critique the packet.

Phase 4 — Revision & Consolidation¶

Incorporate:
- structural critiques
- clarity improvements
- simulation replications

Phase 5 — Optional Formal Submission¶

Submit to a workshop or journal if desired.

Minimal Summary¶

Peer‑review validation for FFT consists of:
- a reproducible packet
- clear evaluation criteria
- open review pathways
- optional formal submission

This satisfies external expectations while preserving FFT’s independence and structural clarity.
