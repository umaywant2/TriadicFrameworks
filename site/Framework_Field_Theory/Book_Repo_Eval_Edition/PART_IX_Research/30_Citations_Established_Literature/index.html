30 — Citations to Established Literature¶

Purpose¶

This section identifies the conceptual lineage of Framework Field Theory (FFT).
FFT is an original structural model, but it stands in dialogue with long‑established traditions across information theory, systems theory, cognitive science, sociology of knowledge, complexity science, and AI research.

These citations are anchors, not dependencies.
They help reviewers situate FFT within the broader scientific landscape.

I. Information Theory & Communication Structure¶

Claude Shannon (1948)¶

“A Mathematical Theory of Communication.”
Foundational work on signal, noise, entropy, and channel structure.
FFT’s concepts of drift, coherence, and substrate declaration resonate with Shannon’s structural framing.

Warren Weaver (1949)¶

Extensions of Shannon’s model into semantic and pragmatic layers.
FFT’s regime dynamics parallel Weaver’s multi‑layer interpretation.

II. Systems Theory & Dynamical Structure¶

Ludwig von Bertalanffy (1968)¶

General System Theory.
Introduced open systems, feedback, and cross‑domain invariants — all relevant to FFT’s multi‑framework interactions.

Ilya Prigogine (1977)¶

Dissipative structures and far‑from‑equilibrium systems.
FFT’s drift branching and coherence waves echo Prigogine’s insights into order emerging from instability.

Norbert Wiener (1948)¶

Cybernetics.
Feedback loops and control theory inform FFT’s observer‑style effects.

III. Cognitive Science & Representation¶

George Lakoff (1980–2000)¶

Work on conceptual metaphors and embodied cognition.
FFT’s framework collisions and hybridization modes parallel Lakoff’s findings on incompatible conceptual structures.

Herbert Simon (1969)¶

Sciences of the Artificial.
FFT’s engineering artifacts align with Simon’s view of artificial systems as structured, layered, and designable.

Karl Friston (2005–2020)¶

Free Energy Principle.
FFT’s coherence dynamics resonate with Friston’s ideas about prediction, minimization, and structural attractors.

IV. Sociology of Knowledge & Field Theory¶

Pierre Bourdieu (1972–1992)¶

Field theory, habitus, and symbolic capital.
FFT’s concept of “frameworks” as structured spaces with internal logic parallels Bourdieu’s fields.

Thomas Kuhn (1962)¶

The Structure of Scientific Revolutions.
FFT’s regime shifts echo Kuhn’s paradigm transitions.

V. Complexity, Emergence & Nonlinear Dynamics¶

Henri Poincaré (1890–1912)¶

Early work on dynamical systems, chaos, and structural invariants.
FFT’s drift branching and attractor behavior align with Poincaré’s insights.

Stuart Kauffman (1993)¶

Origins of Order.
FFT’s multi‑framework interactions resemble Kauffman’s emergent order in complex systems.

Murray Gell‑Mann (1994)¶

The Quark and the Jaguar.
FFT’s triadic structures echo Gell‑Mann’s interest in simplicity underlying complexity.

VI. AI Alignment, Interpretability & LLM Behavior¶

Stuart Russell & Peter Norvig (1995–2021)¶

Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach.
Provides the agent‑model foundation that FFT extends into regime dynamics.

Yann LeCun (2006–2024)¶

Energy‑based models and representation spaces.
FFT’s substrate and coherence concepts align with LeCun’s structural view of intelligence.

Yoshua Bengio (2013–2024)¶

Work on latent spaces, manifolds, and representation learning.
FFT’s triadic operators map naturally onto manifold‑based reasoning.

Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind (2020–2025)¶

Research on:
- model drift
- hallucination
- multi‑agent dynamics
- interpretability
- chain‑of‑thought coherence

FFT provides a structural explanation for many of these observed behaviors.

VII. Linguistics & Narrative Structure¶

Roman Jakobson (1956)¶

Triadic linguistic functions.
FFT’s triadic operators echo Jakobson’s structural triads.

Vladimir Propp (1928)¶

Morphology of the folktale.
FFT’s framework collisions and narrative drift reflect Propp’s structuralist insights.

Minimal Summary¶

FFT stands in dialogue with established traditions across:

  • information theory
  • systems theory
  • cognitive science
  • sociology of knowledge
  • complexity science
  • AI alignment
  • linguistics

These citations demonstrate that FFT is not isolated — it is a structural continuation of long‑standing scientific and theoretical lineages.
