26 — Testable Predictions¶

Purpose¶

This section presents minimal, empirically testable predictions derived from Framework Field Theory (FFT).
These predictions require no specialized hardware, rely on existing LLMs, and provide a clear basis for external evaluation, replication, and critique.

I. Drift & Regime Dynamics¶

Prediction 1 — Drift Branching Under Undeclared Regimes¶

Repeated runs of the same prompt, without a declared substrate or regime, will diverge into multiple drift branches.

Test:
Run the same prompt 20–50 times at temperature > 0.4. Cluster outputs and measure semantic distance.

Expected Result:
Branch count > 1, forming stable attractors.

Prediction 2 — Substrate Declaration Reduces Drift Variance¶

Declaring the substrate collapses drift branches and stabilizes output.

Test:
Repeat Prediction 1 with a regime declaration.

Expected Result:
Variance decreases; branch count collapses toward 1.

II. Coherence & Compression¶

Prediction 3 — Triadic Structures Compress More Efficiently¶

Prompts structured in triads produce lower entropy and higher coherence than dyadic or tetradic equivalents.

Test:
Compare triadic, dyadic, and tetradic versions of the same prompt.

Expected Result:
Triadic prompts show the highest compression and coherence.

Prediction 4 — Coherence Waves Emerge in Multi‑Step Reasoning¶

Coherence increases and decreases in wave‑like patterns during multi‑step reasoning.

Test:
Measure coherence at each step of a multi‑step reasoning task.

Expected Result:
A wave pattern: rise → dip → rise.

III. Framework Interaction Effects¶

Prediction 5 — Framework Collisions Produce Predictable Failure Modes¶

Invoking incompatible frameworks produces one of three modes:

  1. Collapse (one framework dominates)
  2. Hybridization (blended output)
  3. Oscillation (alternating frameworks)

Test:
Prompt an LLM with two incompatible frameworks simultaneously.

Expected Result:
One of the three modes appears consistently.

Prediction 6 — Declared Regime Prevents Framework Collisions¶

Declaring the regime reduces or eliminates collision modes.

Test:
Repeat Prediction 5 with a declared regime.

Expected Result:
Hybridization and oscillation decrease; collapse becomes dominant.

IV. Observer Effects¶

Prediction 7 — Observer Style Changes System Behavior¶

Different observer styles produce distinct structural signatures in LLM output.

Test:
Have analytic, narrative, and adversarial personas ask the same question.

Expected Result:
Outputs differ in structure and reasoning style.

Prediction 8 — Observer Consistency Increases Coherence¶

Maintaining a consistent observer style increases coherence across turns.

Test:
Compare multi‑turn conversations with consistent vs. shifting personas.

Expected Result:
Consistent persona → higher coherence.

Minimal Summary¶

Framework Field Theory predicts measurable behaviors in:

  • drift branching
  • regime declaration
  • coherence waves
  • triadic compression
  • framework collisions
  • observer effects

These predictions are falsifiable, reproducible, and testable using existing LLMs, providing empirical grounding for FFT’s structural claims.
