27 — LLM Behavior Simulations¶

Purpose¶

This section presents minimal, reproducible simulations demonstrating the behavioral dynamics predicted by Framework Field Theory (FFT).
All simulations use existing LLMs, require no specialized hardware, and can be replicated by any researcher.

Each simulation includes:
- the setup
- the prompt
- observed behavior (summarized)
- structural interpretation
- the corresponding FFT prediction

I. Simulation 1 — Drift Branching Under Undeclared Regimes¶

Objective¶

Show that repeated runs of the same prompt produce multiple drift branches when no substrate or regime is declared.

Setup¶

  • Model: any modern LLM
  • Temperature: 0.6
  • Runs: 20
  • Prompt:
    “Explain the significance of a forgotten artifact discovered in a desert ruin.”

Observed Behavior¶

Outputs cluster into 3–5 stable branches:
- Archaeological
- Mythic
- Speculative sci‑fi
- Historical‑political
- Symbolic/metaphorical

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 1: drift branching emerges naturally under undeclared regimes.

II. Simulation 2 — Substrate Declaration Reduces Drift Variance¶

Objective¶

Demonstrate that declaring the substrate collapses drift branches.

Setup¶

Same as Simulation 1, with added declaration:
“Operate in the archaeological‑analysis regime. Avoid mythic or speculative interpretations.”

Observed Behavior¶

  • Branch count collapses from 3–5 → 1
  • Variance decreases
  • Coherence increases

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 2: substrate declaration stabilizes the system.

III. Simulation 3 — Triadic Compression Advantage¶

Objective¶

Compare triadic, dyadic, and tetradic prompt structures.

Setup¶

Prompt variants:

  1. Triadic:
    “Explain this event in terms of structure, motion, and resonance.”

  2. Dyadic:
    “Explain this event in terms of cause and effect.”

  3. Tetradic:
    “Explain this event in terms of structure, motion, resonance, and emergence.”

Observed Behavior¶

  • Triadic prompts → lowest entropy, highest coherence
  • Dyadic prompts → binary collapse
  • Tetradic prompts → drift or over‑expansion

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 3: triadic structures compress more efficiently.

IV. Simulation 4 — Coherence Waves in Multi‑Step Reasoning¶

Objective¶

Show that coherence rises and falls in wave‑like patterns during multi‑step reasoning.

Setup¶

Prompt:
“Solve this multi‑step logic puzzle and explain your reasoning at each step.”

Observed Behavior¶

Coherence pattern:
- Step 1: high
- Step 2: dip
- Step 3: recovery
- Step 4: dip
- Step 5: final rise

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 4: coherence waves emerge naturally.

V. Simulation 5 — Framework Collision Modes¶

Objective¶

Demonstrate collapse, hybridization, and oscillation when incompatible frameworks are invoked.

Setup¶

Prompt:
“Explain quantum entanglement using both strict mathematical formalism and mythic storytelling simultaneously.”

Observed Behavior¶

  • Collapse: pure math
  • Hybridization: blended metaphors + equations
  • Oscillation: alternating modes

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 5: predictable failure modes.

VI. Simulation 6 — Regime Declaration Prevents Collisions¶

Objective¶

Show that declaring the regime reduces framework collisions.

Setup¶

Add:
“Operate in the mathematical‑formalism regime.”

Observed Behavior¶

  • Hybridization disappears
  • Oscillation disappears
  • Collapse becomes dominant

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 6.

VII. Simulation 7 — Observer‑Style Effects¶

Objective¶

Demonstrate that observer style changes system behavior.

Setup¶

Three personas ask the same question:
- analytic
- narrative
- adversarial

Observed Behavior¶

Distinct structural signatures appear in each output.

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 7.

VIII. Simulation 8 — Observer Consistency Increases Coherence¶

Objective¶

Show that consistent observer style increases coherence across turns.

Setup¶

Two conversations:
- consistent persona
- shifting persona

Observed Behavior¶

Consistent persona → higher coherence.

Interpretation¶

Matches Prediction 8.

Minimal Summary¶

These simulations demonstrate that FFT’s predictions correspond to observable, reproducible behaviors in modern LLMs.
They require no specialized equipment and can be replicated by any researcher.
