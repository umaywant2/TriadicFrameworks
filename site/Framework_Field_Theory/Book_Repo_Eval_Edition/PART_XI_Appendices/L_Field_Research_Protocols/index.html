Appendix L — Field Research Protocols¶

Standardized procedures for conducting, documenting, validating, and extending research in Framework Field Theory¶

Framework Field Theory is a field science.
It requires research methods that are:

  • reproducible
  • operator‑aware
  • dimensionally explicit
  • coherence‑aligned
  • ecosystem‑sensitive
  • evolution‑trackable

This appendix defines the official research protocols for FFT.
These protocols ensure that all research — whether structural analysis, dimensional mapping, coherence evaluation, hybridization study, or field‑level modeling — is conducted with rigor and clarity.

1. The Five Pillars of FFT Research¶

All research in FFT must adhere to five foundational principles:

  1. Operator Transparency — all operator usage must be explicit
  2. Dimensional Explicitness — all dimensional layers must be identified
  3. Regime Awareness — stability states must be tracked
  4. Coherence Accountability — paradox and coherence must be measured
  5. Ecosystem Contextualization — frameworks must be situated in their field

These pillars ensure that research is structurally sound.

2. Protocol A — Framework Identification Protocol (FIP)¶

How to formally identify a framework before analysis¶

Steps:

  1. Name the framework
  2. Define its purpose
  3. Identify its domain
  4. Extract its boundary conditions (B‑Ops)
  5. Identify its lineage (L‑Ops)
  6. Map its relational structure (R‑Ops)

Output:
A clear structural identity for the framework.

3. Protocol B — Signature Extraction Protocol (SEP)¶

How to extract a framework’s Field Signature¶

Steps:

  1. Identify operator pattern
  2. Map dimensional envelope
  3. Identify regime structure
  4. Evaluate coherence profile

Output:
A complete Field Signature (Appendix F).

4. Protocol C — Dimensional Analysis Protocol (DAP)¶

How to determine a framework’s dimensional behavior¶

Steps:

  1. Identify active dimensions
  2. Identify dormant dimensions
  3. Identify overloaded dimensions
  4. Identify collapse points
  5. Identify drift tendencies

Output:
A dimensional envelope map.

5. Protocol D — Regime Mapping Protocol (RMP)¶

How to map stability, transition, paradox, interference, and coherence regimes¶

Steps:

  1. Identify current regime
  2. Identify regime boundaries
  3. Identify regime transitions
  4. Identify regime triggers
  5. Identify regime failure modes

Output:
A regime atlas (Appendix D).

6. Protocol E — Coherence Evaluation Protocol (CEP)¶

How to measure coherence and paradox capacity¶

Steps:

  1. Measure paradox load
  2. Identify coherence engine components
  3. Test paradox routing
  4. Test collapse‑recovery behavior
  5. Identify coherence thresholds

Output:
A coherence profile.

7. Protocol F — Evolution Pathway Protocol (EPP)¶

How to determine a framework’s evolution trajectory¶

Steps:

  1. Identify operator emergence
  2. Identify dimensional drift
  3. Identify regime reorganization
  4. Identify hybridization potential
  5. Identify field‑generative potential

Output:
An evolution pathway map (Appendix G).

8. Protocol G — Hybridization Protocol (HYP)¶

How to evaluate and construct hybrid frameworks¶

Steps:

  1. Extract signatures of parent frameworks
  2. Test operator compatibility
  3. Test dimensional alignment
  4. Test regime integration
  5. Test coherence matching
  6. Construct hybrid signature
  7. Evaluate hybrid stability

Output:
A hybrid signature and stability report.

9. Protocol H — Ecosystem Analysis Protocol (EAP)¶

How to analyze multi‑framework ecosystems¶

Steps:

  1. Map dimensional clusters
  2. Identify coherence anchors
  3. Identify paradox fields
  4. Identify hybrid zones
  5. Identify collapse cascades
  6. Identify field evolution patterns

Output:
An ecosystem signature (Appendix F).

10. Protocol I — Generative Engine Protocol (GEP)¶

How to analyze or construct generative engines¶

Steps:

  1. Identify operator recombination patterns
  2. Identify dimensional modulation behavior
  3. Identify regime cascade behavior
  4. Identify paradox integration behavior
  5. Identify generative output patterns

Output:
A generative engine blueprint (Appendix J).

11. Protocol J — Compression & Expansion Protocol (CEP‑2)¶

How to compress or expand frameworks across dimensions¶

Steps:

  1. Identify target dimensional layer
  2. Identify compression/expansion risks
  3. Identify coherence requirements
  4. Apply compression or expansion rules
  5. Validate structural integrity

Output:
A compression/expansion map (Appendix K).

12. Protocol K — Field‑Level Research Protocol (FLRP)¶

How to study entire conceptual fields¶

Steps:

  1. Identify field boundaries
  2. Map dimensional distribution
  3. Identify coherence gradients
  4. Identify paradox fields
  5. Identify hybridization patterns
  6. Identify field evolution trajectory

Output:
A field‑level evolution model.

13. Research Documentation Standards¶

All FFT research must include:

  • Operator transparency
  • Dimensional explicitness
  • Regime mapping
  • Coherence evaluation
  • Evolution pathway identification
  • Ecosystem context
  • Version‑controlled artifacts (GitHub)
  • Reproducible diagrams (SVG‑ready)

These standards ensure research quality.

14. Appendix L in One Sentence¶

Appendix L defines the Field Research Protocols — the standardized procedures for identifying, analyzing, evaluating, evolving, hybridizing, and contextualizing frameworks and fields in Framework Field Theory.
