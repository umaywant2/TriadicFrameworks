A — Field Glossary¶

The canonical definitions of operators, dimensions, regimes, coherence structures, and field‑level concepts in Framework Field Theory¶

This glossary defines the core terms of Framework Field Theory (FFT).
Each entry is written for precision, clarity, and cross‑reference across the entire book.
Terms are grouped by category for ease of navigation.

1. Operator Families (B–C)¶

The seven universal operator families that generate all framework behavior¶

B‑Ops — Boundary Operators¶

Define identity, separation, and scope.
Examples: inside/outside, domain definition, constraint setting.

L‑Ops — Lineage Operators¶

Define continuity, inheritance, and historical progression.
Examples: sequences, chains, developmental arcs.

R‑Ops — Relational Operators¶

Define connections, comparisons, and structural relations.
Examples: networks, categories, mappings.

T‑Ops — Transition Operators¶

Define change, flow, and transformation.
Examples: processes, cycles, state changes.

E‑Ops — Envelope Operators¶

Define context, conditions, and situational boundaries.
Examples: environments, constraints, contextual layers.

H‑Ops — Rhythm Operators¶

Define cadence, iteration, oscillation, and temporal structure.
Examples: sprints, cycles, beats, periodicity.

C‑Ops — Coherence Operators¶

Define integration, paradox resolution, and cross‑layer stability.
Examples: synthesis, harmonization, meta‑structure.

2. Dimensional Layers (0D–9D)¶

The structural layers frameworks inhabit and evolve through¶

0D — Identity¶

A single idea or point of origin.

1D — Lineage¶

A sequence, chain, or progression.

2D — Relation¶

Categories, comparisons, and structural mapping.

3D — Transition¶

Flows, processes, and dynamic behavior.

4D — Envelope¶

Contextual adaptation and situational behavior.

5D — Rhythm¶

Iteration, cadence, and temporal coherence.

6D — Coherence¶

Paradox integration and cross‑layer stability.

7D — Meta‑Structure¶

Frameworks that modulate other frameworks.

8D — Field Behavior¶

Ecosystem‑level coherence and generativity.

9D — Meta‑Field¶

Cross‑ecosystem modulation and field evolution.

3. Regime Types¶

The stability states frameworks move through¶

Stable Regime¶

Low paradox, high coherence, predictable behavior.

Transitional Regime¶

Moderate paradox, shifting structure, dynamic change.

Paradox Regime¶

High paradox, coherence under pressure, potential for evolution.

Interference Regime¶

Operators or dimensions collide, causing instability.

Coherence Regime¶

Paradox integrated, structure upgraded, evolution underway.

4. Coherence Structures¶

The mechanisms that stabilize frameworks across layers¶

Coherence Engine¶

The internal mechanism that measures, updates, and recovers coherence.

Coherence Envelope¶

The range of paradox a framework can hold without collapse.

Coherence Wave¶

A phase of coherence expansion: local → structural → dimensional → meta.

Coherence Cascade¶

A chain reaction where resolving one paradox stabilizes multiple layers.

Meta‑Coherence¶

Coherence that operates across multiple frameworks or dimensions.

5. Paradox Concepts¶

The tensions that drive evolution¶

Structural Paradox¶

Contradictions inside the framework’s structure.

Behavioral Paradox¶

Contradictions in how the framework behaves.

Meta‑Paradox¶

Contradictions across dimensional layers.

Paradox Load¶

The amount of contradiction a framework is currently holding.

Paradox Routing¶

Sending paradox to the operator family best equipped to resolve it.

6. Evolution Concepts¶

How frameworks grow, drift, and transform¶

Dimensional Drift¶

Movement from one dimensional layer to another.

Upward Drift¶

Expansion into higher dimensions.

Downward Drift¶

Collapse into lower dimensions.

Operator Emergence¶

New operators appearing as the framework evolves.

Regime Reorganization¶

Internal restructuring of stability and transition patterns.

Dimensional Integration¶

Simultaneous activation of multiple dimensional layers.

7. Hybridization Concepts¶

How frameworks combine and co‑evolve¶

Hybrid Framework¶

A new framework formed from two or more parent frameworks.

Operator Compatibility¶

Whether operator families align across frameworks.

Dimensional Alignment¶

Whether dimensional envelopes overlap.

Regime Integration¶

Whether regimes can interlock without destabilization.

Hybrid Stability¶

The coherence and viability of a hybrid framework.

8. Ecosystem Concepts¶

How frameworks behave in multi‑framework environments¶

Framework Ecosystem¶

A conceptual environment containing multiple interacting frameworks.

Coherence Anchor¶

A high‑coherence framework that stabilizes others.

Dimensional Cluster¶

A group of frameworks occupying similar dimensional layers.

Paradox Field¶

A region where contradictions accumulate across frameworks.

Ecosystem Collapse¶

A cascade of failures across multiple frameworks.

9. Field‑Generative Concepts¶

How frameworks become engines that generate new frameworks¶

Field‑Generative System¶

A framework capable of producing new frameworks.

Generative Engine¶

The mechanism that recombines operators, dimensions, and regimes.

Generative Modularity¶

Components that can be recombined to create new structures.

Field Evolution¶

The transformation of entire conceptual ecosystems.

10. Translation Concepts¶

How frameworks move across domains¶

Compression¶

Translating a high‑dimensional framework into a lower‑dimensional domain.

Expansion¶

Translating a low‑dimensional framework into a higher‑dimensional domain.

Reframing¶

Translating a framework laterally into a new domain at the same dimension.

Translation Pathway¶

The structural route used to translate a framework.

11. Glossary in One Sentence¶

The Field Glossary defines the operators, dimensions, regimes, coherence structures, paradox types, evolutionary mechanisms, hybridization rules, ecosystem behaviors, generative engines, and translation pathways that form the conceptual backbone of Framework Field Theory.
