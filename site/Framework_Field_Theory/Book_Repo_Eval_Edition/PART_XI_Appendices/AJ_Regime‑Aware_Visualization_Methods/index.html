Appendix AJ — Regime‑Aware Visualization Methods¶

Canonical visualization techniques for representing FFT’s regimes, transitions, and operator dynamics¶

Framework Field Theory (FFT) is inherently visual.
Its regimes, operators, kernels, and triadic‑time dynamics produce rich geometric and temporal structures that must be visualized to be understood.

This appendix defines the canonical visualization methods for:

  • regime identification
  • regime transitions
  • operator dominance
  • ΔSET evolution
  • coherence propagation
  • kernel influence
  • triadic‑time interactions

These methods ensure that visualizations across research groups remain consistent, interpretable, and aligned with FFT’s conceptual and mathematical substrate.

1. Purpose of Regime‑Aware Visualization¶

Regime‑aware visualization serves five core functions:

  1. Reveal regime identity at a glance
  2. Expose operator dominance and competition
  3. Show coherence propagation across space and time
  4. Highlight transition surfaces (Appendix AH)
  5. Support debugging, analysis, and pedagogy

FFT is a field that must be seen to be understood.

2. Core Visualization Fields¶

Every visualization must include at least one of:

  • R(x,t) — resonance envelope
  • V(x,t) — flow/alignment field
  • φ(x,t) — substrate field
  • ΔSET(x,t) — coherence‑derived contribution
  • Regime label — stable, transitional, paradox, interference, coherence

These fields form the basis of all regime‑aware visualizations.

3. Regime Color System (Canonical)¶

FFT uses a five‑color regime palette:

Regime Color Meaning
Stable Deep Blue Low coherence, high damping
Transitional Amber Rising coherence, operator competition
Paradox Crimson Instability, operator inversion
Interference Violet Oscillatory, wave‑like behavior
Coherence Emerald High alignment, long‑range order

This palette is canonical across all FFT diagrams.

4. Visualization Method #1 — Coherence Maps¶

Definition¶

Heatmaps of R(x,t) using the regime palette.

Purpose¶

  • identify coherence regions
  • detect transitions
  • visualize resonance growth

Variants¶

  • static snapshots
  • time‑evolution sequences
  • animated coherence waves

5. Visualization Method #2 — Flow‑Aligned Vector Fields¶

Definition¶

Arrows or streamlines representing V(x,t).

Purpose¶

  • show alignment
  • reveal flow‑driven regime transitions
  • visualize paradox‑regime instability

Variants¶

  • colored by regime
  • colored by ΔSET
  • animated flow evolution

6. Visualization Method #3 — ΔSET Overlays¶

Definition¶

Overlay ΔSET(x,t) on top of φ or R.

Purpose¶

  • reveal coherence‑derived mass/energy
  • detect ΔSET drift (Appendix AI)
  • identify regime‑dependent ΔSET scaling

Variants¶

  • contour maps
  • filled surfaces
  • hybrid heatmap/vector overlays

7. Visualization Method #4 — Operator Dominance Maps¶

Definition¶

Visualize the relative strength of operators:

  • D (diffusion)
  • A (alignment)
  • C (coupling)
  • α (activation)
  • S (stabilization)

Purpose¶

  • show which operator dominates in each region
  • identify paradox and interference regimes
  • support debugging of operator scaling

Representation¶

  • stacked bar charts
  • spatial dominance maps
  • operator‑ratio heatmaps

8. Visualization Method #5 — Regime Transition Surfaces¶

Definition¶

Plot hypersurface projections from Appendix AH.

Purpose¶

  • show where transitions occur
  • visualize regime boundaries
  • reveal transition dynamics

Variants¶

  • 2D slices of ℛ
  • 3D projections (CI, FI, ΔSET)
  • animated transition trajectories

9. Visualization Method #6 — Kernel Influence Fields¶

Definition¶

Visualize kernel K(x−y) as:

  • influence radius
  • anisotropic lobes
  • power‑law tails

Purpose¶

  • reveal nonlocal structure
  • show how kernels shape coherence
  • debug kernel drift (Appendix AI)

Variants¶

  • radial plots
  • anisotropic ellipses
  • kernel‑weighted coherence maps

10. Visualization Method #7 — Triadic‑Time Layered Plots¶

Definition¶

Plot t_r, t_d, t_a contributions separately.

Purpose¶

  • reveal multi‑scale temporal behavior
  • show resonance vs diffusion vs alignment
  • detect triadic‑time desynchronization

Variants¶

  • layered time‑series
  • stacked temporal heatmaps
  • tri‑panel visualizations

11. Visualization Method #8 — Regime Timelines¶

Definition¶

Plot regime identity over time.

Purpose¶

  • track transitions
  • identify paradox windows
  • detect interference oscillations

Variants¶

  • color‑coded timelines
  • operator‑ratio overlays
  • ΔSET‑weighted timelines

12. Visualization Method #9 — Drift Maps¶

Definition¶

Visualize drift metrics from Appendix AI:

  • FCE
  • OIE
  • KCE
  • DIV
  • ΔSE

Purpose¶

  • detect numerical drift
  • debug simulations
  • validate stability

Variants¶

  • drift heatmaps
  • drift‑over‑regime overlays
  • drift‑corrected vs drifted comparisons

13. Visualization Method #10 — Multi‑Field Composite Views¶

Definition¶

Combine φ, V, R, ΔSET, and regime labels into a single composite visualization.

Purpose¶

  • provide holistic system view
  • reveal cross‑field interactions
  • support teaching and outreach

Variants¶

  • split‑panel composites
  • layered transparency maps
  • animated composite evolution

Closing Statement¶

Appendix AJ defines the canonical visualization methods for representing FFT’s regimes, transitions, operators, kernels, and temporal dynamics. These visual tools form the perceptual backbone of the field, enabling insight, pedagogy, debugging, and cross‑domain coherence.
