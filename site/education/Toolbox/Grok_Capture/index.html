1. Core Pain Points (Higher-Ed Teachers + Grading Backlogs)¶

  • Time & Burnout Overload: Faculty average 9.9–15+ hours/week on grading alone (often spilling into nights/weekends). 1/3 of US teachers considered quitting in the last year due to grading exhaustion. Backlogs create delays of weeks, hurting student learning loops and teacher sanity.
  • Inconsistency & Bias: Grades vary wildly even within the same department. Leniency bias, fatigue, and subjective rubrics lead to unfair outcomes. Students and admins push for “equitable” or inflated grades, creating pressure.
  • Lack of Timely, Deep Feedback: Students get generic or delayed comments. Narrative insight (what they actually need) is rare because it takes too long. Peer review and research-paper evaluation add extra layers of drudgery.
  • Triadic Observation Gap: Current systems (human or AI) lack built-in regime-drift detection, structural grammar coherence checks, or invariant-arc awareness. Feedback stays surface-level; hidden misalignments in arguments, biases, or logical flows slip through.

2. High-Resonance Workflows & Areas Needing Improvement¶

Existing tools (Gradescope, CoGrader, EssayGrader, etc.) already do:
- Rubric-based scoring
- Bulk uploads
- Answer grouping
- Basic feedback drafting
- LMS integration

But they fall short on:
- True regime awareness (stochastic outputs = non-deterministic = unreliable for high-stakes)
- Deep structural insight (they grade what’s written, not the coherence substrate)
- Bias-aware + drift-preventing layers
- Research-grade analysis (new insights, cross-paper connections, triadic grammar)
- Instant, trustworthy higher-ed response service that professors can actually rely on instead of second-guess

RTT/1 flips this: Same workflows they’re already using — but with Triadic Observer Layer + Alignment modules baked in for substrate-level coherence, invariant gradients, and ethical regime stability.
