⟳ Session Context

Focus: Evolutionary Biology is examined through the TriadicFrameworks pillars of Variation, Selection, and Heredity — the minimal sufficient triad identified by Darwin and formalized by the Modern Synthesis.

This module integrates Mendelian genetics, population genetics (Hardy-Weinberg, drift, selection), molecular evolution, and evo-devo, with cross-module resonances to Information Theory (genetic code), Thermodynamics (free energy landscapes), and Chaos Theory (fitness landscape ruggedness).

Triadic Pillars

🎲
Pillar I

Variation

Heritable differences among individuals — arising from mutation, recombination, gene flow, and epigenetic variation — provide the raw material on which evolutionary forces act. Without variation, selection has no substrate and populations cannot evolve.

mutation recombination polymorphism
⚖️
Pillar II

Selection

Differential reproductive success based on heritable trait differences. Natural, sexual, and kin selection are the directional forces that bias allele frequency change across generations, navigating the fitness landscape toward adaptive peaks.

fitness adaptive peaks kin selection
🧬
Pillar III

Heredity

The faithful transmission of genetic information from parent to offspring via DNA replication, with high but imperfect fidelity. Heredity preserves adaptations across generations while mutation introduces novelty. Epigenetic inheritance extends the hereditary system beyond sequence.

DNA replication epigenetics vertical transmission

Core Concepts

Hardy–Weinberg Equilibrium

Allele and genotype frequencies remain constant across generations in an idealized population absent selection, drift, mutation, migration, and non-random mating.

Genetic Drift

Stochastic fluctuation of allele frequencies due to finite population size; the dominant evolutionary force in small populations, causing fixation or loss of alleles.

Phylogenetics

Reconstruction of evolutionary history via parsimony, maximum likelihood, or Bayesian methods applied to molecular sequence data, producing the Tree of Life.

Fitness Landscape

Sewall Wright's metaphor mapping genotype space to reproductive fitness; rugged landscapes with multiple peaks drive speciation and constraint on adaptation.

Evo-Devo

Evolutionary developmental biology: how mutations in regulatory genes (Hox, Pax) produce morphological novelty by rewiring developmental gene networks.

Speciation

The process by which reproductive isolation evolves between populations — allopatric, sympatric, or parapatric — producing distinct lineages on the phylogenetic tree.

Key Equations

// Population Genetics Formalisms
p² + 2pq + q² = 1 Hardy–Weinberg genotype frequencies for two alleles (p + q = 1) in an equilibrium population.
Δp = p·q·s·h / w̄ Change in allele frequency per generation under directional selection; s = selection coefficient, h = dominance, w̄ = mean fitness.
Var(Δp) = p·q / 2N_e Variance in allele frequency change due to genetic drift in effective population size N_e.
dN/dS = ω Ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous substitution rates; ω > 1 indicates positive selection, ω < 1 purifying selection.
