Einstein's geometric theory of gravitation — mass-energy curves spacetime, and curved spacetime tells matter how to move. The framework governing black holes, gravitational waves, cosmology, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

⟳ Session Context

Focus: General Relativity is examined through the TriadicFrameworks pillars of Curvature, Geodesics, and Equivalence — the three conceptual pillars that together constitute Einstein's complete reformulation of gravitation as geometry, replacing Newton's instantaneous action-at-a-distance with local field dynamics in a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold.

This module covers the metric tensor, Einstein field equations, Schwarzschild and Kerr solutions, gravitational waves, cosmological models (FLRW), and the Penrose-Hawking singularity theorems, with cross-module resonances to Quantum Mechanics, Thermodynamics, Information Theory, Electromagnetism, and Standard Model.

Pillar I

Curvature

Spacetime is a four-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold with metric gμν. Mass-energy — encoded in the stress-energy tensor Tμν — curves this manifold via the Einstein field equations. The Riemann curvature tensor R^ρ_{σμν} encodes tidal forces; its contractions (Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar) appear directly in the field equations.

Pillar II

Geodesics

Free-falling bodies follow geodesics — the straightest possible paths through curved spacetime. Gravity is not a force but the geometry of these paths. The geodesic equation d²xμ/dτ² + Γμ_{αβ}(dxα/dτ)(dxβ/dτ) = 0 replaces Newton's F = ma for motion under gravity. Light follows null geodesics, which are bent near massive bodies.

Pillar III

Equivalence

Einstein's equivalence principle — that a uniformly accelerating frame is locally indistinguishable from a gravitational field — is the conceptual seed of GR. Its strong form (all laws of physics are locally Lorentz-invariant in freely falling frames) implies gravitational time dilation, gravitational redshift, and the geometric interpretation of gravity.

Core Concepts

Schwarzschild Solution

The exact vacuum solution for a static, spherically symmetric mass: ds² = −(1−r_s/r)c²dt² + (1−r_s/r)⁻¹dr² + r²dΩ². The Schwarzschild radius r_s = 2GM/c² defines the event horizon of a non-rotating black hole.

Kerr Metric

The exact solution for a rotating black hole, parameterized by mass M and spin a = J/Mc. The ergosphere (outside the event horizon) allows energy extraction via the Penrose process.

FLRW Cosmology

The Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric describes a homogeneous, isotropic expanding universe. The Friedmann equations govern the scale factor a(t), giving Big Bang cosmology and accelerating expansion (Λ).

Gravitational Waves

Perturbations of flat spacetime h_μν propagate at c. Produced by accelerating mass-energy asymmetries (binary mergers); detected via interferometric strain ΔL/L ~ 10⁻²¹ by LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA.

Singularity Theorems

Penrose (1965) and Hawking (1970) proved that under physically reasonable conditions, geodesic incompleteness (singularities) is a generic prediction of GR — not a coordinate artifact.

Cosmological Constant Λ

Einstein's Λ term was reintroduced to explain the observed accelerated expansion of the universe. Dark energy, with equation of state w ≈ −1, is observationally consistent with Λ but its origin remains unknown.

Key Equations

// Einstein's Geometric Formalisms
Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴)Tμν Einstein field equations: the Einstein tensor Gμν (geometry) equals the stress-energy tensor Tμν (matter-energy) scaled by 8πG/c⁴. Ten coupled nonlinear PDEs in four dimensions.
Gμν = Rμν − ½Rgμν The Einstein tensor: Ricci tensor Rμν minus half the Ricci scalar R times the metric gμν. Satisfies ∇^μGμν = 0 (contracted Bianchi identity ↔ energy-momentum conservation).
ds² = gμν dxμ dxν The spacetime interval: the invariant "distance" between events. Timelike (ds²<0) for massive particles, null (ds²=0) for light, spacelike (ds²>0) for causally disconnected events.
d²xμ/dτ² + Γμ_{αβ}(dx^α/dτ)(dx^β/dτ) = 0 Geodesic equation: the path of a freely falling particle in curved spacetime. Γμ_{αβ} are Christoffel symbols (metric connection coefficients), not tensors but encoding the curvature effect on motion.
(ȧ/a)² = 8πGρ/3 − kc²/a² + Λc²/3 First Friedmann equation: governs the expansion rate ȧ of the universe from the field equations applied to the FLRW metric. k = spatial curvature, ρ = energy density, Λ = cosmological constant.

Cross-Module Connections

  • Quantum Mechanics — Quantum gravity remains physics' deepest open problem; GR and QM are mutually inconsistent at the Planck scale (l_P ≈ 1.6×10⁻³⁵ m). The black hole information paradox sits at their intersection.
  • Thermodynamics — Black hole thermodynamics (Bekenstein-Hawking): S_BH = kA/4l²_P, T_H = ℏc³/(8πGMk_B). The four laws of black hole mechanics mirror the four laws of thermodynamics exactly.
  • Information Theory — The holographic principle (Bekenstein bound) sets the maximum information storable in a region at S ≤ kA/4l²_P. The AdS/CFT correspondence maps bulk GR to boundary quantum field theory.
  • Electromagnetism — The Reissner-Nordström metric describes charged black holes; the Kerr-Newman metric generalizes to rotating charged black holes. EM and gravity are jointly described by the Einstein-Maxwell equations.
  • Standard Model — The SM describes matter fields that source GR via Tμν; cosmological phase transitions (electroweak, QCD) occurred in the early FLRW universe. Graviton quantization would extend the SM gauge structure.
