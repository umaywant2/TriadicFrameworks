Triadic Pillars

🌀
Pillar I

Field Memory

Morphic fields are proposed non-material regions of influence that organize the form and behavior of self-organizing systems. Unlike known physical fields, they are hypothesized to carry cumulative memory from all past instances of similar systems, regardless of spatial or temporal separation.

morphic field non-locality cumulative memory
🌿
Pillar II

Formative Causation

The hypothesis that form and behavior are causally shaped by morphic fields, not solely by material chemistry. A developing embryo, a crystallizing molecule, or a learning animal is guided by the accumulated pattern of its predecessors via downward causation from field to matter.

chreodes downward causation morphogenesis
🕸
Pillar III

Collective Habit

Natural laws are proposed as habits: regularities that grew more probable through repetition rather than being fixed at the Big Bang. As a species or behavior pattern is repeated across the collective, it becomes progressively easier and more probable for new instances to replicate it.

habit of nature probability amplification resonance transfer

Core Concepts

Morphogenetic Fields

Waddington's pre-existing concept of developmental fields — extended by Sheldrake to include non-material memory components that persist beyond the death of an organism.

Chreodes

Developmental pathways (from Waddington's epigenetic landscape) that canalize organisms toward stable endpoints; Sheldrake proposes these are maintained by morphic fields.

Crystal Habituation

The empirical claim that novel compounds crystallize with increasing ease worldwide after first crystallization — proposed as evidence of morphic resonance in chemistry.

Behavioral Inheritance

Cross-generational transmission of learned behaviors beyond genetic or epigenetic channels — proposed in rats learning maze tasks and cross-cultural skill acquisition.

Extended Mind

Sheldrake's extension of morphic resonance to consciousness: memory as non-locally stored, perceptual fields extending beyond the brain into the environment.

Mainstream Alternatives

Epigenetic inheritance, cultural evolution, and the extended evolutionary synthesis offer empirically grounded mechanisms for many phenomena morphic resonance aims to explain.

Core Postulates

// Sheldrake's Foundational Claims
Morphic Field Hypothesis Each type of organism, molecule, or behavior is associated with a morphic field that organizes its form and activity across space-time.
Resonance Mechanism Morphic resonance occurs when similar patterns in the present "tune into" the cumulative memory of past similar patterns, independent of distance.
Habit Accumulation Repeated behaviors and structures incrementally reinforce their own probability of recurrence across the morphic field, making "laws" emergent regularities.
Non-locality Morphic resonance is proposed to be non-local and atemporal — past instances influence present ones regardless of spatial or temporal separation.

Cross-Module Connections

  • Quantum Mechanics — Non-locality and entanglement offer a physical precedent for action-at-a-distance; Sheldrake draws loose analogies to quantum coherence.
  • Evolutionary Biology — Epigenetic inheritance and cultural evolution provide mainstream scaffolding for some phenomena morphic resonance targets.
  • Chaos Theory — Strange attractors as physical analogues for chreodes; self-organizing attractors provide a mechanistic substrate for field-shaped morphogenesis.
  • Information Theory — Field memory as a non-local information storage mechanism raises questions about channel capacity and encoding in non-material substrates.
  • General Relativity — The concept of fields shaping matter echoes the geometric field theory of spacetime curvature, though morphic fields lack a physical metric.
