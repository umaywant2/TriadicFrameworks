Quantum Field Theory

The synthesis of quantum mechanics and special relativity — describing all fundamental particles as excitations of underlying quantum fields, governed by local gauge symmetry and the principle of least action.

⟳ Session Context

Focus: Quantum Field Theory is examined through the TriadicFrameworks pillars of Quantization, Symmetry, and Interaction — three irreducible structural elements that together generate all known particle physics from the Lagrangian density formalism.

This module covers canonical and path-integral quantization, gauge field theories, renormalization group flows, and the emergence of particles as field quanta, with cross-module resonances to Quantum Mechanics, Standard Model, Electromagnetism, and General Relativity.

Triadic Pillars

⚛
Pillar I

Quantization

The promotion of classical fields to quantum operators satisfying canonical commutation (bosons) or anticommutation (fermions) relations. Particles emerge as discrete excitations — quanta — of the underlying fields. Path integral quantization alternatively sums over all field histories weighted by e^{iS/ℏ}.

Pillar II

Symmetry

Local gauge symmetries — U(1), SU(2), SU(3) — are the generative principle of fundamental interactions. By demanding invariance of the Lagrangian under local group transformations, gauge bosons (photon, W/Z, gluons) are uniquely introduced as force carriers via the gauge-covariant derivative.

Pillar III

Interaction

Interactions arise from coupling terms in the Lagrangian between matter and gauge fields. Feynman diagrams provide a perturbative bookkeeping method for computing scattering amplitudes. Renormalization group methods handle ultraviolet divergences and reveal scale dependence of coupling constants.

Core Concepts

Lagrangian Density

The fundamental object ℒ(φ, ∂μφ) whose integral over spacetime gives the action S. All dynamics — equations of motion, conservation laws, interactions — follow from varying S.

Propagators & Poles

The momentum-space propagator Δ(p) = i/(p² − m²) encodes particle propagation; poles at p² = m² correspond to on-shell particles and define the mass spectrum.

Vacuum Structure

The QFT vacuum is not empty — it seethes with virtual particle pairs. Vacuum expectation values (VEVs) drive spontaneous symmetry breaking, giving the Higgs its role.

Renormalization Group

The RG describes how effective couplings evolve with energy scale. Asymptotic freedom in QCD (couplings → 0 at high energy) was discovered via RG flow equations.

CPT Symmetry

Any local Lorentz-invariant QFT is invariant under the combined operations of charge conjugation (C), parity (P), and time reversal (T) — a theorem, not a postulate.

Effective Field Theory

Low-energy physics can be described by integrating out heavy degrees of freedom, yielding an EFT valid below a cutoff Λ — a systematic approximation framework.

Key Equations

// Core QFT Formalisms
S = ∫d⁴x ℒ(φ, ∂μφ) The action functional: all field dynamics extremize S. Euler-Lagrange variation yields equations of motion.
[φ(x), π(y)] = iℏδ³(x−y) Canonical equal-time commutation relations for a scalar boson field φ and its conjugate momentum π.
Z = ∫Dφ e^{iS[φ]/ℏ} Path integral partition function: sum over all field configurations weighted by the phase factor exp(iS/ℏ).
Dμ = ∂μ − igAμ^a T^a Gauge-covariant derivative introducing the gauge field Aμ and generator T^a; ensures local gauge invariance of ℒ.

Cross-Module Connections

  • Standard Model — QFT is the mathematical framework of the Standard Model; SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) gauge theory is a specific QFT with observed particle content.
  • Quantum Mechanics — QFT is the relativistic extension of QM; in the non-relativistic limit QFT reduces to many-body quantum mechanics.
  • Electromagnetism — QED (U(1) gauge QFT) is the quantum completion of Maxwell's theory, with predictions verified to 10⁻¹² precision.
  • General Relativity — Quantizing gravity remains unsolved; the incompatibility of QFT and GR is the central open problem in theoretical physics.
  • Thermodynamics — Finite-temperature QFT describes the QCD phase transition (quark-gluon plasma) and electroweak baryogenesis in the early universe.
