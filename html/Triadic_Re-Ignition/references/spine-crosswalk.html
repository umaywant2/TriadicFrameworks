2. Drift Overlay

The Drift Overlay is essential because Re-Ignition events can feel compelling before their mechanisms are understood.

A common drift path is:

observation
    ↓
recognition
    ↓
interpretation
    ↓
mechanism
    ↓
ontology
    ↓
certainty

Each transition strengthens the claim.

The Drift Overlay makes those transitions observable.

Re-Ignition Drift Risks

Primary drift risks include:

interpretation drift

binary collapse

form lock

timeline inflation

observer omission

resonance-causation collapse

These risks do not make Re-Ignition observations invalid.

They identify where claim strength can exceed available support.

3. Coherence Overlay

The Coherence Overlay examines whether relationships remain stable, compatible, and revisitable across transitions.

Coherence in Re-Ignition

A re-ignited pattern may initially appear as:

partial

fragmented

unstable

difficult to represent

Reintegration attempts to connect it with the observer's current model.

Stabilization asks whether the relationship remains accessible across repeated investigation.

initial recognition
        ↓
repeated access
        ↓
comparison
        ↓
model revision
        ↓
stabilized coherence

Stabilized coherence does not automatically establish truth.

It establishes that the model or relationship can be examined consistently.

4. Substrate Overlay

The Substrate Overlay introduces a more cautious relationship.

Re-Ignition frequently asks:

What carries the pattern?

What supports continuity?

Where does the relationship persist?

What makes renewed access possible?

These are substrate questions.

However, the module does not assume that a specific substrate has already been identified.

Substrate Boundary

A useful distinction is:

functional substrate question

versus:

physical substrate claim

The functional question is:

What must persist or recur
for renewed access to occur?

The stronger physical claim is:

A specific physical carrier
has been identified.

Triadic Re-Ignition can ask the first without claiming the second.

Candidate Substrate Locations

Depending on the domain, candidate support may involve:

memory

documents

environment

practice

physical state

computational state

social transmission

symbolic representation

shared structure

unknown carrier

The candidate set should remain open until evidence distinguishes among them.
