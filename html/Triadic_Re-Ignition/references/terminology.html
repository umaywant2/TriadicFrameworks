Triadic Re-Ignition Terminology

Shared terminology reduces drift by making it clear which layer of a claim is being discussed.

This document defines the working vocabulary used throughout the Triadic Re-Ignition module.

These definitions are module-local operational definitions unless explicitly identified as originating from another canonical TriadicFrameworks source.

They are intended to support:

consistent language

clear claim boundaries

cross-document coherence

student interpretation

experimental design

drift detection

Terminology should help investigation.

It should not turn exploratory language into ontology merely because a term has been named.

Terminology Discipline

Triadic Re-Ignition distinguishes between:

TERM
    ↓
operational meaning

and:

TERM
    ↓
stronger interpretation
    ↓
physical / metaphysical claim

The existence of a term in this glossary does not establish the physical existence of the thing described by its strongest possible interpretation.

For example:

timeline projection

provides a model for investigating representation.

It does not, merely by being named, establish a physical projection mechanism.

Likewise:

formless

marks a representational boundary within this module.

It does not automatically establish a separate formless realm.

Core Terms

Re-Ignition

Re-Ignition describes renewed access to a pattern, relationship, skill, model, memory, or structure that was previously dormant, obscured, fragmented, unavailable, or not consciously represented.

Working sequence:

dormant / inaccessible
          ↓
       trigger
          ↓
      resonance
          ↓
     recognition
          ↓
    reintegration
          ↓
    stabilization

Re-Ignition describes the pattern of renewed access.

It does not by itself determine whether the mechanism is:

memory

reconstruction

relearning

recognition

new context

new discovery

or another process

Dormant Pattern

A Dormant Pattern is a relationship or structure that is not currently available to the observer in the same functional or representational form in which it may later become accessible.

dormant
    ≠
necessarily absent

and:

later accessible
    ≠
proof of perfect preservation

Dormancy is therefore an access description, not automatically a storage mechanism.

Trigger

A Trigger is an event, condition, observation, cue, or change that occurs immediately before renewed resonance or recognition.

trigger
   ↓
resonance
   ↓
recognition

A trigger should be distinguished from the mechanism inferred to explain its effect.

trigger
    ≠
automatically
cause

Resonance Trigger

A Resonance Trigger is a trigger associated specifically with the emergence or strengthening of a perceived structural correspondence.

pattern / event
      ↓
resonance trigger
      ↓
increased correspondence
      ↓
recognition

The term identifies an observational relationship.

It does not, by itself, establish a causal mechanism.

Resonance

Within this module, Resonance refers to a perceived or measurable correspondence, coherence, recurrence, alignment, or structural relationship that makes one pattern salient relative to another.

Pattern A
    │
    │ correspondence
    │
    ▼
Pattern B

Resonance is treated initially as:

reason to investigate

rather than:

proof of mechanism

Therefore:

resonance
    ≠
causation

Recognition

Recognition is the transition during which a pattern or relationship becomes explicitly identifiable to the observer.

partial access
     ↓
recognition
     ↓
identifiable relationship

Recognition may follow resonance.

Recognition does not automatically establish why the recognized relationship exists.

recognition
    ≠
verification

Recognition Event

A Recognition Event is the identifiable transition during which previously unavailable or weakly represented structure becomes consciously recognizable.

Conceptually:

before
    ↓
relationship unclear

transition
    ↓
recognition event

after
    ↓
relationship identifiable

The event itself should be captured separately from explanations formed afterward.

Recognition Threshold

A Recognition Threshold is the conceptual boundary between partial or weak correspondence and explicit recognition.

partial resonance
       ↓
---------------------
recognition threshold
---------------------
       ↓
explicit recognition

The term does not require the threshold to correspond to a specific physical quantity.

It is an observational concept unless operationalized further.

Reintegration

Reintegration is the process of relating a recognized pattern to the observer's current knowledge, model, skill, or representational structure.

recognized pattern
       +
current model
       ↓
reintegration

Recognition answers:

"What became accessible?"

Reintegration asks:

"Where does it fit now?"

Stabilization

Stabilization describes increasing consistency in the observer's ability to revisit, represent, use, compare, or test a re-ignited relationship.

recognition
    ↓
reintegration
    ↓
repeated access
    ↓
comparison
    ↓
stabilization

Stabilization does not mean:

canonical truth

It means the observed relationship or model has become sufficiently stable for repeated examination.

Observer Terms

Observer

An Observer is the situated participant through whom an observation becomes locally accessible and representable.

Relevant observer variables may include:

position

knowledge

attention

memory

context

access

representation

The Observer should neither be removed automatically from the analysis nor assumed to determine everything.

Local Observer

A Local Observer is an observer operating from a bounded position within a particular physical, temporal, informational, and representational context.

broader structure
       ↓
local access
       ↓
local observer

Locality describes position and access.

local
    ≠
false

Observer State

Observer State refers to the relevant configuration of the observer at the time of an observation.

It may include:

available knowledge

current context

attention

skills

memory access

expectations

representational framework

Observer State becomes particularly important when the same pattern is represented differently at different times.

Observer Transition

An Observer Transition occurs when the observer's available relationships, models, access, or representational capabilities change.

Observer State A
        ↓
transition
        ↓
Observer State B

A Re-Ignition event may coincide with an observer transition.

This does not establish that observer change is the only mechanism involved.

Hidden Observer

A Hidden Observer is an analytical placeholder for an observer position, observational dependency, or observer-related variable that has not yet been explicitly included in the model.

The term does not imply:

a hidden person

a supernatural observer

or a separate conscious entity

Its purpose is structural:

apparent contradiction
        +
missing observer variable?

Triadic Observer

The Triadic Observer is the third analytical position introduced when two observations, models, states, or claims appear contradictory.

Instead of:

A OR B

the model becomes:

A
+
B
+
Observer

The third position asks:

What relationship, context, regime, or observer condition could explain the apparent contradiction?

It does not require both A and B to remain valid.

It creates a position from which their relationship can be investigated.

Time Terms

Physical Time

Physical Time is the measurable temporal reference layer associated with sequence, duration, physical change, and recorded temporal relationships.

t₁ → t₂ → t₃ → t₄

Within Re-Ignition, Physical Time acts as an anchor when stronger temporal interpretations are being considered.

Local Time

Local Time describes temporal accessibility from the observer's present position.

The observer typically relates differently to:

past
    → memory / records / traces

present
    → observation / measurement / action

future
    → expectation / prediction / possibility

Local Time emphasizes the observer's temporal position rather than claiming a complete ontology of time.

Local Timeline

A Local Timeline is the observer-accessible chronological representation through which past events, present conditions, and anticipated future states are organized.

PAST
  ↓
PRESENT OBSERVER
  ↓
FUTURE

The central distinction is:

locally accessible
        ≠
necessarily exhaustive

Resonance Time

Within Re-Ignition, Resonance Time refers to analysis of structural correspondence across chronological separation.

It distinguishes:

chronology
    ↓
sequence

from:

resonance
    ↓
correspondence

Two events may be chronologically distant while nevertheless becoming structurally significant to an observer.

The term does not, by itself, establish a physical mechanism connecting those events.

Chronological Distance

Chronological Distance is the temporal separation between events within a chronological or physically measured sequence.

A ───────────────────── B

Chronological distance should be distinguished from structural distance.

Structural Distance

Structural Distance is a working description of how accessible, related, or coherent two patterns are within a model or observer representation.

A Re-Ignition event may appear to reduce:

accessible structural distance

without changing:

chronological distance

Structural Distance is therefore an analytical relationship unless further operationalized.

Timeline Projection

Timeline Projection is a model describing the mapping between underlying temporal or structural relationships and the timeline representation available to an observer.

STRUCTURE
    ↓
PROJECTION
    ↓
REPRESENTATION
    ↓
OBSERVER

The term does not by itself establish a physical projection mechanism.

Projection

A Projection is a mapping or transformation through which some aspects of structure become represented while others may be:

preserved

emphasized

compressed

transformed

or unavailable

Projection is used as an analytical model unless a specific physical mechanism is independently established.

Projection Regime

A Projection Regime is a set of conditions under which a particular mapping from structure to representation is produced.

Structure + Conditions A
          ↓
     Projection A

Structure + Conditions B
          ↓
     Projection B

Different projection regimes may produce different representations without automatically requiring different underlying structures.

Timeline Revision

Timeline Revision is a change in the observer's model or interpretation of earlier temporal relationships.

old timeline model
       ↓
new evidence / recognition
       ↓
revised timeline model

Timeline Revision should remain distinct from Timeline Alteration.

Timeline Alteration

Timeline Alteration would be a stronger claim that the underlying physical temporal sequence itself changed.

Therefore:

timeline revision
        ≠
timeline alteration

Evidence of revised representation alone does not establish alteration.

Apparent Timeline Multiplicity

Apparent Timeline Multiplicity occurs when multiple temporal representations, histories, possibilities, or timeline-like structures appear within an observation or model.

The multiplicity might occur at the level of:

possibility

memory

observer representation

projection

physical structure

or ontology

The location of the multiplicity should be identified before stronger conclusions are drawn.

Apparent Multiple Timelines

Apparent Multiple Timelines refers to the investigative category used when an observer encounters or models more than one apparently distinct temporal representation.

The phrase does not mean:

multiple physical timelines
have already been established

Instead:

apparent multiplicity
        ↓
locate the difference
        ↓
compare hypotheses

Divergence Point

A Divergence Point is the location at which two temporal representations first become meaningfully different.

A → B → C → D
        │
        └── X → Y

The investigation asks whether divergence occurs in:

physical events

records

memory

projection

representation

interpretation

Convergence

Convergence occurs when different representations, paths, or models arrive at a shared state or relationship.

A → B ──┐
        ▼
        X

C → D ──┘

Convergence may help identify invariants or common structural constraints.

Form Terms

Form

Form is an accessible representation through which a relationship can be identified, communicated, compared, measured, modeled, or tested.

Examples include:

language

diagram

equation

model

symbol

timeline

category

Form is useful.

It should not automatically be equated with the complete structure being represented.

Formless

Within this module, Formless refers to a relationship that is not yet, or not fully, captured by the observer's current representational form.

current form
      ↓
representation boundary
      ↓
unresolved relationship

Formless does not automatically mean:

hidden realm

separate substance

another dimension

or metaphysical domain

It marks an unresolved representational boundary.

Representation

A Representation is a form used to express some observed or inferred relationship.

Examples:

A → B → C

diagram

description

equation

timeline

model

A representation may preserve real relationships without being complete.

Therefore:

representation
      ≠
automatically
structure itself

Representation Boundary

A Representation Boundary marks the current limit of what an observer's chosen form can express or make accessible.

represented
     │
----- boundary -----
     │
unresolved

Crossing a representation boundary may require:

new information

new form

new observer capability

new measurement

or model revision

Form Transition

A Form Transition is a shift between representations.

Form A
   ↓
new observation / paradox
   ↓
Form B

The transition may reveal relationships hidden or compressed by the previous form.

Form Lock

Form Lock occurs when a representation becomes protected from revision despite observations that challenge its adequacy.

useful form
    ↓
preferred form
    ↓
protected form

Form Lock is a potential source of interpretation drift.

Structural Terms

Structure

Structure refers to the relationships, constraints, ordering, transformations, or organization being investigated independently of any single representation.

Within this module:

structure
    ≠
automatically
physical object

A structure may refer to a relationship expressed within:

data

memory

events

models

systems

or observations

The exact ontological status depends upon the claim being investigated.

Relationship

A Relationship is an identified connection, correspondence, dependency, ordering, transformation, or association between two or more elements.

A relationship may be:

observed

measured

inferred

modeled

hypothesized

Those categories should not be treated as equivalent evidence states.

Structural Correspondence

Structural Correspondence describes a relationship in which patterns share some relevant organization, transformation, ordering, or invariant.

Pattern A
     │
     │ correspondence
     │
Pattern B

Correspondence provides a reason for comparison.

It does not automatically establish shared cause.

Invariant

An Invariant is a property or relationship that remains stable across changes in representation, observer, regime, context, or model being compared.

For example:

Representation A ──┐
                   ├── invariant
Representation B ──┘

Possible invariants might include:

event ordering

measurement

relationship

shared artifact

repeatable transformation

Invariants can help locate which layer actually changed.

Regime

A Regime is a bounded set of conditions under which a system, observer, representation, or relationship behaves according to a relatively stable pattern.

conditions
    ↓
Regime A

Changing relevant conditions may produce:

Regime A
    ↓
transition
    ↓
Regime B

Regime Transition

A Regime Transition occurs when the relevant conditions or relationships change sufficiently that a previous model no longer describes the system in the same way.

Regime A
    ↓
transition
    ↓
Regime B

A regime transition may occur in:

observer state

representation

system behavior

projection

or another modeled layer

The location of the transition should be identified rather than assumed.

Paradox and Drift Terms

Paradox

Within Triadic Re-Ignition, a Paradox is an apparent contradiction or incompatibility preserved as an investigative signal rather than immediately eliminated.

A
≠
B

becomes:

A
+
B
+
Observer

The objective is to determine whether the contradiction reveals:

missing information

hidden assumption

observer position

different regime

representation boundary

model failure

or genuine incompatibility

Structural Paradox

A Structural Paradox is a paradox treated as evidence that the current representation may be missing a relationship, variable, scale, regime, or observer position.

It is not automatically proof that both contradictory claims are true.

Drift

Drift is movement away from the original observation or evidence state through increasingly strong interpretation without sufficient support.

A typical drift path is:

observation
    ↓
interpretation
    ↓
mechanism
    ↓
ontology
    ↓
certainty

Drift does not mean interpretation is forbidden.

It means the transition between claim levels should remain visible.

Interpretation Drift

Interpretation Drift occurs when an explanation gradually becomes treated as though it were the observation itself.

Example:

Observation:
A and B correspond.

        ↓

Interpretation:
A caused B.

        ↓

Drift:
The causal explanation becomes
remembered as directly observed.

A primary Re-Ignition practice is preserving observation separately from interpretation.

Narrative Lock

Narrative Lock occurs when an explanatory story becomes resistant to competing hypotheses or corrective evidence.

candidate explanation
       ↓
preferred explanation
       ↓
protected explanation

Narrative Lock reduces the ability of the model to revise itself.

Binary Collapse

Binary Collapse occurs when a complex or paradoxical observation is prematurely reduced to:

A OR B

despite the possibility that observer position, regime, scale, representation, or another variable has not yet been included.

The triadic alternative is:

A
+
B
+
Observer

followed by investigation.

Research Terms

Capture

Capture is the preservation of an observation as close as practical to its original form before substantial interpretation is added.

event
   ↓
capture
   ↓
later interpretation

Capture attempts to preserve:

what happened

what appeared first

what the observer noticed

what the immediate context was

Observation

An Observation is something detected, experienced, measured, recorded, or otherwise noticed.

The method of observation should be preserved where relevant.

Examples:

direct observation

measurement

memory report

documented record

observer report

Their evidence properties may differ.

Interpretation

An Interpretation is a proposed meaning assigned to an observation.

observation
     ↓
interpretation

Interpretation is necessary for model building.

It should remain distinguishable from the observation that generated it.

Hypothesis

A Hypothesis is a candidate explanation or structural model that can be compared against alternatives.

observation
     ↓
hypothesis set
     ↓
comparison
     ↓
testing

A hypothesis should remain revisable.

Alternative Hypothesis

An Alternative Hypothesis is a competing explanation capable of accounting for some or all of the same observations.

Maintaining alternatives reduces narrative lock.

Observation
   ├── H₁
   ├── H₂
   ├── H₃
   └── H₄

Discriminator

A Discriminator is an observation, measurement, comparison, or result capable of helping distinguish between competing hypotheses.

H₁
  \
   \
    discriminator
   /
  /
H₂

A useful discriminator changes the relative support for competing models.

Failure Condition

A Failure Condition is an observation or result that would create difficulty for, weaken, or require revision of a hypothesis.

"If X occurs,
this model needs revision."

Defining failure conditions helps prevent models from explaining every possible outcome after the fact.

Falsification Boundary

A Falsification Boundary marks the point at which a model specifies what kinds of observations could count against it.

A model that permits:

every possible observation

to count as support has little discriminative value.

The boundary therefore asks:

What would make us revise this?

Minimum-Claim Principle

The Minimum-Claim Principle states:

Use the smallest claim that preserves the observation without unnecessarily promoting interpretation into mechanism or ontology.

For example:

Strong claim:

"I accessed another timeline."

may first be captured operationally as:

Minimum claim:

"I experienced a temporal
representation inconsistent with
my current local timeline model."

The stronger hypothesis may still be retained separately.

Claim Ladder

A Claim Ladder organizes statements according to increasing explanatory strength.

A general Re-Ignition ladder is:

OBSERVATION
     ↓
RELATIONSHIP
     ↓
INTERPRETATION
     ↓
HYPOTHESIS
     ↓
MECHANISM
     ↓
ONTOLOGY

Movement upward increases the burden of evidence.

Educational Terms

Student Leaf Note

A Student Leaf Note is a compact educational artifact designed to introduce one working concept without requiring the student to first absorb the complete module.

Current leaf notes include:

Re-Ignition

Flash

Within student materials, a Flash is the initial rapid moment of salience, familiarity, resonance, or recognition that may precede a developed explanation.

trigger
   ↓
[ FLASH ]
   ↓
recognition
   ↓
interpretation

"Flash" is descriptive language.

It does not imply a specific neurological or physical mechanism.

Claim-Layer Vocabulary

When writing new Re-Ignition documents, it is useful to identify which layer a statement belongs to.

LAYER 1
OBSERVATION

"What happened?"

LAYER 2
RELATIONSHIP

"What appears connected?"

LAYER 3
INTERPRETATION

"What might it mean?"

LAYER 4
HYPOTHESIS

"What model could explain it?"

LAYER 5
MECHANISM

"What process produces it?"

LAYER 6
ONTOLOGY

"What does this imply about
the underlying nature of reality?"

These layers are not forbidden territory.

They are evidence boundaries.

Preferred Wording

When a mechanism is unresolved, prefer:

"may"

"appears"

"candidate"

"working model"

"working proposition"

"hypothesis"

"observer-reported"

"currently unresolved"

"consistent with"

"does not by itself establish"

Avoid silently replacing those with:

"is"

"proves"

"must"

"therefore exists"

"demonstrates reality is"

unless the relevant evidence actually supports the stronger claim.

Terminology Update Rule

New terms should be added when they improve structural precision.

Before adding a term, ask:

Does the term distinguish
a real analytical layer?

Does it reduce ambiguity?

Can it be operationally described?

Does it duplicate an existing term?

Could the term accidentally
promote a hypothesis into ontology?

If the term increases clarity:

add it

If the term merely adds another label:

reuse existing vocabulary

where possible.

Working Summary

Triadic Re-Ignition uses terminology to preserve distinctions.

The most important distinction is:

WHAT WAS OBSERVED
       ↓
WHAT RELATIONSHIP APPEARED
       ↓
HOW IT WAS REPRESENTED
       ↓
WHAT IT MIGHT MEAN
       ↓
WHAT MIGHT EXPLAIN IT
       ↓
WHAT CAN BE TESTED

A term should make those transitions easier to see.

It should not hide them.

The terminology therefore follows one guiding discipline:

Name the structure without pretending the name proves the structure.
Define the boundary without turning the boundary into ontology.
Preserve the observation without letting interpretation silently replace it.

In the shortest form:

DEFINE
  ↓
SEPARATE
  ↓
COMPARE
  ↓
TEST
  ↓
REVISE

Terminology is not the end of the investigation.

It is the shared language that lets the investigation continue without losing track of what each claim actually means.

Main writing choice: this acts as the module’s anti-semantic-drift layer. In particular, Formless, Hidden Observer, Resonance Time, Projection, and Apparent Multiple Timelines all get explicit boundaries so their names don’t accidentally become stronger claims than the working models themselves.
