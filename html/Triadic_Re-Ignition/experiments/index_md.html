Experiments

A framework becomes more useful when its claims can be translated into observations, comparisons, discriminators, and failure conditions.

The Experiments branch of Triadic Re-Ignition turns exploratory concepts into structured investigations.

Its purpose is not to prove the framework.

Its purpose is to ask:

What can be observed?

What can be compared?

What can be repeated?

What can be measured?

What would distinguish
competing explanations?

What would make us
revise the model?

The branch begins with clean observation and progressively adds comparison, cue testing, transfer, stabilization, and student simulation.

It must earn that status through evidence.

1. Observation Protocol

File: ./observation-protocol.md

The Observation Protocol is the experimental root of the branch.

Its core sequence is:

OBSERVE
   ↓
CAPTURE
   ↓
ANCHOR
   ↓
LOCATE THE OBSERVER
   ↓
SEPARATE INTERPRETATION
   ↓
IDENTIFY INVARIANTS
   ↓
GENERATE ALTERNATIVES
   ↓
DEFINE DISCRIMINATORS
   ↓
TEST
   ↓
REVISIT

The permanent boundary is:

CAPTURE
    ≠
PROOF

Why Observation Comes First

If the original observation is not preserved, later testing may accidentally test:

the story about the event

rather than:

the event as originally observed

Therefore the protocol attempts to preserve:

prior state

trigger

first signal

recognition

first representation

initial interpretation

later interpretation

as separate layers where possible.

Observation Categories

Experimental records should identify the relevant access channel.

DIRECT OBSERVATION

MEASUREMENT

MEMORY REPORT

EXTERNAL RECORD

OTHER OBSERVER REPORT

MODEL OUTPUT

INFERENCE

These categories may interact.

They should not be silently treated as equivalent.

Minimum Supported Claim

Every experiment should attempt to end with:

MINIMUM SUPPORTED CLAIM

This should be the smallest statement that:

preserves the result

respects uncertainty

avoids unsupported mechanism

and survives current evidence

This is one of the branch's primary anti-drift tools.

alternative explanations

DIFFERENT OBSERVATIONS

3. Resonance Recall Test

File: ./resonance-recall-test.md

The Resonance Recall Test examines whether a defined cue is associated with renewed access to a previously encountered relationship.

Its working sequence is:

EARLIER EXPOSURE
      ↓
EVIDENCE OF EARLIER ACCESS
      ↓
REDUCED BASELINE ACCESS
      ↓
CUE
      ↓
FIRST SIGNAL
      ↓
RECOGNITION
      ↓
RECALL
      ↓
RECONSTRUCTION
      ↓
FUNCTION
      ↓
TRANSFER
      ↓
DELAYED RETEST

Recall-Test Question

The primary question is:

Does a structurally related cue change access to a previously encountered relationship?

The test separately measures:

familiarity

recognition

free recall

reconstruction

functional use

transfer

delayed stability

These outcomes should not automatically be collapsed.

Recall-Test Boundaries

The permanent distinctions include:

CUE
    ≠
PROVEN CAUSE

FAMILIARITY
    ≠
ACCURACY

RECOGNITION
    ≠
RECALL

RECALL
    ≠
RECONSTRUCTION

CONTINUITY
    ≠
IDENTITY

STABILIZATION
    ≠
TRUTH

These distinctions allow a positive result to remain informative without becoming larger than the evidence.

Cue Conditions

Potential comparisons include:

RELATED CUE

SURFACE-SIMILAR CUE

UNRELATED CUE

NO-CUE REPETITION

The purpose is to ask whether changed access follows:

shared structure

rather than merely:

repetition

attention

surface familiarity

or expectation

Cross-Form Testing

A strong experimental pattern is:

Earlier Exposure
    =
Form A

Cue
    =
Form B

Transfer Test
    =
Form C

Then ask:

What relationship survives
across all three forms?

A surviving relationship becomes a candidate structural invariant.

4. Student Simulation

File: ./student-simulation.md

The Student Simulation applies the experimental discipline to reasoning itself.

Instead of teaching:

the correct Triadic answer

students preserve several analytical passes.

PASS 1
Initial Analysis

        ↓

PASS 2
Observer-Aware Analysis

        ↓

PASS 3
Triadic Analysis

        ↓

COMPARE

The change between passes becomes part of the experiment.

Student-Simulation Question

The primary question is:

Does explicitly introducing observer, context, invariant, paradox, alternative hypotheses, and discriminators change how students analyze an ambiguous problem?

Possible outcomes include:

stronger analysis

same conclusion with
better justification

more useful uncertainty

unnecessary complexity

no meaningful difference

All are valid.

Framework Is Not the Answer Key

The Student Simulation preserves:

framework
    =
reasoning instrument

not:

framework
    =
preferred conclusion

A student may complete the triadic analysis and conclude:

the original simple
explanation still fits best

That is a successful result if the reasoning supports it.

Experimental Claim Ladder

Experimental claims should be separated by strength.

Level 1: Observation

A result occurred.

Level 2: Association

The result occurred alongside
a defined condition.

Level 3: Reproducible Difference

Changing a defined condition
systematically changed the result.

Level 4: Structural Relationship

A stable invariant or dependency
connects the conditions and results.

Level 5: Operational Model

A defined model predicts
the observed relationship.

Level 6: Mechanism

A defined process explains
why the relationship occurs.

Level 7: General Mechanism

The process generalizes across
relevant observers, targets,
and conditions.

Level 8: Physical Principle

The mechanism describes
a general physical process.

Level 9: Ontological Claim

The process reveals the
underlying nature of reality.

Experimental work should not skip levels merely because a result is compelling.

What surrounding conditions

REPRESENTATION VARIABLES

How is the target represented?

TEMPORAL VARIABLES

When does the observation

TRIGGER VARIABLES

What cue or condition changes?

RESPONSE VARIABLES

What observation or performance

The relevant variables depend on the hypothesis.

Operationalization

A concept becomes experimentally useful when it can be translated into something observable.

For example:

"Recognition"

might be operationalized as:

correct identification
of a defined relationship

while:

"Stabilization"

might be operationalized as:

successful delayed
or cross-form access
under specified conditions

The operational definition must be stated explicitly.

Operationalization Is Not Identity

An operational measure does not become the complete meaning of the concept.

For example:

Recognition Measure
    =
correct selection

does not mean:

recognition itself
    =
correct selection
in every context

The measure is a testing instrument.

Its scope should remain visible.

Baseline

Whenever possible, establish a baseline.

BASELINE
    ↓
INTERVENTION / CONDITION
    ↓
POST-CONDITION

The baseline helps determine whether:

anything changed

But baseline measurement itself may affect the observer.

This should be documented.

Controls

Useful comparison conditions may include:

no-intervention

unrelated cue

surface-similar cue

alternative representation

different observer condition

different context

repetition-only condition

Controls should be selected to distinguish the actual hypotheses under investigation.

There is no universal control condition.

Confounds

Potential confounding variables may include:

prior exposure

training

expectation

task repetition

representation

context

available information

target change

timing

measurement method

The experiment should record important confounds rather than pretending perfect control.

First Signal

Some experiments may capture the earliest reported signal before a full interpretation forms.

TRIGGER
   ↓
FIRST SIGNAL
   ↓
RECOGNITION
   ↓
INTERPRETATION

Possible first signals include:

familiarity

salience

unexpected correspondence

contradiction

partial recognition

The first signal is data.

It is not proof of mechanism.

Recognition

Recognition should be distinguished from:

familiarity

confidence

recall

reconstruction

functional use

A participant may score differently across all five.

That pattern may be more informative than a single combined score.

Reintegration

Experimental reintegration may be indicated when the participant can:

explain a relationship

revise a previous model

connect the relationship
to current knowledge

or use it coherently

The exact criterion should be defined by the protocol.

Stabilization

Stabilization may be tested through:

repeated access

delayed retest

cross-form transfer

new-context performance

independent application

cross-observer recognition

Again:

stable
    ≠
true

Stability makes further investigation easier.

Invariants

Invariant detection is one of the branch's central tools.

Condition A ──┐
              │
Condition B ──┼── Invariant R?
              │
Condition C ──┘

The invariant may be:

relationship

ordering

function

transformation

measurement

prediction

error pattern

The invariant should be stated as precisely as practical.

Differences

Experiments must also preserve changed components.

BEFORE
│
├── invariant A
└── variable X

AFTER
│
├── invariant A
└── variable Y

The experiment then asks:

What best explains X → Y?

This is more discriminative than:

"Something changed."

Divergence Point

When two paths differ:

A → B → C → D
        │
        └── X → Y

identify:

the first meaningful divergence

Possible locations include:

access

observation

measurement

memory

representation

interpretation

hypothesis

conclusion

The divergence point often identifies the next variable to test.

Alternative Hypotheses

The branch should preserve at least one meaningful competing explanation where practical.

A general structure is:

OBSERVATION
   ├── H1
   ├── H2
   ├── H3
   └── UNKNOWN

Alternatives should be relevant.

The goal is not endless hypothesis generation.

Discriminator

A discriminator asks:

What result would favor
H1 over H2?

This is one of the strongest transitions from conceptual reasoning into experimental design.

Without a discriminator:

two explanations

may remain only:

two stories

Failure Condition

Every preferred hypothesis should attempt to specify:

What would make this
less likely?

If no failure condition exists yet:

NOT YET OPERATIONALIZED

is more honest than inventing one.

Unknown

UNKNOWN remains an explicit experimental result.

Use it when:

data are insufficient

results conflict

mechanism is unresolved

a variable cannot be isolated

or multiple hypotheses
remain viable

Unknown is not failed research.

It marks the current boundary.

Null Results

A null result may be:

no cue effect

no observer effect

no cross-form invariant

no delayed stability

no change after
triadic prompting

These results should be preserved.

They help narrow the model.

Negative Results

A result may directly challenge a preferred claim.

For example:

related cue performs worse
than unrelated cue

or:

observer-condition change
does not alter observation

or:

relationship disappears
under alternative form

Such results are valuable.

They should not be reframed automatically as hidden confirmation.

Failed Hypotheses

When a hypothesis fails:

PRESERVE IT

Record:

what it predicted

what occurred

why the prediction failed

what model replaced it

Failed hypotheses improve the revision history.

Experimental Drift

Experiments can drift too.

A common pattern is:

result observed
      ↓
preferred explanation
      ↓
framework vocabulary added
      ↓
result interpreted as confirmation

The bounded sequence is:

      ↓
minimum supported claim
      ↓

      ↓

      ↓
test
      ↓

Confirmation Drift

Because Triadic Re-Ignition provides rich vocabulary, almost any result could potentially be described using:

observer

resonance

form

drift

paradox

projection

regime

This creates a special danger.

The vocabulary must not become:

automatic confirmation machinery

Instead ask:

Did the concept predict anything?

Did it improve discrimination?

Could the result have been
explained without it?

Framework Falsifiability

Triadic Re-Ignition should remain capable of being narrowed.

For example:

Resonance Before Form

should weaken if:

stable representation
consistently precedes recognition

A proposed observer effect should weaken if:

varying observer condition
does not change results

A trigger hypothesis should weaken if:

relevant controls perform equally

A cross-form structural claim should weaken if:

the relationship disappears
under representation change

The framework should learn from these results.

Simpler Models

Experiments should compare:

TRIADIC MODEL

with:

SIMPLER MODEL

when possible.

Ask:

Does the additional structure
explain something the simpler
model cannot?

Does it improve prediction?

Does it identify a new
discriminator?

Does it reduce unexplained variance?

If not:

prefer the simpler model
for that case

The framework does not need to be present in every explanation.

Observer-Aware Without Observer Inflation

The Experiments branch keeps observer variables available.

It does not assume:

the observer causes
the phenomenon

The relevant question is:

Does including observer condition
improve the model?

If not:

remove or reduce
the observer variable

Representation-Aware Without Form Inflation

Likewise, representation may matter.

But:

representation changes
      ≠
underlying structure changes

and:

representation effects
      ≠
all structure is representation

The experiment should distinguish these levels where possible.

Time-Aware Without Timeline Inflation

Temporal experiments should separate:

PHYSICAL TIME

LOCAL TIMELINE

RESONANCE TIME

TIMELINE PROJECTION

A changed temporal representation does not automatically establish:

changed physical history

or:

multiple physical timelines

Those are stronger hypotheses.

Formless-Aware Without Hidden-Reality Inflation

An unresolved relationship may be marked:

FORMLESS

within the module's operational vocabulary.

But:

FORMLESS
    =
representation boundary

not:

hidden reality verified

Experiments should attempt to move:

unresolved

toward:

operationalized

rather than merely renaming the unknown.

Universe-as-Operator Experimental Boundary

If an experiment invokes:

Universe as Operator

it should specify:

what broader condition
is being modeled

what the operator acts on

what transformation is expected

what invariant should survive

what simpler model competes

Otherwise the concept remains too broad to test.

AI-Assisted Experimentation

AI systems may assist with:

scenario generation

alternative hypotheses

claim classification

comparison structure

question generation

drift checking

experimental brainstorming

But:

AI output
    ≠
experimental observation

AI-generated hypotheses remain hypotheses.

AI-generated interpretations must not be silently inserted into participant data.

AI and Observer Effects

If an AI system interacts with a participant before or during testing, it may alter:

context

expectation

representation

available information

observer state

Therefore AI involvement should be documented as part of the experimental condition when relevant.

Human and AI Comparison

A future experiment may compare:

human observer

AI model

human + AI observer system

But these should not be presumed equivalent observer types.

The comparison would require its own operational definitions.

Ethical Boundary

Experiments in this branch are intended for:

education

reasoning research

low-stakes pattern analysis

model comparison

and exploratory framework testing

They should not be used as unvalidated procedures for:

clinical diagnosis

forensic memory recovery

legal proof

mental-health classification

or high-stakes decision making

Those applications require validated domain-specific methods.

Experimental Success

An experiment succeeds when it produces useful discrimination.

That may mean:

supporting a hypothesis

or:

weakening a hypothesis

or:

rejecting a hypothesis

or:

revealing a confound

or:

showing that
the question is not
yet operationalized

All can improve the framework.

What the Branch Should Learn

The Experiments branch should gradually determine:

which Re-Ignition concepts
are operationally useful

which remain metaphors

which require revision

which duplicate existing concepts

which generate predictions

which improve discrimination

and which should be removed

A framework that cannot lose unnecessary parts cannot learn effectively from experiment.

Research Questions

  1. Which Re-Ignition concepts can be operationalized reliably?

  2. Which concepts improve discrimination beyond ordinary models?

  3. Can renewed access be separated from relearning?

  4. Can reconstruction be distinguished from recall?

  5. Can resonance be separated from familiarity and salience?

  6. Can recognition be shown to precede stable form?

  7. Can observer effects be reproduced?

  8. Can representation effects be separated from observer effects?

  9. Can cross-form invariants be identified reliably?

  10. Can timeline revision be separated cleanly from physical chronology?

  11. Can apparent multiplicity be localized to a specific layer?

  12. Does the Minimum Supported Claim reduce interpretive drift?

  13. Does paradox preservation improve later discrimination?

  14. Does requiring alternative hypotheses reduce narrative lock?

  15. Does defining failure conditions improve model revision?

  16. Do Triadic prompts improve student reasoning?

  17. When do Triadic prompts add unnecessary complexity?

  18. Can experiments be reproduced without Triadic terminology?

  19. Which module concepts should be abandoned if they fail repeatedly?

  20. What evidence would justify stronger formal or mechanistic models?

The Experiments branch is not where Triadic Re-Ignition goes to prove itself.

It is where Triadic Re-Ignition gives the evidence permission to change it.

Main writing choice: I made the branch explicitly framework-falsifiable. experiments/ now has a clear progression of capture → compare → intervene → transfer → teach, while preserving null results, negative results, simpler models, and even the possibility that a Triadic operator adds no useful discrimination in a given case.
