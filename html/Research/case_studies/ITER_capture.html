[ (f_R,\tau_R,Q_R) ]

because electrical current is not itself a frequency, relaxation time, or quality factor.

Axiom A13 — Drift is Relative Change

Drift is defined relative to a declared reference trajectory or bounded region.

Let:

[ \mathbf{e}(t) = \mathbf{y}(t) - \widehat{\mathbf{y}}(t) ]

Then local residual drift is:

[ \mathbf{D}_e(t) = \frac{d\mathbf{e}}{dt} ]

and normalized drift may be represented as:

[ D_n(t) = \left| \frac{d\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}}{dt} \right| ]

Therefore:

[ \text{change} \not\Rightarrow \text{drift} ]

A predicted response to controlled forcing is not drift merely because the system state changes.

Drift occurs when the declared relationship to the reference changes.

Axiom A14 — Boundedness is Constraint Relative

A system is bounded only with respect to a declared admissible region:

[ \Omega_{adm}(\rho) = { \mathbf{x}: \mathbf{g}_{\rho}(\mathbf{x})\leq\mathbf{0} } ]

Boundedness over interval ([t_0,t_f]) requires:

[ \mathbf{x}(t) \in \Omega_{adm}(\rho(t)) \quad \forall t\in[t_0,t_f] ]

Therefore:

[ \text{no reported anomaly} \not\Rightarrow \text{boundedness demonstrated} ]

unless the relevant constraints and observations are known.

Likewise:

[ \text{bounded} \not\Rightarrow \text{unchanging} ]

A system may undergo substantial controlled evolution while remaining inside its admissible region.

Axiom A15 — Alignment is Not Identity

Two structures may be aligned without being identical.

Define alignment through a declared relation:

[ \mathcal{A}(a,b)\in[0,1] ]

where:

[ \mathcal{A}(a,b)=1 ]

indicates maximal alignment under that particular metric.

This does not imply:

[ a=b ]

nor does structural alignment establish common causal origin:

[ \mathcal{A}(a,b)\approx1 \not\Rightarrow \operatorname{cause}(a)=\operatorname{cause}(b) ]

Corollary A15.1 — Structural Resemblance

For an RTT representation (R_{RTT}) and conventional engineering representation (R_{eng}):

[ R_{RTT}\sim R_{eng} ]

may establish structural similarity under a declared mapping, but:

[ R_{RTT}\sim R_{eng} \not\Rightarrow RTT\text{ caused }R_{eng} ]

and:

[ R_{RTT}\sim R_{eng} \not\Rightarrow RTT\text{ predicts }R_{eng} ]

Axiom A16 — Prediction Precedes Evaluation

An RTT-specific predictive claim must be declared before the target observation is used to judge that prediction.

Let:

[ P_{RTT} ]

be an RTT-derived prediction and:

[ Y ]

the subsequently evaluated observation.

Admissible predictive testing requires:

[ P_{RTT} \prec Y_{\mathrm{evaluation}} ]

in the evidential sequence.

If the RTT relationship is constructed after observing (Y), then it is classified as:

[ \mathsf{PostHoc} ]

rather than:

[ \mathsf{Prediction} ]

Post-hoc structural mappings may still be useful for interpretation, education, or hypothesis generation, but they do not constitute prospective confirmation.

Axiom A17 — Independent Discrimination

RTT gains empirical support from a case only when an RTT-derived hypothesis produces a discriminating result relative to an admissible comparison model.

Let:

[ H_{RTT} ]

be an RTT-derived hypothesis, and:

[ H_{eng} ]

the conventional engineering hypothesis.

A discriminating test requires some observable (z) such that:

[ P(z|H_{RTT}) \neq P(z|H_{eng}) ]

or, without probabilistic models:

[ \widehat{z}{RTT} \neq \widehat{z} ]

within a preregistered region sufficient to distinguish the predictions given measurement uncertainty.

If:

[ \widehat{z}{RTT} = \widehat{z} ]

because RTT merely restates the engineering model, the result may demonstrate representational compatibility but does not independently validate RTT.

Axiom A18 — Falsifiability

Every empirical RTT claim (H) shall admit at least one possible observation that would count against it.

There must exist an admissible observation (y_f) such that:

[ y_f \Rightarrow \neg H ]

or weakens (H) under the declared evaluation rule.

If:

[ \forall y,\quad H(y)=\text{compatible} ]

then (H) has no discriminating empirical content within that test.

Accordingly, no case-study hypothesis may redefine coherence, resonance, alignment, regime, or drift after observing the result solely to preserve the hypothesis.

Axiom A19 — Uncertainty Propagation

No derived RTT quantity may claim greater empirical precision than permitted by its inputs.

For:

[ z = f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n) ]

with covariance matrix:

[ \mathbf{\Sigma}_x ]

the uncertainty of (z) must, where quantitatively evaluated, be propagated using an appropriate declared method.

For a differentiable function, a first-order approximation is:

[ \sigma_z^2 \approx \mathbf{J}_f \mathbf{\Sigma}_x \mathbf{J}_f^T ]

where:

[ \mathbf{J}_f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{x}} ]

If input uncertainty is unavailable, the RTT-derived result shall be marked accordingly.

Missing uncertainty shall not be interpreted as zero uncertainty.

Axiom A20 — Dimensional Consistency

Every quantitative RTT expression shall be dimensionally valid or shall declare the normalization that renders it dimensionless.

For addition:

[ a+b ]

the operands must inhabit compatible dimensional spaces:

[ [a]=[b] ]

Otherwise normalization operators must first be applied.

Accordingly, expressions such as:

[ \nabla_{\tau}R+\nabla_R\tau ]

shall not be evaluated numerically until their dimensions have been reconciled through explicitly declared scaling:

[ \widetilde{\mathcal{K}}{RTT} = sR + s_{R\tau}\nabla_R\tau ]}\nabla_{\tau

Dimensional symbolism is not sufficient to establish dimensional validity.

Axiom A21 — Null Preservation

Conventional explanations shall remain available unless evidence distinguishes the RTT hypothesis from them.

Let:

[ H_0 ]

denote the conventional or null explanation.

The presence of a valid RTT representation does not eliminate (H_0):

[ \exists H_{RTT} \not\Rightarrow \neg H_0 ]

If the observation is adequately accounted for under:

[ H_0 ]

and RTT supplies no distinct prediction, then the result shall remain classified as:

[ \text{RTT-compatible} ]

rather than:

[ \text{RTT-confirmed} ]

Axiom A22 — Minimum-Claim Principle

For evidence set:

[ E ]

let:

[ \mathbb{H}(E) ]

be the set of claims compatible with that evidence.

The preferred RTT case-study claim is the least expansive claim sufficient to describe the demonstrated relationship:

[ H^* = \arg\min_{H\in\mathbb{H}(E)} \operatorname{Complexity}(H) ]

subject to:

[ H \text{ accurately represents }E ]

Operationally:

Do not claim resonance when alignment is sufficient.

Do not claim mechanism when correlation is sufficient.

Do not claim prediction when mapping is sufficient.

Do not claim validation when compatibility is sufficient.

Axiom A23 — Revision Under Evidence

RTT case-study mappings remain revisable when additional evidence changes the supported interpretation.

Let:

[ \mathcal{R}_n ]

denote the RTT representation under evidence set (E_n).

When:

[ E_{n+1} = E_n\cup\Delta E ]

the updated representation is:

[ \mathcal{R}_{n+1} = \operatorname{Update} ( \mathcal{R}_n, \Delta E ) ]

There is no requirement that:

[ \mathcal{R}_{n+1} = \mathcal{R}_n ]

RTT coherence therefore applies to the integrity of the analytical mapping, not to preservation of a preferred conclusion.

Under this axiom system, the ITER coil test may be analyzed through RTT if and only if the analysis preserves:

[ \boxed{ \text{Boundary} + \text{Provenance} + \text{Observer} + \text{Regime} + \text{Uncertainty} + \text{Null} + \text{Falsifiability} } ]

The formal posture of the case study is therefore:

[ \boxed{ \text{RTT interpretation} \neq \text{RTT validation} } ]

and:

[ \boxed{ \text{structural awareness} \rightarrow \text{testable hypothesis} \rightarrow \text{measurement} \rightarrow \text{comparison} } ]

rather than:

[ \boxed{ \text{interesting pattern} \rightarrow \text{assumed confirmation} } ]

This boundary is part of the method, not a limitation to be removed.

The key change is that the case now has a claim-control layer underneath the notation. In particular, A11, A16, A17, A18, and A21 make the ITER exercise genuinely capable of saying“no RTT effect demonstrated” without that counting as a failure of the analysis. The axioms now need to cross the boundary from methodological safeguards into claims that could fail against data. The key discipline is that not every axiom becomes a physical hypothesis: some become eligibility conditions, while the empirically productive ones become preregisterable hypotheses with variables, nulls, thresholds, and rejection rules.

Testable Hypotheses Derived from the RTT Axioms

1. Purpose

This section converts the formal RTT axioms into hypotheses that can be evaluated against ITER coil-test data.

The hypotheses are designed to determine whether RTT provides:

  1. a valid structural representation;
  2. an operationally measurable analysis;
  3. improved detection of regime transitions or anomalous behavior;
  4. a discriminating prediction beyond conventional engineering models.

The hypotheses do not begin by assuming an RTT effect.

Each hypothesis must specify:

  • the axiom or axioms from which it is derived;
  • the tested quantity;
  • the conventional reference or null model;
  • the expected observation;
  • the required data;
  • the acceptance or rejection rule;
  • its evidential status;
  • whether the result tests RTT itself or only the RTT mapping.

2. Axiom-to-Hypothesis Conversion Rule

Let:

[ A_i ]

denote RTT axiom (i).

Let:

[ H_i ]

denote a hypothesis derived from that axiom.

A hypothesis is empirically admissible only if there exists an observation set:

[ Y_i ]

and evaluation rule:

[ \mathcal{E}_i ]

such that:

[ \mathcal{E}_i(H_i,Y_i) \in { \mathsf{Supported}, \mathsf{NotSupported}, \mathsf{Inconclusive} } ]

An admissible hypothesis must permit at least one possible result for which:

[ \mathcal{E}_i(H_i,Y_i) = \mathsf{NotSupported} ]

If no observation could count against the hypothesis, the statement remains an interpretive proposition rather than a testable hypothesis.

3. Hypothesis Classes

The hypotheses are separated into four classes.

Class I — Mapping hypotheses

These test whether RTT variables can be connected consistently to engineering quantities.

They do not independently test RTT as a physical theory.

Class II — Structural-response hypotheses

These test whether RTT coherence, drift, boundedness, and regime operators identify measurable structure in the coil response.

Class III — Comparative hypotheses

These test whether RTT-derived quantities add useful information beyond conventional residual and threshold methods.

Class IV — Discriminating hypotheses

These test whether RTT produces a preregistered prediction that differs measurably from the conventional engineering model.

Only Class IV can provide direct evidence for an independent RTT contribution.

4. Class I: Mapping Hypotheses

H1 — Operational Mapping Completeness

Source axioms

  • A2 — Triadic Field Representation
  • A3 — Operational Mapping
  • A4 — Evidence-State Preservation

Hypothesis

Every RTT variable used quantitatively in the coil analysis can be mapped to a controlled, measured, derived, or explicitly declared proxy quantity.

Formally, for the quantitative RTT variable set:

[ \mathbb{Z}_{RTT} = { z_1,z_2,\dots,z_n } ]

the hypothesis is:

[ H_1: \forall z_i\in\mathbb{Z}_{RTT}, \quad \mathcal{M}(z_i)\neq\varnothing ]

The corresponding null is:

[ H_{1,0}: \exists z_i\in\mathbb{Z}_{RTT} \quad\text{such that}\quad \mathcal{M}(z_i)=\varnothing ]

Required record

For each (z_i), the mapping registry must contain:

[ \mathcal{M}(z_i) = (q_i,u_i,m_i,\sigma_i,D_i,\epsilon_i) ]

where:

  • (q_i) is the represented engineering quantity;
  • (u_i) is the unit or normalization;
  • (m_i) is the measurement or derivation method;
  • (\sigma_i) is the uncertainty or an explicit statement that it is unavailable;
  • (D_i) is the admissible domain;
  • (\epsilon_i) is the evidence class.

Evaluation rule

Support (H_1) only if every numerical RTT variable has a complete mapping record.

Reject (H_1) if one or more numerical RTT variables lack an identifiable source, operational definition, unit, normalization, or evidence state.

Interpretation

Support for (H_1) demonstrates mapping completeness.

It does not demonstrate that RTT supplies new physics.

H2 — Evidence-Lineage Preservation

Source axioms

  • A4 — Evidence-State Preservation
  • A19 — Uncertainty Propagation

Hypothesis

RTT transformations preserve the provenance and uncertainty limitations of their inputs.

For a derived RTT quantity:

[ z=f(x_1,\dots,x_n) ]

the hypothesis is:

[ H_2: \epsilon(z) = \mathcal{L} \left( \epsilon(x_1),\dots,\epsilon(x_n) \right) ]

where (\mathcal{L}) is a preregistered lineage rule that prohibits semantic promotion.

In particular:

[ \mathsf{Proxy} \not\rightarrow \mathsf{Measured} ]

and:

[ \mathsf{Unknown} \not\rightarrow \mathsf{Known} ]

through notation or transformation alone.

Null hypothesis

[ H_{2,0}: \exists z \quad\text{whose reported evidence class exceeds that supported by its inputs} ]

Evaluation rule

Audit the full transformation chain for each reported RTT result.

Support (H_2) if every output retains traceable input provenance and proper uncertainty qualifications.

Reject (H_2) if any derived or proxy variable is reported as directly measured, validated, or more precise than its input data permit.

Interpretation

This tests the integrity of the RTT analysis, not the physical behavior of the coil.

H3 — Observer Sufficiency

Source axioms

  • A5 — Observer Separation
  • A19 — Uncertainty Propagation

Hypothesis

The selected instrumentation has sufficient sensitivity, temporal resolution, spatial coverage, and uncertainty characterization to evaluate the declared RTT quantities.

Let the observability condition for RTT variable (z_i) be:

[ \mathcal{Q}_{obs}(z_i) = \left( s_i, \Delta t_i, \Delta r_i, \sigma_i \right) ]

where:

  • (s_i) is instrument sensitivity;
  • (\Delta t_i) is temporal resolution;
  • (\Delta r_i) is spatial resolution or coverage;
  • (\sigma_i) is measurement uncertainty.

Then:

[ H_3: \mathcal{Q}{obs}(z_i) \in \mathcal{Q}(z_i) \quad \forall z_i ]

Null hypothesis

[ H_{3,0}: \exists z_i \quad\text{for which}\quad \mathcal{Q}{obs}(z_i) \notin \mathcal{Q}(z_i) ]

Evaluation rule

Support (H_3) only if the observation system can resolve the smallest effect, transition, or residual claimed by the RTT analysis.

Reject (H_3) for any claim whose predicted magnitude is below instrument sensitivity or whose timescale is shorter than available sampling resolution.

Declare the result inconclusive when instrumentation specifications are unavailable.

Interpretation

Failure of (H_3) means the selected dataset cannot test the proposed RTT hypothesis. It does not establish that the hypothesized effect is absent.

5. Class II: Structural-Response Hypotheses

H4 — Regime Segmentation Hypothesis

Source axioms

  • A6 — Temporal Ordering
  • A7 — Regime Dependence
  • A13 — Drift is Relative Change

Hypothesis

The coil-test trajectory contains measurable operating regimes with statistically or operationally distinguishable response relationships.

Let:

[ \rho(t) \in { \rho_{\mathrm{cooldown}}, \rho_{\mathrm{superconducting}}, \rho_{\mathrm{ramp}}, \rho_{\mathrm{hold}}, \rho_{\mathrm{discharge}}, \rho_{\mathrm{recovery}}, \rho_{\mathrm{fault}} } ]

Then:

[ H_4: \exists \rho_a\neq\rho_b \quad\text{such that}\quad P(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{q},\rho_a) \neq P(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{q},\rho_b) ]

The practical meaning is that the relationship among forcing, state, and response changes across at least two declared regimes.

Null hypothesis

[ H_{4,0}: P(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{q},\rho_a) = P(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{q},\rho_b) ]

for all analyzed regimes after accounting for the conventional input variables.

Required data

  • timestamped control-state records;
  • current-ramp and hold intervals;
  • temperature history;
  • voltage history;
  • structural displacement or strain history;
  • event and protection logs.

Evaluation rule

Regime boundaries must be declared from control states, physical thresholds, or event records before response differences are tested.

Support (H_4) if at least one preregistered response relationship differs across regimes by more than the combined uncertainty and declared effect threshold.

Reject (H_4) if a single stationary response model accounts for the selected intervals within the declared tolerance.

Interpretation

Support for (H_4) establishes regime-dependent structure.

It does not, by itself, show that RTT identified the regimes better than conventional test segmentation.

H5 — Bounded Response Hypothesis

Source axioms

  • A10 — Coherence is Relational
  • A14 — Boundedness is Constraint Relative

Hypothesis

During the selected 34 kA test interval, the observed state remains inside the preregistered admissible region for every monitored constraint.

Let:

[ \Omega_{adm}(\rho) = { \mathbf{x}: \mathbf{g}_{\rho}(\mathbf{x})\leq\mathbf{0} } ]

Then:

[ H_5: \mathbf{x}(t) \in \Omega_{adm}(\rho(t)) \quad \forall t\in[t_0,t_f] ]

Null hypothesis

[ H_{5,0}: \exists t^\in[t_0,t_f] \quad\text{such that}\quad \mathbf{x}(t^) \notin \Omega_{adm}(\rho(t^*)) ]

Primary metric

[ \mu_{min}(t) = \min_i \left[ -\frac{g_i(\mathbf{x}(t))}{s_i} \right] ]

Evaluation rule

Support (H_5) if:

[ \mu_{min}(t)>0 ]

throughout the interval after uncertainty is included.

Reject (H_5) if:

[ \mu_{min}(t)<0 ]

for any confirmed observation.

Declare the result inconclusive if the governing limits or required measurements are unavailable.

Interpretation

Support for (H_5) demonstrates bounded operation relative to the declared constraints.

It does not establish resonance or independent RTT validation.

H6 — Model-Observation Coherence Hypothesis

Source axioms

  • A10 — Coherence is Relational
  • A19 — Uncertainty Propagation
  • A20 — Dimensional Consistency

Hypothesis

The measured coil response remains coherent with the conventional engineering prediction within preregistered, uncertainty-aware tolerances.

Let:

[ \mathbf{e}(t) = \mathbf{y}(t) - \widehat{\mathbf{y}}(t) ]

and:

[ d_M(t) = \sqrt{ \mathbf{e}(t)^T \mathbf{\Sigma}_{e}(t)^{-1} \mathbf{e}(t) } ]

Then:

[ H_6: d_M(t)\leq d_{max}(\rho(t)) ]

for the required proportion of the selected interval.

Here:

[ d_{max}(\rho) ]

must be declared before evaluating the result.

Null hypothesis

[ H_{6,0}: d_M(t)>d_{max}(\rho(t)) ]

for a duration, frequency, or magnitude exceeding the preregistered allowance.

Secondary RTT expression

A corresponding coherence score may be calculated as:

[ \mathcal{C}_{RTT}(t) = \exp \left[ -\frac12 d_M(t)^2 \right] ]

provided the covariance model is valid.

Evaluation rule

Support (H_6) when the residual distribution remains inside the declared uncertainty-aware acceptance region.

Reject (H_6) when the residual exceeds that region according to the preregistered duration or recurrence rule.

Interpretation

This hypothesis tests quantitative agreement between observation and the engineering model.

Because the RTT coherence score is constructed from that residual, support for (H_6) demonstrates RTT-compatible structural alignment, not an independent RTT prediction.

H7 — Residual Drift Hypothesis

Source axioms

  • A13 — Drift is Relative Change
  • A14 — Boundedness is Constraint Relative

Hypothesis

Once the commanded current and relevant environmental conditions enter a declared hold regime, the model-observation residual does not exhibit sustained directional drift.

Define:

[ \mathbf{D}_e(t) = \frac{d\mathbf{e}(t)}{dt} ]

and normalized drift:

[ D_n(t) = \left| \frac{d\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}(t)}{dt} \right|_W ]

The hypothesis is:

[ H_7: E[\mathbf{D}e(t)\mid\rho] \approx \mathbf{0} ]}

within the preregistered uncertainty and drift tolerance.

Null hypothesis

[ H_{7,0}: E[\mathbf{D}e(t)\mid\rho] \neq \mathbf{0} ]}

with a sustained magnitude exceeding the declared threshold.

Required controls

The drift test must account for:

  • remaining current variation;
  • thermal equilibration;
  • known structural relaxation;
  • sensor drift;
  • filtering delay;
  • model interpolation effects.

Evaluation rule

Support (H_7) when no preregistered residual component shows a sustained change beyond the combined measurement and model uncertainty.

Reject (H_7) when at least one component exhibits statistically and operationally significant directional drift.

Interpretation

Rejection may indicate:

  • an omitted conventional process;
  • sensor or calibration drift;
  • incorrect boundary conditions;
  • delayed structural or thermal response;
  • an unmodeled regime transition;
  • a candidate for further RTT investigation.

Rejection does not automatically indicate a novel RTT effect.

H8 — Recovery Hypothesis

Source axioms

  • A6 — Temporal Ordering
  • A8 — Operator Closure
  • A13 — Drift is Relative Change
  • A14 — Boundedness is Constraint Relative

Hypothesis

Following controlled discharge, the monitored response approaches its preregistered recovery reference without persistent excess residual.

Let (t_d) be the start of discharge and let:

[ \mathbf{y}_{rec} ]

be the declared recovery reference.

Define:

[ E_{rec}(t) = \left| \mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}_{rec} \right|_W ]

Then:

[ H_8: E_{rec}(t_f) \leq E_{rec,max} ]

and the recovery trajectory is consistent with the declared conventional relaxation model.

Null hypothesis

[ H_{8,0}: E_{rec}(t_f)>E_{rec,max} ]

or the observed recovery form differs materially from the declared model.

Evaluation rule

Support (H_8) if the final residual and recovery trajectory remain within preregistered bounds.

Reject (H_8) when a persistent offset, hysteresis, or unexplained relaxation component exceeds the declared threshold.

Interpretation

This hypothesis may reveal memory, hysteresis, or incomplete recovery.

Such behavior must first be tested against conventional mechanical, thermal, magnetic, electrical, and instrumentation explanations.

6. Class III: Comparative Hypotheses

H9 — RTT Regime Detection Advantage

Source axioms

  • A7 — Regime Dependence
  • A16 — Prediction Precedes Evaluation
  • A17 — Independent Discrimination

Hypothesis

A preregistered RTT regime-transition detector identifies declared transitions earlier or more accurately than a specified conventional baseline detector.

Let:

[ \widehat{t}_{RTT,k} ]

be the RTT-predicted transition time,

[ \widehat{t}_{base,k} ]

the baseline detector's transition time, and:

[ t_{event,k} ]

the independently recorded event time.

Define transition error:

[ E_{RTT,k} = \left| \widehat{t}{RTT,k}-t \right| ]

[ E_{base,k} = \left| \widehat{t}{base,k}-t \right| ]

Then:

[ H_9: E_{RTT}<E_{base} ]

across the preregistered evaluation set by at least the minimum improvement:

[ \Delta_{min} ]

Null hypothesis

[ H_{9,0}: E_{RTT}\geq E_{base}-\Delta_{min} ]

Required safeguards

  • Parameters must be fitted on training data only.
  • Evaluation must use held-out ramps, intervals, or coils.
  • Event labels must be independent of the RTT detector.
  • The baseline detector must be declared before evaluation.
  • False alarms and missed transitions must be included.

Evaluation rule

Support (H_9) only if the RTT detector meets the preregistered improvement threshold without unacceptable increases in false positives or missed events.

Reject (H_9) if performance is equal to or worse than the baseline.

Interpretation

Support would demonstrate analytical utility.

It would not yet establish that RTT represents a new physical mechanism.

H10 — RTT Anomaly-Detection Advantage

Source axioms

  • A8 — Operator Closure
  • A13 — Drift is Relative Change
  • A17 — Independent Discrimination
  • A21 — Null Preservation

Hypothesis

A preregistered RTT score combining coherence, drift, boundedness, and regime information detects labeled anomalous intervals more effectively than conventional residual-threshold monitoring alone.

Define the RTT anomaly score:

[ S_{RTT}(t) = f \left( 1-\mathcal{C}{RTT}(t), D_n(t), -\mu(t), \rho(t) \right) ]

Define the baseline score:

[ S_{base}(t) = g \left( \mathbf{e}(t) \right) ]

The hypothesis is:

[ H_{10}: \operatorname{Perf}(S_{RTT})

\operatorname{Perf}(S_{base}) + \Delta_{min} ]

where (\operatorname{Perf}) is a preregistered performance metric.

Null hypothesis

[ H_{10,0}: \operatorname{Perf}(S_{RTT}) \leq \operatorname{Perf}(S_{base}) + \Delta_{min} ]

Required data

  • labeled normal and anomalous intervals;
  • sufficiently resolved time series;
  • held-out evaluation data;
  • independently defined event labels;
  • declared false-alarm costs.

Evaluation rule

Support (H_{10}) only when the RTT score exceeds the baseline by the declared minimum improvement on held-out data.

Reject (H_{10}) when it fails to exceed the baseline or gains apparent sensitivity through an unacceptable false-alarm rate.

Interpretation

Support would show that RTT organization adds operational information.

It would not by itself show that the added information is unique to RTT rather than to the inclusion of additional engineered features.

H11 — Cross-Ramp Generalization

Source axioms

  • A7 — Regime Dependence
  • A17 — Independent Discrimination
  • A23 — Revision Under Evidence

Hypothesis

RTT parameters estimated from one set of current ramps generalize to unseen ramps without outcome-specific retuning.

35. Preregistration Principle

The purpose of preregistration is not to make RTT difficult to support.

It is to make support meaningful.

The evidential sequence is therefore frozen as:

[ \boxed{ \text{Axiom} \rightarrow \text{Hypothesis} \rightarrow \text{Operationalization} \rightarrow \text{Prediction} \rightarrow \text{Freeze} \rightarrow \text{Observation} \rightarrow \text{Evaluation} \rightarrow \text{Conclusion} } ]

and explicitly not:

[ \boxed{ \text{Observation} \rightarrow \text{Pattern} \rightarrow \text{RTT reinterpretation} \rightarrow \text{Prediction claim} } ]

An unexpected result may generate the next hypothesis.

It may not rewrite the hypothesis that produced it.

The important addition is the freeze block. It gives us a concrete evidentiary boundary between “we noticed something interesting in ITER” and “RTT predicted something before we looked.” I also separated Not Supported from Inconclusive, so missing ITER data cannot accidentally become either evidence for or evidence against RTT.
