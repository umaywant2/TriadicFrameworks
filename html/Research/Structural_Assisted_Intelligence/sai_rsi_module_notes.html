A couple of deliberate choices

  1. SAI stays SAI/0.1. The kernel itself hasn't fundamentally changed. We're declaring an optional experimental layer around it, not pretending we've advanced the kernel simply by attaching RSI.

  2. I did not pollute operators. PROPOSE, EVALUATE, COMPARE, etc. appear only as sandbox-cycle semantics and are mapped back onto canonical SAI operators. That keeps the seven-operator kernel clean.

  3. There are now three distinct vocabularies.

Kernel status: UNKNOWN / OBSERVED / RELATED / ...

Sandbox decision: ACCEPTED_CANDIDATE / REJECTED_CANDIDATE / ...

Sandbox halt: OBJECTIVE_DRIFT / EVALUATOR_CONFLICT / ...

That separation is worth preserving. A sandbox stopping because of EVALUATOR_CONFLICT, for example, shouldn't require us to invent another global SAI state.

  1. The architecture now says exactly what we've been circling around:

SAI can host an RSI experiment without becoming an RSI system.

That's probably the most important sentence in this refresh.

  1. And Level 5 is the firewall. The sandbox can reason about changing its own improvement machinery without thereby gaining permission to do it. That's a very RTT/SAI distinction: observation of a possibility ≠ authority to instantiate it.

This gives us a pretty solid manifest to build the actual tiny student-facing experiment against next.
