Algorithms for detecting, classifying, routing, and correcting drift across the full Framework Field Theory canon

Drift is inevitable in any evolving conceptual system.
In Framework Field Theory, drift occurs when:

  • operators shift unintentionally

  • lineage fragments
  • fields diverge from canonical structure

The Canon Drift‑Correction Algorithms (CDCA) provide the formal, reproducible, computational logic for detecting and correcting drift at every scale.

1. The Five Drift Types

All drift in FFT falls into one of five categories:

  1. Operator Drift — operator usage deviates from lineage
  2. Dimensional Drift — dimensional mapping becomes inconsistent
  3. Regime Drift — regime transitions become unstable
  4. Coherence Drift — paradox overwhelms integration capacity
  5. Field Drift — entire fields diverge from canonical structure

Each drift type has its own detection and correction algorithm.

3. Drift Classification System

Drift is classified into three severity levels:

Level 1 — Soft Drift

  • minor misalignment
  • easily corrected
  • no structural risk

Level 2 — Hard Drift

  • structural misalignment
  • requires algorithmic correction
  • moderate risk

Level 3 — Critical Drift

  • coherence collapse
  • paradox saturation
  • field fragmentation
  • high risk

Critical drift triggers emergency correction protocols.
