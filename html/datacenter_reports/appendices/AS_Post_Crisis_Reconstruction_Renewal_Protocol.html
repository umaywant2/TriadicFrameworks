The Post‑Crisis Reconstruction & Renewal Protocol (PCRRP) defines the RTT‑aligned procedures for rebuilding, realigning, and renewing datacenter ecosystems after a crisis, emergency, or regime‑shift event.
It ensures that reconstruction remains:

  • structurally coherent
  • dimensionally aligned
  • temporally stabilized
  • operator‑balanced
  • drift‑bounded
  • coherence‑anchored
  • infrastructure‑synchronized
  • culturally grounded
  • future‑proofed

PCRRP is the long‑range reconstruction backbone of the Datacenter Reports canon.

AS.1 — Purpose of the Reconstruction Protocol

The protocol exists to:

  • rebuild datacenter ecosystems after crisis events
  • restore structural, dimensional, temporal, operator, and tensor stability
  • prevent post‑crisis drift and collapse cascades
  • realign infrastructure and environmental envelopes
  • reinforce stewardship continuity (Appendix AG)
  • integrate reconstruction with governance (Appendix AF)
  • ensure long‑range resilience and renewal

It is the canon’s post‑crisis stabilization and renewal system.

Reconstruction occurs across six canonical domains:

Repair structural fields, templates, boundaries, and lineage.

AS.3 — Reconstruction Architecture

PCRRP operates across three layers:

Layer 1 — Stabilization Layer

Immediate post‑crisis stabilization (structural, dimensional, temporal).

Layer 2 — Reconstruction Layer

Repair, rebuild, and realign all fields and envelopes.

Layer 3 — Renewal Layer

Strengthen, future‑proof, and evolve the ecosystem beyond pre‑crisis levels.

These layers form the Reconstruction & Renewal Stack.

Reconstruction follows a structured RTT cycle:

Assess → Stabilize → Rebuild → Realign → Renew → Verify → Document
