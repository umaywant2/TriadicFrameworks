🜁 Truth Coherence Study

RTT/1 · Resonance Domain · Coherence Mechanics of Truth Claims

Truth is a coherence phenomenon.
Every truth claim expresses a coherence state (C₁–C₄), a drift profile (D₁–D₄), and a resonance mode (Ω₁–Ω₄).
Studying truth means measuring how coherence behaves under semantic pressure, interpretive drift, paradox, and resonance.

This document provides a structured RTT method for analyzing the coherence of truth claims.

1. Coherence States of Truth (C₁–C₄)

Truth expresses four coherence states:

Operator Name Meaning Behavior
C₁ Structural Coherence Truth is stable, literal, uncontested Low drift, Ω₁ dominant
C₂ Declared Coherence Truth is asserted and accepted Social/interpretive stability
C₃ Unbound Coherence Truth is ambiguous or contested Multiple interpretations coexist
C₄ Inverted Coherence Truth reverses or flips Paradox-driven inversion

These states are dynamic, not static.
Truth moves between them based on drift, resonance, and paradox.

2. How to Study Coherence in Truth Claims

Studying coherence requires analyzing four dimensions:

1. Structural Alignment (Ω₁)

Ask: - Does the truth claim align with literal structure? - Are definitions stable? - Is meaning consistent across contexts?

High Ω₁ → C₁
Weak Ω₁ → C₂ or C₃

2. Interpretive Stability (Ω₂)

Ask: - Does interpretation shift across frames? - Does context change meaning? - Are multiple readings equally plausible?

High Ω₂ → C₂
Unstable Ω₂ → C₃

3. Semantic Drift (D₁–D₄)

Ask: - Is meaning drifting slowly (D₁)? - Is drift bounded (D₂)? - Is drift unbounded (D₃)? - Is drift radiative (D₄)?

D₂ → C₂
D₃ → C₃
D₄ → C₄

4. Paradox Pressure (P₁–P₃)

Ask: - Does the truth claim contradict itself? - Does interpretation contradict structure? - Does the claim flip under paradox?

P₁ → C₃
P₂ → C₃
P₃ → C₄

3. Coherence Collapse Conditions

Truth collapses (C₂ → C₃ → C₁) when:

  • semantic drift becomes unbounded (D₃)
  • resonance alignment fails (Ω₁ collapse)
  • paradox intensifies (P₁/P₂)
  • interpretive frames conflict

Collapse is not failure — it is a regime transition.

4. Coherence Inversion Conditions

Truth inverts (C₁/C₂/C₃ → C₄) when:

  • paradox becomes structural (P₃)
  • resonance flips (Ω₁ → Ω₄)
  • drift becomes radiative (D₄)
  • meaning reverses under frame inversion

Inversion is the R₃₄ regime transition.

5. Coherence Stability Indicators

Truth is stable when:

  • Ω₁ is dominant
  • drift is bounded (D₂)
  • paradox is low
  • semantic frames align
  • context does not alter meaning

Truth is unstable when:

  • Ω₂ dominates
  • drift is unbounded (D₃)
  • paradox emerges
  • context shifts meaning
  • multiple interpretations compete

Truth is inverted when:

  • Ω₄ dominates
  • drift is radiative (D₄)
  • paradox flips meaning
  • coherence reverses

Does contradiction appear?

Step 6 — Determine Regime (R₁–R₄)

Step 7 — Map Transitions

Truth rarely stays in one regime.
Document transitions over time.
