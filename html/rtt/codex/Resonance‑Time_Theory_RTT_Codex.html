Chapter 1 — The Foundational Triad

1.1 Overview

The Foundational Triad is the structural seed of Resonance‑Time Theory (RTT). It establishes the three core components that appear in every stable system:

  • Resonance (R) — the coherence or alignment within a system
  • Time (T) — the gradient of resonance resistance
  • Mass (M) — the density of resonance within a system

These three elements form the backbone of RTT’s conceptual and mathematical structure.

1.2 The Three Components

Resonance (R)

Resonance represents the degree of internal coherence within a system. High resonance corresponds to stability, alignment, and reduced internal friction.

Time (T)

RTT defines time not as a dimension but as a gradient. Specifically, time emerges from the rate at which resonance becomes easier or harder to maintain.

Mass (M)

Mass is treated as a form of resonance density — the amount of resonance “stored” or “held” within a system.

1.3 The Triadic Relationship

The Foundational Triad is expressed as:

$$ T = \nabla R^{-1} $$

This equation states that time is the inverse gradient of resonance. When resonance changes rapidly, time behaves differently; when resonance is stable, time flows smoothly.

1.4 Why This Triad Matters

The Foundational Triad provides:

  • A unified definition of time
  • A resonance‑based interpretation of mass
  • A structural basis for all other triads in RTT

It is the conceptual root from which the entire Codex grows.
